# JHSAA 2095 Annual Report

This report covers both 2095 seasons, girls in spring and boys in fall, against 2094 and the years before. It draws on the association's research exports for 2087 through 2095. Only two programs per gender changed class this year, the ordinary yearly reassignment. The season's real news was on the courts.

This was the first year of 5A's 6S5D postseason pilot, adopted after the 2094 vote. It gets its own section below.

## The year in brief

Westfield Friends won a fourth straight 5A boys title, and for the first time that streak came against a genuine format, not the old 1S4D sprint: an 11 flight, 7-4 final over Condotti Vanguard Academy, the widest championship dual the association has ever played. Pacific Friends won 6A in both genders again, its third straight double, and the girls added the Tournament of Champions.

No boys or girls program won a first state title this year, the first season since these reports began that no class produced a new champion. Two long droughts ended anyway: Arthur Ashe (the program renamed from Gruesome Ridge) reached the 2A final, its first trip to a final in 69 years on record, and Sacred Heart boys reached a 7A semifinal after 69 years without one.

Marigny won 7A boys from the 6 seed, and Chicago Island won Group 1 from the 16 seed, the lowest seed to win a title this year in either gender.

Only one program collapsed from a winning season to a losing one, Monongahela girls in Group 1, down from 22-9 to 6-22.

## The 5A pilot, one year in

Every 5A postseason dual this year, from the first Sectional round through the state final, was played 6 singles and 5 doubles, eleven flights, replacing the 1S4D format 5A had used since before these records begin. The Tournament of Champions is unaffected and still plays 1S4D for every class, including 5A's own entrants.

The clearest change is that blowouts are down. Under the old format 5A produced the most sweeps of any class in the association, 37.5 percent of its state duals over the eight seasons before the switch. This year, across the full postseason ladder, that fell to 19 percent for the boys and 18.7 percent for the girls, close to 6A's own rate of 14.6 and 15.6 percent.

What did not move the way the format study projected is the one point dual rate. 5A ran the closest postseason format math in the association for years, and the study argued that adding flights would preserve competitive matches while cutting the chance a slightly weaker team could sweep its way to an upset. In year one, one point 5A duals fell to 20.6 percent for the boys and 18.1 percent for the girls, both lower than 6A's 28.7 and 25.0 percent. Average margin, as a share of the total points on the dual, actually ran a bit higher in 5A than in 6A this year, 53 to 55 percent against 51 percent.

None of that means the format failed. A wider dual gives a genuinely stronger team more chances to prove it, which is the opposite of an upset problem, and one season is a small sample against eight years of history under the old shape. It does mean the season did not produce the specific signature, more close duals, that the original pitch emphasized, and the association may want to watch whether that holds in year two before treating it as settled.

On brackets: the 5A boys final, Westfield over Condotti 7-4, was the widest margin scoreline the format has produced and also one of its closest finals by share of flights, 63.6 percent to a champion. The 5A girls final, Romero-Finniski's 9-2 win, was the most lopsided final in the class this year.

## Stories of the year

### The longest waits

Arthur Ashe reached its first state final in program history, 69 years of records, losing 2A's final 3-2 to Banfield Day. The program carried its old name, Gruesome Ridge, for all but one of those seasons.

Sacred Heart boys reached a 7A semifinal, also a 69 year first for that program. The girls team won its own first state title a year earlier, in 2094.

### Low seeds, big runs

Chicago Island won Group 1 from the 16 seed, the lowest seed to win a title in either gender this year. Marigny won 7A from the 6 seed, beating Glen Park, the top seed at 38-2, 6-3 in the final. Mater Dei girls won 9A from the 5 seed, the program's first title since 2088.

### No first titles

Every champion this year had won before, the first time that has happened across the seven seasons these reports cover. The closest to a first title was Chicago Island, which last won in 2068, and Marigny, last a champion in 2042.

## What the numbers say

### Close duals

25.6 percent of boys varsity duals and 24.5 percent of girls varsity duals were decided by one point, close to the historical norm. 49 duals across both seasons ended level and went to the deciding tiebreakers.

### Records likely to slip or climb in 2096

The same check as in past reports, teams whose dual record and flight record disagree.

**Likely to slip:**
- Boys: Grizzly Gulch (5A, 19-12 after a 35-2 season, but the drop is mostly graduation, see below), Fort Paynes (7A, 6-10 on a roster that graduated most of a 25-4 team)
- Girls: Charles Harbor (Group 1, 18-15 following a 24-2 season)

**Likely to climb:**
- Boys: Lake Esperanza (6A, 21-9, up from 8-19, still undervalued on paper), Narragansett (7A, 16-13, up from 5-21)
- Girls: Golden Gate (7A, 8-14, up from 1-22), Franklin (8A, 9-16, up from 1-23)

**Biggest climbs**

- Boys: Montpelier (5A), 15-11 to 26-3 and a quarterfinal; Delbarton (8A), 17-16 to 28-6 and a quarterfinal; Halbrook (9A), 7-15 to 21-11.
- Girls: Edgewater (Group 1), 9-17 to 20-10; Blue Valley (5A), 11-16 to 24-8 and a quarterfinal; North Antler (2A), 10-10 to 14-3.

**Biggest falls**

- Girls: Barley Gap (1A), 23-10 to 4-25; Monongahela (Group 1), 22-9 to 6-22; Portola-Caverly (1A), 22-4 to 10-12.

## Players

### Players of the year

Kian Chung of Lake Esperanza was 6A player of the year as a freshman, going 32-0. Jacob Hyatt of Anchor, last year's blocked talent case at 10-20 with the team, moved to a program good enough to make him its player of the year at 32-6.

### Unbeaten in 20 or more matches

Nasir Dwyer of Gagarin went 39-0 at No. 4 doubles, the most wins without a loss by anyone. His teammates Nolan Gamboa (37-0, freshman) and Nicholas Young (37-0, sophomore) gave Gagarin three unbeaten doubles players on its 4A championship roster. Dorothy Coleman of Romero-Finniski went 35-0 at No. 3 singles for the 5A girls champions.

## Looking to 2096

Pacific Friends brings back a young core in both genders and projects as the top team in 6A again. Westfield Friends graduates Shepard Butler, its 5A player of the year, but returns enough of its lineup to remain a top projection alongside Condotti Vanguard Academy and Romero-Finniski, the class's three most consistent programs since the 2093 move.

## Notes on the data

Programs are matched on program id throughout. Records, honors and titles are as the exports state them. No player ratings are used anywhere in this report. Brackets and finals are taken from the championships file. A collapse means winning at least 70 percent of duals one year and under 30 percent the next; one occurred this year.
