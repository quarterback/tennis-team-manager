# JHSAA 2098 Season Retrospective

The 2098 season is the last full-season baseline under the existing Road-to-State structure before the 2099 16-team State pilot. It is especially useful because it produced both the normal pattern — Zonal champions dominating the title picture — and several unusually strong exceptions.

## 2098 in one sentence

**The boys postseason was almost perfectly sorted by the Zonal system; the girls postseason produced three genuine Semi-State champions; everything below Semi-State generated exactly one semifinal run in 24 brackets.**

Twenty-one of 24 champions were Zonal champions: **87.5%**.

All 12 boys champions won their Zonal.

The only three teams to win State without one were Blackpine, Central West End and Elk Run on the girls side. All three came through **Semi-State**, not one of the deeper recovery mechanisms.

That matters.

## The Road-to-State evidence got even stronger

Across both genders, 2098 had 192 Zonal champions and 192 Semi-State winners in State. Beneath them were another 488 entrants from Divisionals, State Specials, committee/at-large routes, etc.

| Route | QF | SF | Finals | Titles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Zonal champions | 144 | 79 | 43 | 21 |
| Semi-State winners | 37 | 16 | 5 | 3 |
| Divisional winners | 7 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| State Specials | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Committee/other | 3 | **1** | 0 | 0 |

**The 488 teams below the Semi-State line produced eleven quarterfinalists, one semifinalist, no finalist and no champion.**

The entire competitive argument for retaining that enormous lower recovery apparatus in 2098 boils down to one team.

And that team was excellent television.

### Minidoka is the argument against making the cutoff too doctrinaire

Boys Group 1 Minidoka entered State as the **No. 34 seed**.

It then:

- beat Pendleton 5-4 in Parastate;
- beat Toussaint 6-3;
- beat Frontier 6-3;
- hammered Grant 8-1 in the quarterfinal;
- finally lost 8-1 to Canal View in the semifinal.

That is a real run. And it was not a random terrible team getting hot: Minidoka was 23-10 and had made State in each of the previous two years. In 2097 it had reached the octofinals.

So there is a genuine cost to a hard 16-team field: **2098 Minidoka disappears.**

But it is revealing that the one obvious counterexample came from **488 lower-route entries**.

This is almost exactly the trade the 2099 pilot is testing.

## The three girls who justified Semi-State

These are much more important.

Blackpine was already the defending 7A champion, but it lost its Zonal and entered State No. 11. It then ran through:

- Manzanita, 9-0
- Moreno, 6-3
- Cherry Hill East, 7-2
- Golden State, 7-2
- West El Paso, 5-4

That is not an accidental champion. Blackpine simply failed to win one postseason dual before State.

Central West End is an even cleaner argument for the recovery structure. It had been the 6A State finalist in 2097. In 2098 it went 30-7, lost before earning a Zonal title, entered State No. 10, then beat Coltrane, San Borondón, Hawk Lake, Belle Rive and Fruitvale for the championship.

Elk Run entered 3A State No. 10 and won five straight:

- Norbrook 3-2
- Cassius 3-2
- Barclay 3-2
- Fogline 3-2
- No. 1 California Canyons 3-2

That is possibly the purest Cinderella championship of 2098: **five consecutive one-position wins.**

Those three teams are why Semi-State remains competitively important.

They are also why simply taking the eight Zonal champions would be too restrictive. The recovery mechanism caught exactly the teams it was supposed to catch.

## The boys championship picture

The boys side was much more establishment-heavy, but not boring.

Evans Larsen Day finally converted its recent 9A run into the title. It had gone quarterfinal → finalist → champion from 2096-98 and beat No. 7 Plainfield 5-4 in the final.

Baptist completed a **three-peat in 8A**. Its last three seasons are 32-1, 42-0 and 33-2, with three consecutive State titles. It beat No. 1 Caldera 7-2 in the final.

Kernwood County repeated in 6A after moving down from 7A in 2097. It beat Providence Academy 5-2.

The 5A rivalry stayed excellent. Romero-Finniski followed its 2097 title with a 28-2 season and the No. 1 State seed, but Condotti Vanguard Academy beat it **6-5** in the final. Over the last three seasons, those two programs have accounted for every 5A final appearance except one side of 2096.

Salmon Bay's trajectory remains absurd. It won 1A in 2096, moved to 2A, won 2A in 2097, and repeated in 2098. This year's final was a 3-2 win over No. 1 Villard.

Kishwaukee went **42-0**, repeated as Group 1 champion, and then won the Tournament of Champions.

That was the strongest complete boys season.

## Tournament of Champions

The boys TOC reinforced Kishwaukee's season.

It beat Salmon Bay 3-2, then shut out Baptist 5-0, then beat Evans Larsen Day 3-2 in the championship.

So Kishwaukee did not just go undefeated against Group 1. It closed the year by beating the 2A, 8A and 9A champions.

The girls TOC belonged to Evans Larsen Day.

It had already completed a third straight 9A championship and entered TOC at 41-1. It beat Kilbride Switch 3-2, Gottschalk-Herman 4-1, then Fletcher-Garrison Hall 3-2 in the final.

That gives Evans Larsen Day girls the clearest claim to being the defining program of this particular stretch: three straight 9A championships and the 2098 TOC.

## Some of the best seasons were not championships

Los Remolinos is the obvious boys example.

It was only the No. 14 seed in 3A after going 25-8. Then it beat Cape Angeles 5-0 and won **three straight 3-2 duals** over Fogline, Governor Woods and Quarmont before finally losing 3-2 to Barclay in the State final.

That is exactly the kind of Semi-State-team run the association does not want to eliminate.

Naylors Landing did something similar in girls Group 2. It entered State at No. 19 but had come through Semi-State. It beat Riverview, Nurmes and Joseph and got all the way to the semifinal before losing to eventual champion Doyleville Union on a 3-3 tiebreak.

And Adrian girls had another meaningful season: 27-5, Golden West champion, Zonal champion, Epiregional champion, and a Group 2 semifinal appearance after losing in the octofinals in 2097.

There were some very large non-title stories.

- **Forge:** 5-18 → 19-12 and into State.
- **Fir Valley:** 4-22 → 17-14 and into State.
- **Hermiston:** 6-20 → 19-12 and into State.
- **St. Teresa:** 12-14 → 24-6 and reached the octofinals.
- **Driftwood:** 16-15 → 26-5 and reached the octofinals.
- **Kuusamo:** 8-15 → 20-10 and reached the octofinals.

### Girls

- **New Baltimore:** **0-26 → 13-13**, then reached State and the Round of 24.
- **St. Teresa:** 5-21 → 20-10.
- **Tungsten:** 3-20 → 20-14 and reached State.
- **Siskiyou Valley:** 4-19 → 19-12 and reached State.
- **Willow Creek:** 5-18 → 19-13 and reached State.
- **Powder Valley:** 12-16 → 19-7 and reached the octofinals.
- **Marshfield Prep:** 18-11 → 27-4 and reached the 9A quarterfinals.

St. Teresa girls are especially interesting because **20-10 still was not enough to make the State main draw**; they died at Metastate. That is a useful example of a team for whom the broader Road still mattered even under the oversized system.

The collapses went the other way just as violently. Boys Van Buren fell from 17-12 to 2-21. Girls Ninemile went from 20-10 and a State appearance to **1-25**.

## Transfers produced real stories

The best one was not an elite team stockpiling talent.

Boys Group 2 **Liam Kim** transferred from Southridge Christian to Tungsten. Tungsten itself was only 11-14 and did not make State.

Kim, an 11th-grader, then won the **Group 2 No. 1 singles State championship as the No. 5 seed**.

That is nearly the ideal result for the transfer system: a very good player leaves a crowded context, lands somewhere that is not a powerhouse, and creates an individual story that otherwise would not exist.

Girls Group 3 had two of them.

**Harmony Dunn**, a sophomore transfer from Tallulah Central to Eatonville, won the Group 3 first-singles championship as the **No. 8 seed**. Eatonville itself was only 16-16 and finished in the Round of 24.

And Burnt River's State-finalist lineup was materially built through transfers:

- Brooke York, formerly Canal View, won No. 2 doubles.
- Brielle Beehler, formerly Kernwood County, partnered York on that title.
- Ririsitiri Manganyi, formerly Coyote Bend, was part of the No. 3 doubles championship.

Burnt River went from 20-11 and a Parastate exit in 2097 to **26-6, No. 2 seed, Group 3 finalist**.

That is a transfer market actually changing the competitive map without simply handing a title to a superteam.

A few first-singles champions particularly stood out.

### Boys

- 9A: **Ryan Morgan**, Mickey Mantle, No. 9 seed. Mickey Mantle was only a No. 26 team seed and lost in the Round of 32.
- Group 2: **Liam Kim**, Tungsten, No. 5. Tungsten did not make State.
- 4A: **Steve Campbell**, Walter-Kenny, No. 8.
- 2A: **Jimmy Kitchen**, Puerto Alma, No. 5; Puerto Alma was only the No. 19 team seed.

### Girls

- Group 3: **Harmony Dunn**, Eatonville, No. 8; 16-16 team.
- Group 2: **Khloe Campos**, Guthrie, No. 7; Guthrie was only the No. 27 team seed.
- 3A: **Jocelyn Slagle**, Fogline, No. 7.
- 9A: **Emery Brown**, Puerto Reyes, No. 5; Puerto Reyes was No. 12 in the team field.

Those are good outcomes. The individual tournament is not just reproducing the team hierarchy.

## JV also produced something worth following

Jackson Hole boys won JV State as the No. 2 seed after going 19-0 in JV competition. Its varsity also won Group 2 State.

That is the scary version of a championship program: the developmental team is winning too.

Girls JV State was stranger. **Evans Larsen Day won from the No. 9 seed**, while No. 31 Central West End reached the final.

Both schools also had enormous varsity seasons — Evans Larsen Day won 9A and TOC; Central West End won 6A.

So the two strongest girls stories at varsity level also had the deepest JV postseason.

## V2/V3 reached meaningful scale

2098 recorded:

- **boys:** 166 V2 duals and 27 V3 duals
- **girls:** 127 V2 duals and 12 V3 duals

This is no longer a hypothetical roster designation. There are now hundreds of matches creating playing opportunities below the first varsity lineup.

One season is not enough to draw a strong competitive conclusion, but it is enough volume that 2099 should begin to produce useful multi-year player histories from the split-squad system.

There are a lot of returning individual champions.

On the boys side, Ryan Morgan, Jimmy Kitchen, Liam Poland, Shane Brooks, Faheem Mahmood, Eric Langston, Liam Kim, Tate Cho and Kristian Sokk all won first-singles titles before their senior year.

The particularly interesting ones are:

- **Liam Kim**, junior, now with the unusual combination of being a State singles champion on a non-State team.

Girls has a younger set too:

- Adriana Cordero, Lukky Kambuaya and Emery Brown are juniors and also return.

So there should be considerable continuity in Individual State even if some of the team landscape turns over.

## What 2098 says about the 2099 State pilot

The 2098 results make the natural competitive hierarchy look like:

**Zonal championship = direct State berth.**

**Semi-State = legitimate second chance.**

**Everything after Semi-State = overwhelmingly access/participation.**

2098 supplied unusually good stress tests for that claim:

- Three champions failed to win Zonals. **All three survived through Semi-State.**
- Sixteen of the 17 non-Zonal semifinalists came through Semi-State.
- A No. 14 reached a boys State final through Semi-State.
- A No. 19 reached a girls State semifinal through Semi-State.
- Among the enormous pool below that line, only Minidoka reached a semifinal.
- Nobody from Divisionals, State Specials or committee/at-large routes reached a final or won State.

So the statistic to watch under the 2099 pilot is not championships anymore. That question is largely settled.

The revealing comparison will be how much memorable competitive value disappears when seeds 17 and below no longer have a State route — especially whether 2098 Minidoka proves to be the kind of rare anomaly the association can accept losing in exchange for making State meaningfully scarce.
