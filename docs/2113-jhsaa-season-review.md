# The 2039 (2113) JHSAA Tennis Season

*Internal annual review · boys and girls · from the two 2039 research exports*

Two classification runners-up played for the boys' Tournament of Champions title. Penobscot lost the 8A State final 5–4 to Oakhaven, returned through the qualifier and won four TOC duals, including the final 4–1 over Chaminade, another State runner-up. Mercy Academy Valley's girls finished the season 39–1, won 10B for the first time since 2043 and returned to the TOC final they had lost in 2112, this time defeating Porterfield 3–2. Across the association, Coltrane swept the 5A State titles; the girls won their first championship in the archive and the boys their second.

This season also carried **288 boys' and girls' classification changes** from the previous season into another year of substantial movement, the seventh Circuit round, a 120-team JV State draw in both genders, and the first substantial recorded year of the JHSAA's attribute-specific coaching model. The complete technical assessment of coach bond, skill development and graduated players' synthetic college careers is in the [2113 coaching, development and alumni companion](reports/JHSAA-2113-coaching-development-and-alumni-review.md).

## Executive findings

**Penobscot won the boys' TOC after losing State.** A 38–3 season included a third straight 7A title in 2112, promotion to 8A, a 5–4 loss to Oakhaven in the 2113 State final, and then the TOC championship. Penobscot beat Black Springs Country Day 4–1 in the qualifier, California Canyons 3–2, Southridge Christian 3–2, Jackson Hole 4–1 and Chaminade 4–1. Chaminade had lost the 10B final 7–2 to Southridge Christian, qualified 3–2 against Banneker and subsequently reached the TOC final. Both finalists had entered through the two runner-up qualifiers.

**Mercy Academy Valley returned and won.** Its girls lost the 2112 TOC final 3–2 to undefeated Romero-Finniski. In 2113, Mercy won 10B 6–3 against Goldbank Hall, beat Silton Ridge 4–1, Pacific Gate 4–1 and Perry Green 4–1, and edged Porterfield 3–2 for the TOC. The 39–1 season ended a 70-season wait between the program's two recorded State championships.

**Coltrane swept 5A.** The boys (24–6, fourth seed) defeated Crater View 7–4 for their first State title since 2078. The girls (24–5, seventh seed) won their first championship of the archive 6–5 over Roscoe Village. Their win stopped Roscoe Village's attempt to win a first recorded final since its previous appearance in 2051.

**Four teams won their first recorded State title.** Corey Canyon North (3A boys, eleventh seed), Pine Barrens (6A boys), Coltrane (5A girls) and Ferris County (6A girls). Corey Canyon North defeated Villard 3–2 after entering the bracket from No. 11; Ferris County won the championship in its first recorded State final.

**L.B. Johnson won a fourth consecutive 9A boys title.** The 38–2 team defeated Banneker 7–2. Romero-Finniski's boys extended their 11B run to five consecutive titles; its girls repeated in 11B, then lost 3–2 to Eden in the TOC.

**The Group 2 boys' championship ended in a 3–3 dual.** Union won the title over Twin Rivers on the association's tiebreak procedure. Both teams won three flights; the stored State dual identifies Union as the winner.

**The seventh Circuit round produced one State champion and considerably more deep runs.** Thorn Summit's girls won 1A at 23–9 from the No. 7 seed. Buckeye Ridge's girls reached the 4A final. Thirteen additional Circuit champions reached a State semifinal. None of the 72 boys Circuit winners reached a final.

**Realignment still shaped the scores.** Between 2112 and 2113, 142 boys programs and 146 girls programs changed classification. Programs moving up lost approximately 14–15 winning-percentage points on average, while those moving down gained approximately 11–14. Boys Benchlands went from 14–20 in 6A to 26–2 in 5A; girls Paddock Episcopal moved from 7–16 in 10B to 24–2 in 11B. Girls Brackwood Pass, 37–1 and a 3A champion in 2112, finished 18–10 and lost in the round of 32.

**Senior varsity participation fell modestly.** Of 13,668 seniors, 1,884 (13.8%) played no varsity flight in 2113, against 12.6% in 2112. Nearly all of those students—1,871—had JV appearances. Meanwhile 585 seventh-graders and 1,026 eighth-graders were on the association's rosters.

## How this review reads the season

The 2039 boys' and girls' research ZIPs identify the JHSAA season as **2113**. Program histories run back to 2027. A “first” means the earliest such outcome in that archive; “State” means the classification team championship, while the Tournament of Champions is the later tournament bringing together State champions and qualifier winners. Private schools retain the ordinary league placement in their program histories and compete for team titles on the 10B or 11B road. The 2113 champion names, records and seeds below come from the archived championship brackets and program history, with direct dual records used for TOC and JV results.

Individual player ratings are internal calibration and do not appear in this sporting review. The separate research companion uses those backend data only to assess the development model. Results such as the 2113 college-alumni entries are explicitly identified as precomputed college-career histories; their four college seasons are generated at high-school graduation rather than played by the college engine.

## Two very different Tournament of Champions

### Boys: an all-qualifier final

The qualifier paired **9A runner-up Banneker** against **10B runner-up Chaminade**, and **8A runner-up Penobscot** against **11B runner-up Black Springs Country Day**. Chaminade advanced 3–2; Penobscot advanced 4–1.

Chaminade then defeated L.B. Johnson 4–1, Pine Barrens 5–0 and Seamus Town 4–1. Penobscot beat California Canyons 3–2, Southridge Christian 3–2 and Jackson Hole 4–1. It was an all-qualifier final, the same structural surprise the girls' event produced in 2111.

Penobscot won three doubles flights and No. 1 singles in its 4–1 final victory. **Archie Rocha** beat Conrad Watson **6–3, 4–6, 6–2** at No. 1 singles; **Lawson Willems and Garrett Moreno** won D1 in three sets, **Willie Dalton and Brian Feliciano** won D2 in straight sets, and **Leo Deshazo and Alexander Smith** won D3 in three. Chaminade won D4 through Trent Susnjar and Joey Dewar.

Penobscot's run followed three consecutive 7A titles through 2112 and a move into 8A, where Oakhaven beat it 5–4 for State. The TOC was a second route through a different mix of opponents. Both 8A finalists were strong enough to win something consequential; only one could take the classification title.

### Girls: Mercy Academy Valley answers 2112

**Seawall** beat Goldbank Hall **4–1** in the 9A/10B qualifier. **Coastal Christian** beat Oakhaven **3–2** in the 8A/11B qualifier. Seawall beat 3A champion Bay Oregon 4–1 before losing 3–2 to Porterfield; Coastal Christian lost 3–2 to 9A champion Pacific Gate in its first TOC dual.

Mercy Academy Valley entered as the 10B champion. It beat Silton Ridge 4–1, Pacific Gate 4–1 and Perry Green 4–1. Porterfield eliminated Ferris County 3–2, Seawall 3–2 and Ocean Park 3–2 to reach the final. Both sides had beaten championship-caliber programs to get there.

In the final, **Alexa Urban** won Mercy's 10B State No. 1 singles title but lost the TOC final's S1 to **Zenda Gowan** of Porterfield, 6–3, 4–6, 6–2 from Gowan's perspective. Mercy took three of four doubles flights: Victoria Masson and Nora Abraham won D1, Jessica Archie and Ella Downey won D3, and Fiona Farrell and Gianna Day won D4. Porterfield's D2 pair Erica Verma and Eliana Hood won its other flight. Three doubles victories gave Mercy the 3–2 championship.

Romero-Finniski won another 11B title, then lost its opening TOC dual 3–2 to Eden. Its 2112 perfect season and consecutive TOC titles had been the standard against which the new season was measured. Mercy finished the 2113 season with the title Romero-Finniski had kept from it a year earlier.

With four qualifier winners in 2113, the running history since the TOC qualifier was introduced rises from **six champions in forty qualifying entries through 2112** to **seven champions in forty-four entries through 2113**: Penobscot added the seventh.

The opposite direction contains several of the largest recoveries. **Benchlands boys** moved 6A → 5A and went from 14–20 to 26–2, reaching a quarterfinal. **Yandell boys** moved Group 2 → Group 3 and went 9–17 to 25–7, reaching the final. **Paddock Episcopal girls** moved 10B → 11B and went from 7–16 to 24–2, reaching an octofinal. **Montpelier girls** moved 6A → 5A and went 3–23 to 16–12.

Porterfield and Bay Oregon went in opposite directions and both won. The competitive change belongs in any reading of their records.

One program illustrates two steps of the moving map. **Barley Gap** swept 1A after dropping from 3A in 2112. Both genders were placed in 2A for 2113 and finished 20–4 (boys) and 20–5 (girls), each giving up the 1A titles but remaining competitive. The earlier title sweep remains a real achievement; the next season simply asked it of different opposition.

## Four first titles, and long waits that ended

**Corey Canyon North's boys** won their first recorded State title at 33–6, from the **No. 11 seed** in 3A. Gunnar Sands coached the team to a 3–2 victory over Villard. **Pine Barrens' boys**, 33–5, won their first 6A title 5–2 over Morne Caribou under Timothy Schubert. **Ferris County's girls** won 6A 5–2 over Grand Fond, reaching their first State final and winning it under Eddie Miralles. **Coltrane's girls** did the same in 5A, winning a 6–5 final over Roscoe Village under Brayden Lopez.

Several other waits were substantial. **Meridia's girls** won Group 3 for the first time since **2028** (85 seasons). **Thorn Summit's girls** won 1A for the first time since **2041** (72 seasons). **Mercy Academy Valley's girls** waited **70 seasons** between 10B titles. **Kendricks Landing's boys** had last won State in **2050**, and the 2113 Group 3 title ended a 63-season wait. **Coltrane's boys** ended a 35-season gap since 2078.

Teams that reached important rounds without winning State also changed their histories. **Yarmere's boys** reached their first recorded 1A final; **Black Springs Country Day's boys** reached their first 11B final. Among the girls, **East Simmons** reached its first Group 3 final from the **No. 20 seed**, **Goldbank Hall** its first 10B final from No. 10, and **Carolina Island** its first 1A final. **Fort Lassiter's girls**, a No. 12 Group 2 finalist, ended a 52-season wait since 2061. **Naylors Landing's boys** reached their first Group 2 semifinal after 69 archive seasons, while **Antler Falls's girls** reached their first 1A semifinal after 69.

## The fourth straight 9A title and the private-school road

**L.B. Johnson's boys** won 9A for the fourth consecutive year at 38–2, beating Banneker 7–2. Its top singles and doubles performances included **Jessie Parrish**, the 9A boys' Player of the Year at 30–9, and the squad remained among the league's leading sources of returning varsity flights. **Pacific Gate's girls** won 9A 6–3 over Seawall at 33–2, ending Seawall's consecutive title run. That final was particularly doubles-driven: Pacific Gate won D1 through D4 and two of four singles flights.

The 10B boys' State final looked unlike the later TOC. **Southridge Christian**, the No. 6 seed, beat No. 8 **Chaminade** 7–2 for State. Chaminade then beat three State champions in the TOC before losing to Penobscot. In 11B, **Romero-Finniski's boys** won their fifteenth recorded State title, fifth straight, 5–2 over Black Springs Country Day. **Romero-Finniski's girls** won a twenty-first title, second straight, 5–2 over Coastal Christian, then exited the TOC against Eden.

## The JV competition and who actually played

The JV team championship in the 2113 export used **thirty geographically ordered JV Regions**, with four survivors from each regional qualifier entering a **120-team State field**. Its archived rounds were R120, Triple Octafinals, Doubles Octafinals, Octafinals, Quarterfinals, Semifinals and Final. This is the **active 2113 format recorded in the export**, superseding the earlier 36-team selected-field version of the JV event.

**Canyonlands's boys** won JV State as the **No. 16 seed**, beating Southridge Christian **4–1** in the final after defeating Rampart 4–1 in the semifinal. **Seawall's girls**, the **No. 2 seed**, beat Ocean Park **4–2** in the final after defeating Los Remolinos 4–2. The JV State archive contains 119 State duals per gender plus the qualifying competitions preceding it.

Across all varsity and JV phases, the 2113 export records **13,301 boys varsity duals and 10,066 boys JV duals**, and **13,876 girls varsity duals and 10,492 girls JV duals**. Deep programs also staged second and third squads: 662 boys and 737 girls dual records carried a V2 or V3 designation. These are distinct schedule entries, and the squad side does not become a varsity record simply by opposing another school's V1.

Seniors' varsity participation remained an important measure. There were **980 of 6,693 boys seniors (14.6%)** and **904 of 6,975 girls seniors (13.0%)** with no varsity flight in this season. Of those students, **973 boys and 898 girls had at least one JV flight**. Just seven boys and six girls seniors recorded no varsity or JV flight in the exported season. This is a different picture from the early association's years when most players outside the first eleven lacked any recorded schedule.

The youngest participants remained a distinct small-school population. **181 of 293 seventh-grade boys** and **167 of 292 seventh-grade girls** appeared in varsity flights; **311 of 484 eighth-grade boys** and **339 of 542 eighth-grade girls** did. The class-specific participation and early-development rules are assessed in the companion technical review. The standard ninth- through twelfth-grade participation rates continue to rise with grade.

## Coaches, recognition and the changing schools

The State Coach of the Year awards mostly followed significant team results, with a few important exceptions. **Hunter Gracia of Penobscot** won 8A boys Coach of the Year after the 38–3 season and State runner-up finish that preceded the TOC title. **Nabil Nava of Yarmere** won 1A boys Coach of the Year after taking the school to its first recorded final. **Samuel Clark of Yandell** earned Group 3 boys Coach of the Year as the No. 6 seed finalist after the move from Group 2. Among the girls, **Courtney Brown of Coastal Christian** earned 11B Coach of the Year after a 30–7 season and final against Romero-Finniski. Each award considers performance relative to expectations, beyond simply naming the State champion's coach.

The coaches of the four first-time State champions were **Gunnar Sands** (Corey Canyon North boys), **Timothy Schubert** (Pine Barrens boys), **Brayden Lopez** (Coltrane girls) and **Eddie Miralles** (Ferris County girls). **Joseph Key** coached the Coltrane boys' side of the 5A sweep. **Miles Hodges** led Mercy Academy Valley's girls through their 10B and TOC titles. Head-coach careers now have their own persistent history, and the new bond/annuity system can recognize prior success separately from an individual season's award.

The first substantial player-development panel is now available for analysis: more than fourteen thousand players have been credited with skill-specific instruction under named coaches. The [technical companion](reports/JHSAA-2113-coaching-development-and-alumni-review.md) separates that instructional record from the reputation and match results of the head coaches, and documents an accounting issue identified in the final skill caps.

## The graduating class, and the college epilogue

The JHSAA-only game now creates abbreviated, **precomputed four-year collegiate careers** for selected graduating players. The first Model 2 class in these exports is the **2113 graduating class**: 245 boys and 294 girls received college records, with 39 boys and 52 girls initially signing in the simulated D1 tier. The rest went to D2, D3 and the game's academic-focused D4. Careers include reserves, doubles specialists, transfers, honors and players whose tennis ended before graduation from college.

Some of the 2113 high-school individual honorees appear at familiar colleges in those generated histories. **Ty Antrim**, Estuary Bay's 3A boys Player of the Year, signs at LSU and follows a transfer-success career. **Benjamin Woods**, the 2A boys' Player of the Year and No. 1 singles champion from Salmon Bay, signs at Oregon and leaves the team after one season in this simulation. **Alexa Urban**, Mercy Academy Valley's 10B girls' Player of the Year and singles State champion, signs at Indiana and follows a stalled-prospect trajectory over four years. These are consequences supplied by the graduated-player tracker, not results of actual played NCAA seasons. The companion report sets out the complete division and attrition distribution.

## Alternate 2113

Each counterfactual changes one recorded result and leaves everything before it as played. A State-final change would alter subsequent TOC qualification and placement; its later TOC bracket would require a new simulation.

**Fork: Penobscot closes one flight in the 8A State final.** Oakhaven won 5–4. A one-flight reversal gives Penobscot the State title as well as the talent demonstrated by its later TOC win; the qualifier and TOC draw would then change.

**Fork: Canal View closes one flight in the Group 1 boys' final.** Jackson Hole won 5–4. A reversal gives Canal View another Group 1 title after its 40–2 season; the TOC field would be reordered accordingly.

**Fork: Twin Rivers takes the Group 2 tiebreak.** Union and Twin Rivers split their championship six flights 3–3. A different tiebreak outcome gives the title to Twin Rivers after its 27–6 season.

**Fork: Roscoe Village closes one flight in the girls' 5A final.** Coltrane won 6–5. A reversal gives Roscoe Village its long-awaited State title and ends Coltrane's boys/girls 5A sweep.

**Fork: Porterfield takes one more doubles flight in the girls' TOC final.** Mercy Academy Valley won 3–2. Reverse a single winning Mercy doubles flight and Porterfield becomes the Tournament of Champions winner; Mercy finishes as 10B champion and TOC finalist again.
