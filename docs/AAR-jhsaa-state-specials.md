# AAR — State Specials as a quality-control round

**Date:** 2026-08  
**Status:** Proposed/implemented owner rule, 2026-08  
**Purpose:** Preserve the broad-access Road to State while reducing the number of weak regular-season teams that reach State solely through a favorable recovery path.

## Background

The JHSAA Road to State was designed to give teams multiple ways to recover from one bad postseason result. That remains the core principle.

Earlier in the dynasty, losing-record teams rarely reached State. As the association matured, league strength spread out, talent diffused unevenly, and the recovery ladder began producing more path-dependent qualifiers. A team with a poor regular season can sometimes survive the right sequence of recovery matches and reach State, while a much stronger regular-season team can be eliminated by one difficult matchup.

Neither outcome is invalid. The problem is that the existing recovery system sometimes gives the weaker regular-season team multiple ways back while a strong team can still have its season end on one fluke loss.

State Specials is intended as a mitigation, not a replacement for the Road to State.

## What the recent data showed

A review of the 2045–2050 exports found that losing-record State qualifiers are concentrated in the final recovery gate.

Across boys and girls combined:

| Qualification route | Losing-record State qualifiers |
| --- | ---: |
| Conference | **230** |
| Semi-State | 61 |
| Divisionals | 60 |
| Super Regional | 34 |
| Zonal | 11 |

Conference is therefore the clearest pressure point.

The pattern is not theoretical. Recent seasons contain examples where teams with poor regular-season records reached State through Conference while substantially better teams in the same classification were already eliminated.

Examples from 2050 included:

| Class | Conference winner | Record | Strong nonqualifier | Record |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Boys Group 1 | Valois Bluffs | 7-15 | Terrace Park | 19-8 |
| Boys 4A | Lonepine Canyon | 7-16 | Driftwood | 17-9 |
| Boys Group 2 | Clear Lake | 8-14 | Ruby County Catholic | 15-8 |
| Boys 7A | Wheeler Academy | 10-12 | Mercer City | 18-7 |
| Boys 9A | Malpais | 11-10 | Southridge Christian | 21-6 |
| Girls 3A | Tunnelton | 8-19 | Norbrook | 17-6 |

| Girls 7A | Bahía Leal | 10-10 | Vicksburg | 20-7 |
| Girls 9A | Basin Gate | 11-13 | Readbury | 19-9 |

Similar mismatches appeared in 2048 and earlier seasons.

The useful conclusion is narrow: Conference is where the current system most often converts a weak regular season into a State berth.

## The change

Conference winners no longer qualify directly for State.

They advance to **State Specials**.

For each classification and gender:

1. Play the existing Road to State through Conference.
2. Collect every Conference winner.
3. Build a challenger pool from **every team in that classification and gender that is not already qualified for State and is not a Conference winner**.
4. Rank that challenger pool by regular-season performance.
5. Select the same number of challengers as Conference winners.
6. Pair Conference winners against challengers in one State Specials round.
7. State Specials winners take the final State berths.

The challenger pool is statewide within the classification. It is not limited to teams that reached Conference, recovery, or any particular postseason round. It can include teams eliminated in Conference, Semi-Conference, Divisionals, Semi-State, Super Regionals, Zonals, Regionals, Wards, Sectionals, Areas, or teams that never reached the postseason path.

## Challenger ranking

State Specials exists to restore weight to sustained regular-season performance.

Challengers are ranked by:

1. regular-season winning percentage
2. regular-season wins
3. ATR as the final tiebreak

Postseason results do not count toward the winning percentage or win total used to select challengers.

## Current field arithmetic

### 32-team State field

The current recovery structure produces:

- 8 Zonal champions
- 12 Semi-State qualifiers
- 6 Divisional qualifiers
- 6 Conference winners

Under State Specials, those six Conference winners become provisional:

- 8 Zonal champions
- 12 Semi-State qualifiers
- 6 Divisional qualifiers
- 6 State Specials winners
- **32 total**

State Specials field:

- 6 Conference winners
- 6 best remaining nonqualifiers by regular-season record
- 12 teams
- 6 dual matches
- 6 State qualifiers

### 24-team State field

The fixed 24-team shape produces four Conference winners.

State Specials therefore has:

- 4 Conference winners
- 4 challengers
- 4 State Specials winners

### 40-team State field

The 40-team recovery shape produces 14 Conference winners.

State Specials therefore has:

- 14 Conference winners
- 14 challengers
- 14 State Specials winners

The number of Specials is derived from the number of Conference winners rather than hard-coded to a field size.

## Why Conference is the right gate

The data does not support cutting Semi-State in half and protecting those removed berths for Specials. Semi-State does produce some losing-record qualifiers, but Conference produces far more.

Keeping Semi-State and Divisionals intact preserves the existing recovery logic while applying the quality-control round exactly where the weak-qualifier problem is concentrated.

The revised hierarchy is:

**Zonals → direct State qualification**

**Semi-State → direct State qualification**

**Divisionals → direct State qualification**

**Conference → State Specials**

**State Specials → final State berths**

This also creates a useful symmetry in the Road to State.

The cleanest path requires no recovery. A Zonal champion is in.

Teams that fall into recovery must keep proving themselves. The deepest recovery route now requires the most proof: a team that reaches Conference must still defeat one of the strongest regular-season teams left outside the field.

The recovery system therefore continues to protect teams from having an otherwise excellent season end on one fluke postseason loss, while State Specials prevents the last recovery gate from becoming too permissive.

## What State Specials is meant to solve

State Specials is not designed to keep every losing-record team out of State.

A losing-record team can still qualify. If it wins through Conference and then beats one of the strongest remaining nonqualifiers, it has earned the berth on court.

The goal is narrower:

- preserve second chances for teams that lose once in the postseason
- protect strong regular-season teams from having no route back after one bad match
- reduce the number of weak regular-season teams that reach State only because their recovery path was unusually favorable
- keep every final berth decided by a dual match rather than an at-large selection

The point is not that a 7-16 Conference winner is illegitimate. The point is that a 19-8 team left outside the field should have one final opportunity to prove that it belongs there more.

## What to evaluate after several seasons

State Specials should be evaluated from actual outcomes, not assumed to work because the format sounds cleaner.

Track:

- number of State Specials by classification and gender
- records of Conference winners entering Specials
- records of challengers entering Specials
- challenger win rate
- number of losing-record State qualifiers before and after the change
- number of strong regular-season teams rescued by Specials
- whether Conference remains the main source of weak provisional qualifiers
- whether Specials disproportionately affects any classification
- whether the challenger pool becomes weak in classes with large numbers of Specials
- whether State field quality improves without making recovery meaningless

The key measure is not whether challengers win most of the time.

If a Conference winner with a poor regular season beats a 20-win challenger, the system has done its job: the berth was tested directly.

If Specials rarely changes the field, that is still useful evidence. If it regularly replaces weak Conference winners with clearly stronger challengers, that validates the original concern.

## Design principle

The Road to State exists so that one bad match does not automatically erase a strong season.

State Specials extends that principle to the teams left outside the field.

A team that survives Conference has earned the right to play for State. A team with one of the best remaining regular-season records has earned one final chance to challenge for that berth.

The final place is decided on court.

## Addendum (2026-08): the recovery rebalance the Specials were supposed to bring

When Conference winners stopped qualifying directly and became the Specials
field, the berth arithmetic underneath was left as it was — and it was
front-loaded. A 32-team field ran **8 Zonal + 12 Semi-State + 6 Divisional +
6 Conference**: half of all recovery berths came out of one rung, because
Semi-State was sized by a `ceil(4·berths/3)` FLOOR and absorbed the readmitted
Super Regional losers on top of its own field. Owner: *"having that many teams
get through via semi-state doesn't make any sense … it should be adjusted at
every level per the AAR for creating specials."*

The corrected pathway — one ladder for every class, only the counts moving:

```
Areas → Sectionals → Wards → Regionals → Zonals
      → Super Regionals → Semi-State → Divisionals
      → (Semi-Conference) → Conference → State Specials → State
```

| field | Zonal | Semi-State | Divisional | Specials | classes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 24 | 8 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 1A, Group 3 |
| 32 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 2A-7A, Group 1, Group 2 |
| 40 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 16 | 8A, 9A |

Super Regionals award no berths (they qualify for Semi-State), and the
Conference is always the round that FEEDS the Specials — never State directly.

Three rules produce every row, and the ladder's own geometry does most of the
work:

1. **Semi-State is exactly the Super Regional winners plus the Zonal losers.**
   No readmission window, no floor — it is 16 teams at full size and delivers
   8. The Super Regional losers are readmitted one rung LATER, into the
   Divisionals, which is what makes both rounds the same size.
2. **The Divisionals take at most one Zonal-champion-sized block** (8), capped
   by the berths actually outstanding — 4 at a 24 field, 8 at 32 and 40 — and
   their field is **RESERVED HALF AND HALF** between the two tiers: the best
   half of the Semi-State losers and the best half of the Super Regional
   losers, ranked inside each bucket and nothing else.
3. **The Conference absorbs the remainder**, as it always has: 4, 8, 16.

Everyone still gets exactly two live chances (a Regional loser: Super
Regionals, then the Divisionals; a Zonal loser: Semi-State, then the
Divisionals), which was the inequity the Divisional round was created to fix.

‼️ **Rule 2's reserved halves are what keep that true, and the first cut of
this change did not have them.** The Divisional pool was ranked as ONE list,
Semi-State losers ahead of Super Regional losers on strict tier priority. That
is invisible at 32 and 40, where both tiers hold 8 and all 16 fit the field
anyway — and silently wrong at 24, where the field is 8: the Semi-State losers
took every slot and **no Super Regional loser reached the round at all**. A
Regional loser then had Super Regionals and nothing berth-bearing until the
Conference, against three berth-bearing rounds for a Zonal loser — a whole
stage of the recovery ladder deleted for one tier, and a regression from the
retired `_recovery_24`, which had routed them through Semi-State. Owner:
"4 + 4. No weighting. No alternating. No new formulas."

The lesson is the one this file keeps relearning in a new costume: **a rule
that is exercised identically at every size you looked at is not yet tested.**
Strict tier priority and reserved halves agree at 32 and 40 and disagree only
at 24 — the shape that had just been migrated onto this ladder in the same
change.

### `_recovery_24` is retired

The 24-field classes had their own hand-wired ladder whose berths came out of
**Super Regionals** while Semi-State awarded none. The dynamic ladder produces
the owner's 8/8/4/4 exactly once the Divisionals are capped, so every class now
runs `_recovery`. The function is kept, unwired, because seasons are archived
under it and its docstring is what explains them; the district-champion
priority split (first claim on Super Regional slots) is the one thing it had
that the shared ladder does not.

### Consequences to watch

- The **Semi-Conference field grows** with the Conference it qualifies for: 8
  at a 24 class, 16 at a 32, 48 at a 40. The 40-field sponsor floor therefore
  moves 76 → **80**; 8A and 9A sit at 86/86 and 85/85 and clear it. Every other
  class is at the 48 ward gate with room. `sponsor_floor` lost its 24-field
  special case in the same pass — one formula for every class now, and the
  24 answer is unchanged at 48 because the ward gate dominates there.
- **A 40 field is deliberately not even.** 8/8/8/16 is the shape the owner
  specified: the two automatic recovery rungs deliver one block each and the
  Specials take everything left, which is 16 duals in 8A/9A.
