# The 2037 (2111) JHSAA Tennis Season

*Internal annual review*

This was the first season under the class-specific district-dual formats and the revised Tournament of Champions qualifier pairings. It was not a realignment year. The Circuit ran for a fourth season.

The export also carries one map inconsistency forward: Evans Larsen Day is now private and on the 10B road, while Basalt, Peregrine and Washington San Cordero are marked public but still have `championship_group` / `road_group` 11B. This review records all four exactly where the 2111 season actually played them.

## Executive findings

**Mater Dei's boys finished 37-0, swept 10B and won a third straight Tournament of Champions.** They beat Chaminade 5-4 for the 10B title, then Vespertine North 4-1, Villard 5-0, L.B. Johnson 4-1 and Canal View 3-2 in the TOC. It was Mater Dei's 12th boys State title and sixth boys TOC title.

**The revised TOC qualifier pairings split 2-2 between public and private runners-up, then produced an all-qualifier girls final.** Pacific Gate (9A) beat Chaminade (10B) 3-2 and Glen Park (8A) beat Condotti Vanguard Academy (11B) 3-2 on the boys side. On the girls side, Evans Larsen Day (10B) beat Pacific Gate (9A) 4-1 and Romero-Finniski (11B) beat Glen Park (8A) 3-2. Evans Larsen Day and Romero-Finniski then both reached the final, where Romero-Finniski won 3-2.

**The district-format change ran cleanly.** The export contains 14,195 district duals across both genders, and every class used the expected card size: 9 lines in 9A/8A/7A/Group 1/10B, 11 in 5A, 7 in 6A/11B, 6 in Group 2 and 5 in 4A/3A/2A/1A/Group 3. Group 2 produced five 3-3 district draws, which were recorded as ties rather than losses. Individual State also widened to 110 championships per gender, from the old 84, keeping the core flights and adding the extra flights required by the class formats.

**The Circuit is sending more teams deep into State without replacing the Zonal path as the dominant title route.** One Circuit champion won State: Los Remolinos girls in 3A, the 10 seed. South Rim's boys reached the 2A final from the Circuit. Eleven Circuit champions reached a State semifinal or better and 36 reached a quarterfinal or better. But 17 of the 18 State champions in Circuit classes had won their Zonal; Los Remolinos was the only exception.

**Four programs won a first State title:** Golden State (7A boys), Vespertine North (5A boys), Verde Highlands (5A girls) and Irrigon (Group 3 girls). Golden State and Vespertine North won the first State finals they had reached in the archive.

**The ordinary 2111 portal class landed cleanly.** It moved 242 boys and 264 girls. All 506 appear on the destination roster named in the portal data, and all 506 played at least one varsity flight. 202 boys and 235 girls played at least 10 varsity flights; the median was 20 for boys and 22 for girls.

**The old large-city transfer cohort has stabilized below its 2106 peak.** The 11 boys programs won 41.6 percent of their matches and five made State; the 11 girls programs won 43.0 percent and seven made State. Those rates are slightly above the 2109 collapse and essentially flat with 2110, but none reached a State final.

The headline is not the one championship. It is the depth. Thirty-six Circuit champions reached at least the quarterfinal, up from 34 in 2110 and 24 in 2109. Eleven reached a semifinal or better, the same as 2110 and up from nine in 2109.

Los Remolinos was the only Circuit champion to win State, taking 3A girls from the 10 seed. South Rim's boys reached the 2A final from the 8 seed. ## The Tournament of Champions

The new qualifier pairings were 9A runner-up vs 10B runner-up and 8A runner-up vs 11B runner-up.

**Boys.** Pacific Gate beat Chaminade 3-2. Glen Park beat Condotti Vanguard Academy 3-2. Pacific Gate then beat Canyonlands 3-2 before losing 3-2 to Canal View in the quarterfinal. Glen Park beat Kishwaukee 3-2 and lost 4-1 to L.B. Johnson. Mater Dei entered as the 10B champion and beat Vespertine North 4-1, Villard 5-0, L.B. Johnson 4-1 and Canal View 3-2.

**Girls.** Evans Larsen Day beat Pacific Gate 4-1. Romero-Finniski beat Glen Park 3-2. Evans Larsen Day then beat Canal View 3-2, E. Baker 4-1 and Banfield Day 4-1. Romero-Finniski beat Bay Oregon 5-0, Star City 5-0 and Mater Dei 4-1. The two qualifier winners met in the final, and Romero-Finniski won 3-2.

**Qualifier record since it began.** There have now been 36 qualifier winners across nine seasons. Six have won the Tournament of Champions. Eight more reached a semifinal or final without winning it. The other 22 were out within two rounds.

## The 9A boys boost, seven seasons on

The top of 9A still carries the fingerprints of the old intervention. L.B. Johnson repeated as champion, 7-2 over Pacific Gate. Chaparral and Wells were the other semifinalists. Seawall, Natchitoches and Rampart reached the quarterfinals, and Banneker reached the Round of 16.

Pacific Gate, the program deliberately left out of the boost, was the top seed at 31-2 and reached the final. L.B. Johnson was also 31-2. The boost no longer reads like a one-season intervention; it has become part of the class's competitive history.

## The large city programs, year six

The 2106 large-city intervention is no longer producing the extreme records of its first two seasons, but the cohort has stopped falling.

- Boys: 41.6 percent combined winning percentage, 5 of 11 made State.
- Girls: 43.0 percent combined winning percentage, 7 of 11 made State.

That is up from roughly 37 percent in 2109 and essentially flat with 2110. The programs are now more middle-of-the-association than championship cohort. Valderra's boys reached the 8A Round of 16; no large-city program reached a quarterfinal in either gender.

## The expanded private map, year two

The 25 private programs added before 2110 are now through a second season.

Six of the 25 boys programs made State; none reached a quarterfinal. Nine of the 25 girls programs made State, and four reached a quarterfinal: Valderra Catholic (29-7, 7 seed), The Greaves School (25-2, 5 seed), Canyon Creek (24-9, 10 seed) and Blue Mountain Christian (21-10, 20 seed).

The expansion is therefore producing more girls postseason depth than boys depth so far, but no new program from the group has reached a State semifinal or final.

## Public/private status carried into the played season

Evans Larsen Day is unambiguous in the export: `private=1`, 10B championship road. The boys went 29-6 and reached the 10B Round of 16. The girls went 47-2, finished second in 10B, won the new qualifier over Pacific Gate and reached the TOC final.

The other three status cases are internally inconsistent in the 2111 export:

- Basalt is `private=0`, classification 4A, but championship/road group 11B. Its boys went 22-8 and reached the 11B Round of 16; its girls went 23-9 and reached the Round of 24.
- Peregrine is `private=0`, classification 3A, but championship/road group 11B. Its boys went 20-8 and made the Round of 24; its girls finished 15-11 at Super Regionals.
- Washington San Cordero is `private=0`, classification Group 3, but championship/road group 11B. It finished 5-21 boys and 9-17 girls.

Those are canonical 2111 results because they are what the season played. The map fields should be reconciled before 2112 if those public schools are supposed to return to public championship roads.

## The 2111 portal class

The regular portal moved 506 players: 242 boys and 264 girls.

Every move landed at the recorded destination. Every mover played varsity. Boys had a median of 20 varsity flights; girls 22. 83.5 percent of the boys and 89.0 percent of the girls played at least 10 varsity flights, while 55.8 and 65.9 percent respectively played at least 20.

The destination geography remained mostly local: 333 of the 506 moves were county-tier, 112 neighbor-tier and 61 area-tier.

## Pre-high-school development

There were 521 seventh graders and 956 eighth graders, 1,477 in all. 305 seventh graders (58.5 percent) and 614 eighth graders (64.2 percent) appeared in a varsity dual. They won 48.5 and 48.2 percent of their varsity flights.

982 of the 13,621 seniors had played early. Among players with an early-development record, the median early POT boost is 16.2 percent of base ceiling; the bottom tenth is 5.7 percent or less and the top tenth 40.3 percent or more. 28.8 percent received at least 25 percent, 4.4 percent at least 50 percent and 26.6 percent less than 10 percent. About 59 percent have two early seasons.

Within their own program and grade, early participants in grades 10 to 12 sit about 0.62 standard deviations above classmates on current ability.

## Alternate 2111

Each entry changes one named result. Everything before it remains as played.

**Fork: Canal View closes one flight in the boys TOC final.** Mater Dei won 3-2. Flip one flight and Canal View wins its next Tournament of Champions while Mater Dei's perfect season ends at the final hurdle.

**Fork: Evans Larsen Day closes one flight in the girls 10B final.** Mater Dei won 5-4. Flip one flight and Evans Larsen Day wins 10B at 48-1 entering the TOC, while Mater Dei becomes the 10B runner-up and enters the new 9A-vs-10B qualifier instead.

**Fork: Evans Larsen Day closes one flight in the girls TOC final.** Romero-Finniski won 3-2. Flip one flight and Evans Larsen Day completes the runner-up-to-TOC-champion route instead, and Romero-Finniski's fourth girls TOC title waits.

**Fork: Virginia City wins the deciding tiebreakers in the Group 2 boys final.** The dual finished 3-3 and Kishwaukee advanced as champion. Reverse the deciders and Virginia City wins the title from the 7 seed.
