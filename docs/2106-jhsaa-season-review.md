# The 2032 (2106) JHSAA Tennis Season

*Internal annual review*

No program changed class this year. The season belonged to the large city programs that received transfers last winter, to the TOC Qualifier, and to a long list of first time champions.

## Executive findings

**The large city boost turned a list of 22 weak programs into contenders.** All 22 made State. Together they went from winning 20 percent of their matches in 2105 to 87 percent (boys) and 90 percent (girls) in 2106. Four won titles: Alameda (7A boys), Belyakov Vo-Tech (8A boys), Potrero Hill (9A girls) and Narragansett (8A girls). Three more reached a final: Bienville (8A boys), Shasta (8A girls) and Valderra (7A girls).

**Potrero Hill won the 9A girls title, 8-1 over Natchitoches, and reached the 9A boys semifinal.** The girls were 4-22 a year earlier. The boys were 4-19.

**The TOC Qualifier produced another champion.** Jesuit's boys lost the 10B final, won the qualifier, and then won the Tournament of Champions, beating Canal View 3-2 in the final. Across the four seasons since the qualifier began, qualifier winners have won the Tournament of Champions twice. The girls title went to Glen Park (7A), 5-0 over Fletcher-Garrison Hall.

**Eleven first titles went to ten programs:** Alameda, Belyakov Vo-Tech, Dutchfork, Miles Davis, Puerto Alma and Quaking Aspen among the boys; Glen Park, Monongahela, Narragansett, Potrero Hill and Quaking Aspen among the girls. Quaking Aspen won Group 3 in both genders.

**The expansion class cooled.** The 63 programs per gender won 43 percent (boys) and 46 percent (girls) of their matches, down from 62 and 59 percent in 2105. Boys programs with losing records rose from 19 to 38. Purcell Crossing's girls won 2A, the class's only title.

**The 2106 transfer files landed.** 2,213 of the 2,362 players in the class file and 299 of the 308 in the large city file were on 2106 rosters, and all but 6 were at the school named. Nobody went to Trois Ilets.

**Six programs collapsed from winning to losing.** Boys: Leidesdorff Country Day, Mount Ruth and Veritas Academy. Girls: Powder Valley, Prairie and Tippah.

Team results come from records, seeds and the championship brackets. Program history comes from the archive back to 2027. Private schools keep their league class in program history, and their State result is on the 10B or 11B road. No player rating appears anywhere in this review.

## The 9A boys, one year on

Seawall won the title, its third and first since 2099, 6-3 over Rampart. Banneker and Potrero Hill were the other semifinalists. Pacific Gate went 28-3 under Nolan Martinez as the 3 seed and lost in the quarterfinal for the second straight year.

Of last year's 14 boosted programs, three reached the semifinals and two reached the final. Chaparral, the 2105 champion, and Wells, the 2105 finalist, both fell to 21-6. Rampart went from 25-3 to 31-4 and finished as runner-up.

## The Tournament of Champions

**Boys.** Jesuit beat St. Lucia Academy 3-2 in the qualifier, then Porterfield 3-2, Miles Davis 4-1, Seawall 3-2 and Canal View 3-2. Canal View beat Quaking Aspen 5-0, Monongahela 4-1 and Rampart 3-2 on its way to the final. Rampart won the other qualifier, over Bienville 4-1, and beat Coastal Christian 3-2 and Belyakov Vo-Tech 4-1.

**Girls.** Glen Park beat Mt Jacqueline 3-2, Jackson Hole 3-2, Mater Dei 3-2 and Fletcher-Garrison Hall 5-0. Mater Dei beat Gottschalk-Herman 3-2 in the qualifier, then Pine Barrens 5-0 and Monongahela 5-0. Natchitoches, the 9A runner-up, beat Shasta 3-2 in the qualifier, then beat the 9A champion, Potrero Hill, 3-2 in the first round before losing to Fletcher-Garrison Hall 3-2 in the quarterfinal. Fletcher-Garrison Hall went on to the final.

**Qualifier record since it began.** Of 16 qualifier winners across four seasons, two won the Tournament of Champions (Pacific Gate girls in 2104, Jesuit boys in 2106) and four more reached a semifinal or final.

## Pre high school development, second season

There were 471 seventh graders and 871 eighth graders, 1,342 in all, up from 1,171 in 2105. They played at the same rate: 67 and 69 percent appeared in a varsity dual, winning 49 and 48 percent of their flights.

Compared with classmates in the same program and grade, early players in grades 10 to 12 sit about 0.65 standard deviations ahead on ability, about the same as the 0.61 of a year ago. The boost recorded for early players has a median of 15 percent of base ceiling, with a bottom tenth at 5 percent or less and a top tenth at 39 percent or more. About 27 percent got a boost of 25 percent or more and 3.5 percent got 50 percent or more. Players with one early season have a median boost of 13 percent (12 a year ago) and players with two have 18 percent (23 a year ago). The overall median is lower mainly because 55 percent of this year's recorded players have two early seasons, against 87 percent in 2105.

## Firsts and long waits

**First title in the archive:** boys Alameda, Belyakov Vo-Tech, Dutchfork, Miles Davis, Puerto Alma and Quaking Aspen; girls Glen Park, Monongahela, Narragansett, Potrero Hill and Quaking Aspen.

## Program arcs

**Biggest climbs outside the large city programs.** Boys: Assay (Group 3), 5-22 to 18-8; Granite Basin, 11-16 to 25-6 and a semifinal; Sutter Basin, 12-16 to 22-7. Girls: Gagarin (4A), 6-18 to 23-8 and a semifinal; Iisalmi, 9-17 to 24-6; Carden Pass, 9-22 to 20-12.

**Biggest falls.** Boys: Veritas Academy, 24-8 to 3-22; Cardinal Echevarria, 23-10 to 3-29; Mount Ruth, 24-6 to 5-18; Nyssa, 30-1 to 12-16. Girls: Tippah, 21-8 to 4-19; St. Isidore Academy, 15-13 to 1-25; High Timber, 22-5 to 8-16.

Most of the fall comes from graduation. Between six and ten of the eleven players who played the most for each of these boys programs were seniors, and between seven and nine for the girls programs. Nyssa's boys also sent three of their regular eleven to other schools in last winter's files.

## Alternate 2106

Each entry changes one named result. Everything before it is as played.

**Fork: Rampart closes one flight in the 9A boys final.** Seawall won 6-3. Flip three flights and Rampart, 31-4 and boosted a year earlier, wins its first title, and Seawall's third waits another year.

**Fork: Glen Park loses its first TOC dual.** Glen Park beat Mt Jacqueline 3-2 in round one. Flip one flight and Mt Jacqueline, the 1A champion, moves on, and the 7A girls' first title in the event does not happen.

**Fork: Canal View closes one flight in the TOC final.** Jesuit won 3-2. Flip one flight and Canal View wins its second Tournament of Champions, and the qualifier winners' two TOC titles become one.

**Fork: Valderra closes one flight in the 7A girls final.** Glen Park won 5-4. Flip one flight and Valderra, a 5-18 program a year earlier, wins 7A, and the large city programs add a fifth title.
