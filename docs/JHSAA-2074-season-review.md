# JHSAA 2074 Season Review

## Executive summary

2074 was the first season in which the new 48-team / Parastate / at-large architecture actually operated in 7A and Group 1, and the first season in which both groups also joined 8A and 9A in the 4S/5D postseason. The season therefore provides the first real test of both reforms at once.

The major findings:

- **The title field was less chalky than 2073:** 11 of 24 champions were No. 1 seeds and 20 of 24 were top-four seeds, compared with 15/24 and 23/24 in 2073.
- **The finals were exceptionally close:** 16 of 24 State finals were decided by one team point.
- **The 48-team field did what it was supposed to do without taking over the championship:** 17 of 64 at-large teams won in Parastate, but only two advanced beyond the Round of 32. No at-large team reached a semifinal or final.
- **One at-large team made a genuine run:** Mesa Co-op girls in 7A won three State duals from the at-large line and reached the quarterfinals. Boyle Heights girls in Group 1 won twice and reached the Round of 16.
- **No district champion needed an automatic committee berth** in any of the four 7A / Group 1 gender fields.
- **7A and Group 1 really did use 4S/5D throughout State:** all 94 State duals in each group across both genders contained S1–S4 and D1–D5.
- **The format continued to change winners materially:** against the old 1S/4D subset, 7A changed 21.3% of winners across both genders and Group 1 changed 18.1%.
- **Individual State became less top-heavy:** 79 of 144 champions were No. 1 seeds and 129 were top-four seeds, down from 86 and 138 in 2073. Two champions came from seed 10 or worse.
- **No freshman won No. 1 Singles State in 2074**, after three did so in 2073.
- **Daphne Talbert’s four-S1 chase ended:** the two-time Group 3 girls champion reached the semifinal as a junior but did not win a third straight title.
- **The transfer layer recovered dramatically from the 2073 failure:** among 1,235 inferred returning-player school changes, the median move was from roughly No. 16 on the source roster to No. 9 at the destination; 74.7% finished top 11, 77.8% made 10+ varsity appearances, and only 2.5% made zero varsity appearances.
- **The match engine remains stationary:** favorite win rates by OVR gap in 2074 are essentially the same as 2073.

## II. First real year of the 48-team / Parastate system

Across 7A and Group 1, boys and girls, the committee selected **64 at-large teams**: 16 per field.

| Field | Committee locks | At-large Parastate wins | Teams advancing beyond R32 | Deepest at-large run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 7A boys | 12 | 4 | 0 | Four teams reached R32 |
| Group 1 boys | 12 | 5 | 0 | Five teams reached R32 |
| 7A girls | 10 | 2 | 1 | **Mesa Co-op — quarterfinal** |
| Group 1 girls | 11 | 6 | 1 | **Boyle Heights — Round of 16** |

Overall:

- **17 of 64 at-larges (26.6%) won their Parastate dual.**
- **2 of 64 (3.1%) advanced beyond the Round of 32.**
- **0 reached the semifinals.**
- **0 district-champion automatic berths were required.**

The architecture therefore expanded access without converting the committee into a parallel championship route. Most at-larges were competitive enough to justify the game, but the Road-qualified teams still dominated once the field converged.

The most important exception is Mesa Co-op girls. They were not a unanimous lock, entered through the bubble/Borda path, then won three duals and reached the quarterfinal. That is exactly the kind of team the at-large mechanism exists to find.

## III. 4S/5D: 7A and Group 1 joined the large-school model

All State duals in 9A, 8A, 7A, and Group 1 used the full nine-flight postseason lineup: **S1–S4 + D1–D5**.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 9A | 15.4% | 28.2% | **21.8%** |

| 7A | 14.9% | 27.7% | **21.3%** |

For the two new groups:

- **7A:** 20 of 94 State duals (21.3%) change winner if scored using only the old 1S/4D subset.
- **Group 1:** 17 of 94 (18.1%) change winner.

That is a substantial first-year effect. The 7A girls sample is especially strong at 27.7%; one season is not enough to call that the long-run rate, but the reform is clearly not cosmetic.

## IV. Individual State: less chalky than 2073

Across 144 varsity Individual State championships:

- **79 were won by No. 1 seeds — 54.9%.**
- **129 were won by top-four seeds — 89.6%.**
- **2 were won by seeds 10 or worse.**

For comparison, 2073 produced 86 No. 1 champions, 138 top-four champions, and only two seed-10+ champions. The individual tournament therefore became meaningfully less top-heavy in 2074.

The two deep champions were both boys:

- **Morgan Valentini, Bravewoman — 1A No. 3 Singles, No. 16 seed.**
- **Brody Thomas / Hudson Diaz, Vermillion — Group 1 No. 2 Doubles, No. 10 seed.**

### No. 1 Singles

No freshman won S1 State in 2074. Three repeat S1 champions from 2073 defended successfully:

- **Elijah Wolf — 4A boys**
- **Melquiades Solis — Group 2 boys**
- **Kamryn Celestine — 7A girls**
- **Tiana Welling — Group 2 girls**, completing a third consecutive S1 title.

The most important failed repeat was **Daphne Talbert**. After winning Group 3 girls S1 as a freshman and sophomore, she entered 2074 as the active player closest to the unprecedented four-title S1 record. She reached the semifinal as the No. 2 seed and lost, ending the four-title path with one season still remaining.

## V. Transfers: the opportunity model recovered

Comparing returning player IDs between the 2073 and 2074 exports identifies **1,235 school changes** among players who had eligibility remaining.

| Metric | Boys | Girls | Combined / read |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Movers | 614 | 621 | **1235** |
| Median projected source rank | 16 | 16 | **16** |
| Median destination rank | 9 | 9 | **9** |
| Finished top 11 | 73.9% | 75.5% | **74.7%** |
| 10+ varsity appearances | 76.7% | 78.9% | **77.8%** |
| Zero varsity appearances | 2.0% | 3.1% | **2.5%** |
| Median varsity appearances | 21 | 22 | **21–22** |

This is the opposite of the 2073 concentrated transfer sweep, where only 20.8% finished top 11, 23.4% made 10+ varsity appearances, 28.8% made zero varsity appearances, and the median was one varsity appearance.

The distribution pattern also looks much healthier: the inferred movers landed at 378 boys programs and 391 girls programs; the median recipient received two movers, and 98–99% of recipient programs received no more than two. The system is again dispersing surplus players instead of creating new reserve superclasses.

Juniors remain the hardest population: only about two-thirds finished top 11 at the destination. That is still far better than 2073, but they remain the group where the opportunity test should stay strictest.

## VIII. What 2074 says about the reforms

### The 48-team field passed its first basic test

The committee found enough teams to make Parastate meaningful, but Road qualifiers still controlled the championship. A 26.6% at-large win rate is high enough that the games are not ceremonial; a 3.1% rate of advancing beyond the Round of 32 is low enough that the expansion did not overwhelm the earned pathway.

### The committee produced one validating outlier

Mesa Co-op girls is the cleanest first-year argument for the committee. A bubble/Borda selection reaching the 7A quarterfinal demonstrates exactly why a second selection mechanism can add value without making the road irrelevant.

### 4S/5D matters immediately in both new groups

The 7A and Group 1 winner-change rates are too large to describe the expansion as mere participation policy. The extra singles and doubles flights materially alter who survives State.

### The transfer system is back to its intended purpose

2073 created reserve depth. 2074 created varsity seats. The outcome difference is large enough that the source-filter + opportunity-first destination logic should be treated as a core institutional guardrail.

---

## Bottom line

2074 is one of the more consequential JHSAA seasons because several institutional experiments became real simultaneously. The results are unusually coherent:

- the expanded field found some legitimate teams without producing an at-large title run;
- Parastate functioned as a real competitive boundary between selection and the conventional State field;
- 4S/5D changed enough winners in 7A and Group 1 to justify the format as substantive;
- the transfer layer returned to broad varsity opportunity rather than reserve accumulation;
- individual championships became less deterministic while the underlying match engine remained stable;
- and the season produced substantially more deep-seed team champions than 2073 without becoming chaotic.

The first-year evidence supports continuing the 48-team / committee / Parastate model and the 4S/5D expansion in 7A and Group 1 without structural intervention for 2075.
