# Prep sports columnists, 1990-1998 — findings

35 columns, all 1990s: Morning Call (Allentown PA) 13, News & Record (Greensboro NC,
including its High Point zoned edition) 15, Chicago Tribune 6 (metro contrast),
Spokesman-Review 1. Openings, closings and excerpts in `articles.jsonl` were checked
verbatim against the fetched text; the `devices` examples were hand-copied.

Types (most columns mix two): opinion 10, notes 6, tribute 5, picks 3, event 3,
season-wrap 2, season-preview 2, mailbag 2, humour 2. The single-topic opinion column
is mostly a metro form; small-paper opinion sits on top of a notes column.

Tennis: two. One argues three girls' teams were right to forfeit new dual-team
playoffs over travel and missed school; one gives district singles two items inside a
field-hockey column and asks coaches to phone in records.

## Personas

- **Keith Groller (12 columns), Allentown.** A chatty host with a Monday column of
  1,500-2,800 words. Says "I", mocks his own picks and waistline, credits colleagues,
  prints readers' letters and answers them. Annual rituals: the "Keggers" awards and
  the "All-Groller team".
- **"Uncle Swami" (8), High Point.** A picks persona by an outside contributor.
  Third person, boastful, feuds with the paper over pay and with one town. Each pick
  is a predicted score plus a one-line gag; most columns open on last week's record.
- **Brian Tomlin (5), Greensboro.** A sober analyst with no "I". Leads with a verdict
  on a rule or format, concedes the other side, then drops to short notes.
- **Barry Temkin (6), Chicago.** An essayist: one subject, built on a metaphor or a
  paradox, ending on an aphorism or someone else's quote.

## Structure

- The small-paper column is a container: a lead item of 200-400 words, then notes
  under bold capped lead-ins ("COURT REPORT —", "KUDOS —", "MAILBAG —", "GRASS
  ROOTS."), separated by asterisks or ellipses.
- It ends on a coach's quote or a contact line.
- It doubles as a noticeboard: poll notes, stat leaders, schools seeking opponents,
  a former player's baby.

## How opinion is licensed and hedged

- Admitting it is uninvited: "Nobody asked. But I've never been one to let something
  so trivial stop me".
- Borrowing authority: coaches make the picks; "I'm told by a reliable source".
- Conceding first: "isn't much of a thank you ... but there's a rational reason
  behind it".
- Casting it as a vote or a wish: "Here's another vote against."; "Let's hope".
- Self-mockery before and after the point.

The targets are adults and institutions: state associations, playoff formats, coaches
as a group, officials, TV, the paper itself.

## Teenagers

- Praise is by name and about character ("classy kids").
- Criticism is collective and unnamed: "pouters and gripers", a visiting student
  section as "WORST FANS", showboating addressed to "guys".
- Tears are described as a group. An injury or a death is told as resilience, with a
  parent quoted.
- Transfers are discussed as what parents and schools do.
- The picks persona aims gags at teams, fans, TV and the paper.
- One metro columnist states the convention outright: some papers never name the
  player who erred. He defends naming a kicker and still does not name him.

## Devices worth imitating

- A standing opener ("Random thoughts, opinions and things to ponder as I thaw
  out...").
- A named annual award and a named team, each with a repeated disclaimer.
- A running record for picks, and "home teams are in capital letters".
- Team nicknames used as names (Konkrete Kids, Catty, Zephyrland).
- "Kudos" for scorekeepers and referees.
- A reader's letter printed whole, then answered.
- Direct thanks to seniors.
- A programme note ("This column concludes next week and then we make the transition
  to softball").
- The crystal ball.

## Not obtained

- Nothing from the 1980s, and no true small-town weekly sports editor's column.
  Allentown and High Point are small cities; the scanned weeklies were too rough to
  quote.
- No first-person columns from the Lewiston or Spokane pages, only notebooks.
- No column on a coach being fired, a referee, or a named transfer; no retirement or
  coach's-death tribute; no stand-alone staff mailbag.
- Standing column titles and bylines are mostly missing from the archive text.
