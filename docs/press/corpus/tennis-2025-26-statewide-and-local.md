# Tennis coverage, 2025-26 — WyoPreps, Houston Chronicle, Hood County News, a wire story

Four items pasted by the owner (2026-11) plus eight WyoPreps tennis pages fetched
directly, covering one whole Wyoming season from preview to all-state. People are
replaced with NAME below; excerpts are short and verbatim.

‼️ 2025-26 writing. Take the SEASON RHYTHM, the agate and the news patterns. The
period voice comes from the 1980s-90s files.

## 1. A statewide desk's season, week by week (WyoPreps, Wyoming)

One writer covers the whole state's tennis. A seven-week season produced this
sequence, and the same sequence the year after:

1. **Season preview** (mid-August). "The hard courts are calling." Dates and sites
   for regionals and state up top; last year's team champions; how many individual
   champions return; then "Girls Tennis Outlook" and a boys outlook, school by
   school, in order of expected finish.
2. **Weekly scoreboard**, one per week ("Scoreboard: Sept. 2-6", "Results Week 4").
   A three-sentence top on what the week means ("Big conference matches for seeding
   at regionals ..."), who travels and who is home, then every dual by day.
3. **Regionals** (week six). Sites, who hosts, how seeding works ("based on
   conference records"), what is at stake ("The top four singles players and doubles
   teams from each bracket get a seed at the state tournament"), then results.
4. **State preview.** Start times, the four sites, defending champions, the regional
   champions, then favourites flight by flight. "Neither has lost a match in their
   prep career, and they will be favored this weekend."
5. **State scoreboard** and photo galleries, day by day.
6. **Champions react**, one piece per flight champion, built from a video interview.
7. **All-state**, with the rule stated: no vote, places at state decide it.

That is a ready edition calendar: preview, N weekly scoreboards, a qualifying
round, a championship preview, results, reaction, honours.

## 2. The weekly scoreboard's agate

    Tuesday, Sept. 2:
    Girls = Jackson 5 Campbell County 0
    1S: NAME (JK) def. NAME: 6-0, 6-0
    2S: NAME (JK) def. NAME: 6-1, 6-1
    1D: SURNAME/SURNAME (JK) def. SURNAME/SURNAME: 6-3, 6-3
    2D: SURNAME/SURNAME (JK) def. SURNAME/SURNAME: 6-2, 1-6, 6-3
    3D: SURNAME/SURNAME (JK) def. SURNAME/SURNAME: 7-5, 6-4

- Flights by code (1S, 2S, 1D-3D). Wyoming plays two singles and three doubles.
- Full names in singles, surnames joined by a slash in doubles.
- School code in parentheses on the winner only.
- A score nobody reported is printed as "??" and the result still runs.
- The day's schedule is posted first and results are filled in as they arrive; the
  story carries "Updated:" and "All schedules are subject to change."

## 3. The champions story (~250 words plus video)

- Lead names both pairs, the event, the weekend and the town.
- One sentence on how each final went: "The girls split the first two sets but took
  control in the third. The guys lost the first set and rallied to win the crown."
- The interview reported in indirect speech, not quoted: they "said controlling what
  they could control was the biggest key", "Chemistry was another key".
- A subhead, "How the Champions Got to the Top", then each pair's bracket path round
  by round with every score: opened with, "ran into their cross-town rivals", "In
  the semis", "faced ... in the finals".
- Links to every earlier piece of the season at the bottom.

## 4. All-state as a rule, not a vote

"All-State awards do not include a voting process. They are determined based on a
player's finish at the tennis state tournament." The cut is stated by flight: top
six at No. 1 singles, top three at No. 2 singles and No. 1 doubles, top two at No. 2
and No. 3 doubles. The list is name, school, and prior years in parentheses:
"( All-State in 2024 & 2025 )". The headline is the repeat honourees: "Two Four-Time
Standouts Highlight ...".

## 5. A state team final (Houston Chronicle, ~350 words)

Texas fall team tennis is co-ed: boys and girls singles and doubles in one dual,
first to ten.

- Lead: the title, the opponent, the score, the day, the site.
- Second paragraph: which title this is for the program, with the earlier years in
  parentheses, and its first trip back since a named year.
- One paragraph that is the whole playoff run as a list of scores: "opened with a
  19-0 victory over ..., followed by an 11-0 win against ... and a 10-0 sweep of
  ...", then the closer rounds, the semifinal "10-9", and the fact that ties it
  together: 18 straight wins after losing the opener to the team it beat in the
  semifinal.
- How the final was won, by discipline: swept the girls doubles, six singles wins.
- Then pure results in sentences: "NAME and NAME beat NAME and NAME 6-3, 6-4".
  Comeback wins give the match tiebreak: "4-6, 6-2, 10-3".
- Last line: the tournament's MVPs.
- No quotes anywhere. The box score is a link.

## 6. A local feature on a first (Hood County News, Texas, ~650 words)

Headline: "History made: Granbury tennis becomes first GHS program to reach 6A
postseason". Byline "By NAME | Hood County News Contributor".

- The hook is a program first, and the coach undercuts it at once: "We may be the
  first, but a big part of that is simply because our season ends before most other
  sports". The writer answers him: "But someone has to be the first".
- Record line: "finished 9-7 overall and 5-2 in District 3-6A".
- Four capitalised subheads: MAKEUP OF TEAM · UNIQUE SITUATION · SETTING UP FOR THE
  SPRING.
- MAKEUP OF TEAM is the whole roster by class, boys then girls.
- UNIQUE SITUATION is the program's handicap (no middle-school program) and what
  that says about the players.
- The last section looks ahead to the spring individual season and who returns.
- Nearly all of it is the coach talking, in long quotes. He credits his assistant
  by name.
- Mascot doubled for a co-ed team: "the Pirates/Lady Pirates".

## 7. A coach fired, with the other side (wire story, ~250 words)

The second dismissal story in the corpus, and unlike the first the coach talks:

- Lead: fired "after a player overheated and passed out during a September
  tournament".
- The coach's own account of the incident, quoted.
- The fact that complicates it, stated flat: the player's mother is on the school
  board.
- The vote: four members, one abstaining.
- A parent of another player, by name, objecting: "it seems extremely unfair to
  assign nearly all of the blame".
- What he keeps: his teaching post and the assistant athletic director's job.

So a dismissal can be written two ways: from the institution's statement alone, or
with the coach and parents on the record and the vote count given.

## 8. What this means for the generator

- The statewide desk's skeleton is a calendar of recurring pieces, and a short
  season fits in about ten of them.
- A weekly scoreboard with a three-sentence top is the cheapest running coverage
  there is, and it is nearly all agate.
- A champion's bracket path, round by round with scores, is a story by itself. The
  archive has every one.
- "First in program history" and "N-time all-state" are facts the archive can
  supply for every program and player.
- An honours list can state its own rule. The sim's awards have rules; print them.
- Unreported scores exist in real coverage. A paper can leave a hole.
