**Conventions.**
- Headlines: all-caps archive form, active verb + score, sport as trailing slug ("NORTHERN LEHIGH WHIPS DISTRICT 3 CHAMPION 6-0 SOFTBALL"). Verbs: whips, cops, trips, blanks, stuns, bows to. Town Topics: caps head + run-in deck ("PHS NETMEN STUMBLE In NJSIAA Tournament.").
- Ledes: staff gamers usually delay — a conceit, pun, pop reference (Go-Go's, Bon Jovi, Bengals) or second-person address — then a long nut sentence with winner, score, opponent, site, "yesterday"/"last night". Roundups and the Daily Press go hard-news: name, verb, score, day.
- Order: lede, nut with score, what it means/next opponent, winner quote, chronological account, loser quote, look-ahead. Notes tails are labelled ("Net Notes —").
- Quotes: 6-15 in a staff gamer, none in roundups. "Said" dominates, but 1980s copy rotates offered, remarked, noted, admitted, observed, volunteered, lamented. Coaches speak most.
- Scores/records: "a 6-7, 6-2, 6-1 victory"; tiebreaks "7-6 (7-2)"; records in parentheses "(21-3)" or "Tabb (2-2 overall, 2-1 district)"; roundup score lines "Tabb 11, Franklin 4".
- Vocabulary: netters/netmen, hurlers, nine, charges, mentor, squad, club, tourney, crown, titlist, champ, duo/tandem/pair, campaign, foes; downed, topped, stopped, trounced, edged, ousted, blanked, whitewash, breezed past, "15th victim".

**Tennis specifically.**
- A dual brief is formulaic: record + shutout verb + team score, then "In singles play" position by position with set scores, "In doubles play", opponent's record.
- NJ says "first singles / second doubles"; PA, VA, VT say "No. 1 singles".
- Longer duals are told through the deciding court.
- Tournament finals are set-by-set (breaks, match points, seeds), then who advances to states.
- Previews and wraps run team by team: last year's record, graduation losses, ladder order, individual W-L by position ("13-0 at No. 2").
- All-area teams are capsules: "NAME, School. The senior ... went 10-1."
