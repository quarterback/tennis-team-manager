## 1. What a statewide desk publishes at season's end

The girls tennis index for one day (Nov. 11) lists, each as its own story:

- Player of the Year · Team of the Year · Coach of the Year
- All-State First Team · Second Team · Third Team
- **All-Flight Teams** (the best at each flight, statewide)
- All-Group 1 · All-Group 2 · All-Group 3 · All-Group 4 · All-Non-Public teams
- one "Player of the Year and Postseason Honors" story PER CONFERENCE (six visible:
  BCSL, Big North, Cape-Atlantic, Colonial, Colonial Valley, Greater Middlesex)
- a searchable list of college commitments
- an umbrella story: "All-State and full postseason honors"

and in the days before it:

- "Final Girls Tennis Top 20: State tournament rearranges rankings"
- "Final N.J. girls tennis group & conference rankings"
- the state singles and state doubles finals as separate stories
- the tournament brackets as a standing page

Standing section tabs: Home · Scores · Standings · Rankings · Power Points · Watch ·
Brackets.

So the statewide desk's product is a LADDER OF HONOURS that reaches every level the
association has: state, flight, enrollment group, conference. One day, about twenty
stories, most of them lists with a short top.

## 2. The honours story (Player of the Year, ~450 words)

Order:
1. Three short paragraphs of set-up about how hard it is to stand out as an
   upperclassman. No name yet.
2. The record line: "went 23-0 in her senior season, winning all but one set".
3. A one-sentence paragraph that is the award itself: "For that, NAME is the 2026
   NJ.com Girls Tennis Player of the Year."
4. Coach quote, long, on character: "the kind of player every coach dreams of
   having".
5. The résumé: notable wins by opponent and school, the state singles run round by
   round (quarterfinal, semifinal, final, each opponent named with school), the
   final's score, the site.
6. The player's own quotes, before and after the final.
7. What it was a first of: "the first title of her career and the first for the ...
   program"; the county title "for the first time".
8. Recruiting facts: college commitment, star rating, UTR and where it ranks.
9. A closing line about the program's history.

Things 1990s copy did not have: the star rating and UTR, the commitment in the
second reference, the award sentence set alone as a paragraph.

## 3. The team honours story (Team of the Year, ~500 words)

- Opens on the streak, in numbers: "has won 76 straight matches dating back to the
  beginning of the 2022 season. Since 2020, ... has gone 102-1."
- The hook is one fact stated flat: every one of 16 wins was 5-0.
- The award sentence alone as a paragraph.
- Coach quote, then the tournament record: both doubles flights' runs in the state
  doubles tournament, the one set lost all season and who took it, the match
  tiebreak that closed it "10-7".
- Family: a freshman is "the younger sister of" the team's top player.
- A scheduling anecdote: the top player flew to a national junior tournament between
  the section semifinal and final.
- One metaphor, late: "In this Big Blue machine, every player is vital".
- Closes on two coach quotes about culture.

## 4. Rankings

- A final Top 20 is rank, team, record. Three columns, nothing else.
- The headline carries the story: "State tournament rearranges rankings".
- Rankings also exist by group and by conference, published separately.
- 1990's final boys Top 20 ran from a 22-1 No. 1 to an 18-1 No. 20; records in the
  list range from 14-2 to 29-6. A 29-6 team at No. 17 shows how many matches a New
  Jersey season held.
- School names in a list are disambiguated the newspaper way: "St. Joseph (Met.)",
  "Iselin Kennedy".

## 5. Feature and game-story openings (2026)

All three paywalled openings are delayed, scene-first leads with no score:

- A championship: the drought first. "A championship drought of 12 years feels like
  a century from ...'s perspective."
- A profile: a famous college coach in the stands on his bye week, "700 miles from
  South Bend", then why he came.
- A semifinal: the player's 4:15 a.m. workouts and the stadium "stared at him
  through a glass window", then a one-line turn: "How's that for motivation?"

Headlines are long, written for search, and withhold the name: "N.J. boys soccer
power ends 12-year drought, wins record 26th state title", "N.J. tennis tandem stuns
2x champs, wins program's 1st-ever doubles tournament title".

## 6. The archive as a product

The "HS historical project" republishes old Top 20s and all-state teams year by
year, 1990 onward, with one hub page per sport linking each season's "Top honors &
All-State" and "Final Top 20". Its stated purpose is to let readers "settle
Thanksgiving Day 'remember when' arguments". A statewide desk treats its own past
honours as something readers come back for.

## 7. What this means for the generator

- The statewide desk needs an honours ladder that mirrors the association: for the
  JHSAA that is State, flight, classification, region, district, the sim's existing
  award slates. Most of it is lists with a short top, published in one batch.
- Rankings are a weekly three-column list plus a headline that says what moved.
- "First in program history" is the statewide desk's favourite fact. The archive
  already knows it for every program.
- Streaks in numbers (76 straight, 102-1 since 2020) lead team stories.
- A season hub page per year, linking that year's honours and final rankings, is
  the long-run reader surface. The sim already archives every season.
