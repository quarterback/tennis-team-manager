# The Courier-News (central New Jersey), 1991-2001 — owner-supplied clippings

Fifteen phone screenshots of scanned pages supplied by the owner (2026-11). One page
is dated May 19, 1991; one is from 2000; the rest carry no visible date and sit
between roughly 1996 and 2001 on internal evidence. This is the New Jersey material
the collectors could not reach. Player names are replaced with NAME in the agate
patterns below; prose excerpts are short and verbatim.

This paper is the AREA desk done well: one staff covering three or four counties,
with the same kids and coaches turning up season after season.

## 1. Vocabulary that differs from the rest of the corpus

- Flights are ORDINAL WORDS, never "No. 1": **first singles, second singles, third
  singles, first doubles, second doubles**. A New Jersey dual is five flights.
- "d." for defeated in agate; "def." appears in the same column for some duals.
- A tournament is won on **team scores**, flights are **titles**, players are
  **representatives**: "winning all five individual spots to run away with the team
  title".
- State play is named by section and group: "North Jersey Section 2, Group 4 first
  round", "Central Jersey Group 2 first round", "North Jersey Parochial A".
- Mascot on second reference, school on first: "the Blue Devil representatives",
  "The Raiders have 10 points", "The Chiefs' ...", "the Cardinals".

## 2. The tournament story (unsigned, with agate under it)

Headline: "Westfield takes all 5 flights in Union tennis tournament"
Byline: "By The Courier-News Staff". Dateline: "PLAINFIELD —".

Lede: "The Westfield boys tennis team turned the Union County Tournament into its
own invitational Friday, winning all five individual spots to run away with the team
title."

Order of the story:
1. lede: winner, the size of it, one image ("its own invitational")
2. the team score against the next two finishers, in parentheses
3. one sentence naming every champion with the flight in parentheses
4. a turn to a SECOND tournament the paper also covers: "At the Greater Middlesex
   Conference Tournament, North Brunswick took control."
5. that tournament's team race in points
6. the local schools' individual results, including the losses ("reached the
   quarterfinals before falling in straight sets")
7. last line is the schedule: "The tournament resumes with the semifinals today, and
   the finals slated for Monday."

No quotes at all. About 200 words. The agate does the rest.

## 3. Agate

Tournament box, in this order:

    Union County Boys Tennis Tournament
    (at Hub Stine Courts, Plainfield)
    Team scores: School 100, School 34, School 32, ... School and School 20, ...
    Finals
    First singles: NAME (School) d. NAME (School) 6-4, 6-1.
    Third place matches
    First singles: NAME (School) won by default over NAME (School).
    Semifinals
    First singles: SURNAME d. SURNAME 6-0, 6-1; SURNAME d. SURNAME 6-0, 6-0.

- Tied teams share one entry: "New Providence and Summit 20".
- Full names and schools in the finals; surnames only in earlier rounds.
- Tiebreaks in parentheses: "7-6 (9-7)", "6-4, 7-6 (8-10), 6-1".
- A later season numbers the team scores and abbreviates schools, and prints seeds:
  "1. School (OK) 92, 2. School (S) 84" and "(1) NAME (OK) d. (2) NAME (SPF), 6-1, 6-1."

Dual match, under a "REGULAR SEASON" or "STATE TOURNAMENT" rule:

    BRIDGEWATER-RARITAN (12-0) 4, WATCHUNG HILLS 1
    Singles: NAME (BR) d. NAME 6-2, 6-3; NAME (BR) d. NAME 6-4, 6-2; NAME (W) d. NAME 6-4, 6-2.
    Doubles: NAME-NAME (BR) d. NAME-NAME 6-2, 6-4; NAME-NAME (BR) d. NAME-NAME 6-4, 0-6, 6-2.

- Winner in capitals first, records in parentheses, then the team score.
- By about 2000 a "RECORD:" or "RECORDS:" line closes each dual: "RECORD: Ridge 4-2."
- A dual nobody phoned in still gets its line: "No scores available".
- A page-wide scoreboard rail ("SCORES & SCHEDULES") lists every sport by day:
  "FRIDAY'S SCORES", then "TODAY'S SCHEDULE" with times.

## 4. The weekly sport page ("Varsity")

One page per sport per week, built from fixed furniture:

- **Stars of the Week** box. Name in capitals, then one sentence of evidence:
  "Franklin's first singles player reached the second round of the NJSIAA singles
  tournament and won two regular-season matches."
- **Top 10** box, team and record only.
- A lead story on one team or issue.
- A notebook run under bold one-line subheads: "Plainfield plays big", "Team match
  of the year", "notes".

Notebook prose is records first, then one coach quote, then the coach's view in
indirect speech: "Plainfield went 15-2 on the way to winning the American Division of
the Watchung Conference for the first time." ... "We're now a force in tennis," Perle
said.

## 5. The final Top 10 with capsules

Each entry is rank, team, record, then one or two sentences on how the season ended
and one judgement:

- "9. Plainfield (17-3): The Cardinals rolled to their best record in 20 years and
  reached the North Jersey, Section 2, Group 4 semifinals."
- "7. Ridge (14-4): The Red Devils quietly hustled to a 4-1 finish to come away with
  one of the better records in the area."
- "Could go even further next season since most of its top players return."

The capsule is where the area desk allows itself an opinion without a column.

## 6. The season preview

Organised by COUNTY under bold subheads ("Union County", "Hunterdon County",
"Middlesex County"), one or two paragraphs a team:

- last year's record and how far the team went
- who graduated and who returns ("Four starters are back from last year's 12-9 squad")
- who plays where ("who played second singles in 2000, is slated for first singles")
- a hedge word per team: "may be the strongest team in the county", "might be a
  potential sleeper", "it looks like a rebuilding year", "a team to keep an eye on",
  "a nice 1-2 punch"
- family continuity: "the younger sister of former Highlanders star ..."
- facilities as news: the team returns to its home courts after new ones were built

## 7. The all-area package (end of season)

- Lists: ALL-AREA, then one list per county grouping (ALL-SOMERSET, ALL-HUNTERDON,
  ALL-UNION-MIDDLESEX), each line name / school / class (Sr., Jr., So., Fr.).
- HONORABLE MENTION set as one run-in paragraph, school in bold, players after.
- A photo card per all-area player: "1997 record: 17-3", then three fixed fields:
  **Season highlights**, **Career highlights**, **Favorite athlete**.
- A signed **Coach of the Year** story and a signed **Player of the Year** story.

Coach of the Year lede: "Don Van Blake may be 75, but it's his involvement with the
tennis program at Plainfield High School, as well as in the city of Plainfield itself,
that keeps him young." It then gives the record against the last comparable season,
the division title, the quote, the coach's biography (started playing at 55, took the
job because nobody else applied), the feeder programs he built, and a closing quote
from home.

## 8. Features

Headlines pun on "net": "The net result is very good tennis" (1991), "City tennis
program's net worth: priceless". Each has a deck: "It wasn't always that way — but
with one man's help, tennis has undergone a renaissance in Plainfield."

Lede with a turn: "PLAINFIELD — At 82, Donald Van Blake has no plans to give up his
racket. / Tennis racket, that is."

Features are about the program behind the team: a youth tennis association, a
recreation department, who taught whom, where the courts are, who paid. Quotes are
long and plain. One coach will keep coaching "as long as the Lord is willing and the
creeks don't rise."

Photo cutlines report a result: "... lost his second singles match with ...'s ...,
6-4, 6-2." Credit line: "Courier-News photo by ...".

## 9. What this means for the generator

- Half of the experience is agate. A box score with every name is content, not
  decoration, and it needs its own fixed formats per event type.
- The same people recur across years: a county semifinalist one spring is all-area
  the next and a "returning first singles" in the following preview. Storyline
  memory has to span seasons, and family ties are a standard preview line.
- A weekly page per sport with fixed furniture (stars, Top 10, lead, notebook) is a
  ready edition template for the area desk.
- Opinion lives in capsules and hedge words long before it reaches a columnist.
- Tournament stories carry no quotes; weekly notebooks and features do. Quotes can
  be rationed by story type instead of being required everywhere.
- Coaches are characters with biographies, tenures and feeder programs. The sim's
  named staffs can carry that.
