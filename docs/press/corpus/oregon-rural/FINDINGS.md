# FINDINGS — eastern Oregon one-town weekly (Heppner Gazette-Times)

**Source.** Heppner Gazette-Times (Morrow County; covers Heppner Mustangs/Fillies, Ione Cardinals, sometimes Riverside), OCR page text from Historic Oregon Newspapers, read issue by issue (site search was not used). Online and readable: every year probed 1985-1999; 1980 and 1983 returned nothing. Three contrast records come from The Sandy Post, a staffed weekly near Portland. Not reachable: The Independent (Vernonia).

**Counts (58 records).** Tennis 24 (plus 2 exchange-student features that are half tennis), football 9, basketball 6, baseball 4, track 4, volleyball 3, golf 2, softball 1, other 3. By year: 1985 3, 1986 1, 1987 7, 1988 3, 1989 6, 1990 6, 1991 11, 1992 6, 1993 2, 1994 6, 1995 6, 1996 1. Tennis exists only for Ione, 1988-1995; Heppner has no team.

**Who writes.** Nobody on staff. High-school students ("By NAME", no title) cover their own school, sometimes their own team: a tennis player reports her doubles loss in third person; a volleyball player scores six straight points in her own story. From 1990 one adult Ione correspondent writes everything for that town, from pro-set results to a classmate's funeral. Coaches' write-ups run unsigned and drift into first person. Unsigned office copy is the frankest.

**Leads.** One sentence: team + what happened + opponent + day and date (+ site). Three stock shapes: travel ("traveled to Condon on Saturday, May 6 to take on the Blue Devils"), host ("hosted the Stanfield Tigers ... racking up three wins"), and week-in-review ("had one last busy week before winding their season down"). Occasion can outrank result: senior night, "a great night ... as a big crowd saw".

**Scores and names.** Own team's number first even in defeat ("upending the Lady Cardinals, 37-41"; "was defeated ... 2-7"). Records follow as "league mark now stands at 2-1 and their season record moved to 8-4". Town + mascot on first reference. Full names for both sides; opponents at tournaments become "NAME of Umatilla" or surnames only. Earliest items use first names alone.

**Tennis.**
- Flights: "number one singles", "number-two doubles team", also "first singles", "second singles", and loosely "number-one seed".
- A dual is a "match" or "meet"; there is no team score, only "winning four matches and losing two", "went 3-3", "were shut out".
- Boys and girls are one squad, tallied separately. Lower flights play an 8-game set: "8-3", "9-8", once labelled "(pro-set)". Tiebreaks print as an extra number ("7-6, 7-2").
- Verbs: defeated, downed, bested, fell to, dropped her match, easily dispatched, chalked up, pulled out, wore down, prevailed, eliminated, ousted, "lost a squeaker".
- Each result gets a small cause: "played off their opponents mistakes", "good teamwork and court position".
- Format is explained to lay readers: a "playback" for second place; "The top three teams advance."; state has no size classes.
- Slips stay in: "games" for sets or matches, "aced" for a 6-0, 6-0.

**Losses.** Short, flat, no excuses: "suffered a setback at the hands of", then what was won. Softeners: "played well but lost", "too strong and too quick", "showed what experience can do". Coach "was unavailable for comment."

**Weather, travel, crowd.** Routine facts: "very cold and windy weather", "a stiff and chilly wind", "As temperatures soared into the 90's", rainouts "called because of rain". Thin squads are explained (a convention; an opponent with one boy). The coach thanks the travelling crowd in print.

**Quotes.** Zero or one, coach only, never an opponent. Said, commented, remarked, noted, added; much indirect ("sees continued improvement", "credited").

**Headlines.** Sentence case, present tense, mascot nickname, plain verb: rack up, suffer setback, wind up, end season, grab, dashed. Often no result. Surname alone suffices.

**Furniture.** Players of the Week (coach-picked, one per sport, tire-dealer sponsored); "Other placers were:" semicolon lists; line scores; "not available at press time"; next match with time; JV paragraph every time; bowling and ladies' golf beside it.

**Opinion.** Almost none: a townspeople's pro-football pick panel; asides in game stories ("I'm sure we'll hear about it"; officials who "hit the showers in record time"). No letters about school sports found.

**Versus daily copy.** No angle, no delayed lede, no player quotes, no standings; completeness beats selection, the week beats the game, and school life (exchange students, honor roll, flowers) sits inside the sports story. The Sandy Post records show the trained alternative.

**Gaps.** No wrestling (the schools have none; "wrestling" here is steer wrestling), no cross country, no season preview beyond one paragraph, no banquet story, no state-tournament tennis beyond a first-round loss. 1985-86 OCR is poor; several headlines and bylines are marked not legible.
