# Oregon, 2026 — owner-supplied pages (The Oregonian/OregonLive and Philomath News)

Pasted by the owner in 2026-11. Five pieces: three from the statewide paper (a coach's
dismissal, a playoff game story, an all-league list) and two from a one-town
independent news site (its sports index and a full state-meet story). People are
replaced with NAME / COACH below; excerpts are short and verbatim.

Written 2026.

## 1. The local desk: one town, one school (Philomath News)

Three weeks of the sports index, every item about the same high school:

- a fundraiser preview (the boys basketball pie auction: date, time, what the money
  covers, the gimmick)
- all-league stories, ONE PER SPORT, each led by the local picks: "Howard, Peters
  lead Philomath's all-league football picks"; a volleyball player "earns first-team
  all-conference honors for 4th straight year"; "places eight on all-Oregon West
  girls soccer team"; "Five Warriors earn all-conference honors"
- a state-meet preview, the state-meet story, and a separate photo gallery of it
- a playoff preview (opponent, time, seed, how each side got there) and the playoff
  loss
- a coaching change: a wrestling coach "resigned this fall as head coach after 20
  seasons", his record in titles, and the assistant who succeeds him
- a district-title story
- the season-ending football loss, framed on the seniors: "season ends with loss as
  team's 11 seniors say goodbye"

Headline pattern: SURNAME(S) + verb + school + result. The team is "Philomath" in
headlines and "the Warriors" in copy.

What this shows: the local desk covers every sport, every level of result, and the
things around the games (fundraisers, galleries, coaching changes). A loss gets a
story. An all-league list becomes a local story by leading with the local names.

## 2. The staffed state-meet story (local, ~1,100 words, signed)

Byline "By NAME, Philomath News". Dateline "EUGENE —".

Lead: two runners set against each other, no result. "One of Philomath High's top
runners ran with nothing to lose. The other had a title to defend and a lot on her
mind."

Order:
1. the contrast lead, then each runner in her own words
2. the nut paragraph, late: places, site, team score against the winner and the two
   teams behind
3. the coach on the winner, blunt: "They're better than us."
4. program context in counts: the seventh top-three trophy in eight years, split
   into titles, seconds and thirds
5. the race in order: who went out fast, who closed, times to the tenth
6. each athlete's plan, quoted, then the coach's read of how it went
7. the scoring five by place; the two non-scorers "also ran"
8. one comparison number that explains the result (the spread between the first and
   fifth runner, against the winner's)
9. next year: no seniors in the top seven, who the rivals lose
10. a joke from the coach to close the section
11. a subhead, then the OTHER team's race in the same order, shorter
12. a senior's last word
13. agate: team scoring for both races, then "PHS RESULTS" with place, name, time

Quote habits: many quotes, athletes as much as the coach, "said" throughout. The
coach is candid and funny; athletes describe what went wrong as well as right.
Photo cutlines name everyone in a team photo "from left, kneeling ...; standing ...".

## 3. A coach is let go (statewide, ~350 words)

‼️ The corpus had no example of this. The pattern, with the person removed:

1. Lead states the action and its formal cause in one sentence: let go "following an
   internal investigation by the school district".
2. What he had achieved: the titles, the sports, the months.
3. The district's spokesperson, by name and title, confirming the decision and the
   date it was made, by email.
4. The district's statement, quoted: student safety, an investigation "after being
   made aware of accusations", the decision not to offer a contract.
5. What the district would NOT say: it "did not elaborate on the nature of the
   allegations".
6. Who replaces him and that person's statement about preparing the team.
7. "... did not respond to requests for comment."
8. Background: his record ("went 92-24 in his four seasons"), his titles, one line
   of family history.
9. Context: a second program in the same class also has an interim coach, and why.

No adjectives, no speculation, nothing about the allegations beyond what the
district said. The headline verb ("booted") is the only loose word. Every claim is
attributed to a named office.

For the sim: a dismissal story needs only the action, the program's record under the
coach, the successor, and a line for what is not known. The carousel already
records moves without a stated reason, which matches how this is written.

## 4. The playoff game story (statewide, ~300 words)

- A bold score line first: "Glencoe 41, West Salem 34".
- Lead: the deciding play with its clock time, the score it broke, the site, and
  what the title is.
- The star's season line inside a subordinate clause: "ran for 207 yards and three
  touchdowns on 40 carries to finish the three ... games with 850 yards and 11
  touchdowns".
- Records in parentheses on first mascot reference: "the Crimson Tide (7-5)".
- The lead change told in three sentences with clock times.
- Two player quotes, both from the star.
- Then one sentence each of stat lines for the other notable players, both teams.

## 5. The all-league list (statewide)

- A one-sentence top naming the league, the classification and who chose it: "as
  determined by a vote of the league's coaches".
- Individual awards first: offensive and defensive player of the year, linemen of
  the year, coach of the year.
- FIRST TEAM / SECOND TEAM / HONORABLE MENTION, each split Offense / Defense.
- One line a player: position, name, school, class. "QB NAME, Cascade, junior".
- A player can appear at several positions and on both sides; the list does not
  dedupe.
- Honorable mention is the longest section.

## 6. What this means for the generator

- The local desk's week is not only results: previews with a time and a seed, an
  all-league story per sport, galleries, fundraisers, coaching changes, senior
  farewells.
- Every statewide list has a local rewrite that leads with the home names. One
  honours batch feeds many local stories.
- A long local story about a second-place finish works by being honest about the
  winner and specific about next year.
- A coach's departure can be written entirely from the record and the succession.
