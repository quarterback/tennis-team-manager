Weather, nickname and ritual each appear in six stories or fewer.

**What the richer sports carry that tennis does not.** Crowd and place (the "orange half of the capacity crowd", a pep band, a school-song lyric), bodies (heights, a cane, a knee brace), injuries, and ONE play told in slow motion (a fourth-down throw "almost from a seated position"). They also quote the loser: in ten big-sport gamers the losing side got 19 of 66 quotes, in nine tennis result stories 4 of 54. Tennis stories instead run on arithmetic (flights advanced, seeds, 106-6, 46.125 points), on the player's own account of nerves and tactics, and on back-references to earlier meetings. The exceptions show what is possible: KSHSAA's two singles finals (about 1,100 words each) and CHSAA's dual final told in the order the team points fell, ending in a "flood of Raiders on the court".

**Quotes.** About one per 125 words; median 8 per story. Of roughly 450 counted: coaches 42%, players/alumni 39%, family 9%, losing side 7%, others (athletic director, sports editor, archives) 3%. Coaches explain, credit and set expectations; players relive a moment, admit nerves, name a teammate; parents supply childhood and logistics; losers concede ("They were the better team today"). Jokes come almost only from older coaches. "Said" is nearly universal; "added", "recalled", "joked", "said with a laugh" are the variations.

**Length.** Features and finals: 800-1,400 words (median about 1,150). Long history pieces: 2,000-2,450. Short newspaper-like gamers: 420-630. Releases with no quotes: 290-390.

**How features are built.** Open on an object or scene (a blue towel kept 34 years, a "State Champs" group chat, an old photo, a T-shirt reprint, a wife fetching car keys); nut paragraph with the number; then biography hung on the school calendar; two to five voices introduced by relationship and graduating class; a block of current-season results; often a full lineup. Coach pieces add rules and sayings ("an automatic two miles"), a spouse, a mentor, former players who now coach. Athlete pieces add siblings, other sports, GPA, college and one remembered game. Small-school sharing of athletes recurs. Twins and parent-coaches appear in nine stories.

**Endings.** Quote kicker in 31 of 55 (look-ahead, tribute, reflection, a joke, the loser's last word). The rest close on a stat or record line, a list or agate block, a return to the opening image, or, rarely, the writer's own line ("Onward.").

**Not obtained.** OSAA (403). MHSAA bylines were not captured, and MHSAA dates are the first date found on each page. Football is one short of ten: one file tagged fb is a basketball rivalry game. Arizona's features are by journalism students.
