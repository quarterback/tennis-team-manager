# JHSAA longitudinal god-mode analysis — HS2079–HS2086

Eight complete seasons, with HS2085 now filling the previous gap. The analysis uses varsity `duals`, `lines`, `line_players`, season-specific player ratings, program history, individual championships, and the Program Coefficient.

## Expected-value model

For individual line results, expected win probability is estimated from rating differential with slot, gender, and season controls. Player WAE is actual wins minus summed expected wins. Program-flight residual is actual flight win percentage minus rating-model expectation.

## Longitudinal findings

### Persistent nemeses

The one-season 2086 repeat-upset list was too permissive. Requiring at least three meetings across at least two seasons produces durable H2H anomalies. The strongest historical case in the eight-season window is Kendall Artiga of Northgate (8A), 5–0 against Nada Ismail of Orellana Central (8A) across 2081–2083 despite averaging 6.6 rating points lower. Among matchups still active in 2086, Ty Roehl of Passaic (4A) is 4–0 across 2085–2086 against Javier Falcón of Edgewater (4A) while averaging nine points lower; Tohi Ngatai of Trailsend (3A) is 4–0 against Tristan Gonzalez of Hartford City (3A) while seven points lower; Drayk Chinh of Lodestone County (Group 2) is 3–0 against Hayden Chen of Minidoka (Group 2) while 13.7 points lower.

### Career WAE and punch-up

The highest complete-career WAE in the window belongs to Alexis Gomez of West El Paso (6A): 63 wins in 92 singles matches against 47.2 expected, +15.8 WAE. Yeon-seo Ryang of Romare Bearden (Group 1) went only 58–59 but accumulated +15.5 WAE because of repeated difficult placements. Delaney Elms of Metropolitan Country Day (5A) won 10 matches while down 10+ rating points and finished +12.4 career WAE. Toni Singh of Vesper (1A) won only 18 of 72 overall, but 12 of those wins came while down 10+ points; the worst rating deficit faced was 57 points.

### Great careers without titles

Restricting to complete four-year careers, no individual championship, and no team State championship during the player's active seasons yields a real no-hardware class. Bria Stephens of Joseph (1A) went 84–7 (92.3%) with four individual entries and a semifinal as the best finish. Grayson Bates of Romero-Finniski (7A) went 93–8 (92.1%) with a quarterfinal as the best finish. Evalyn Gamble of Klickitat (1A) went 99–9 (91.7%). A more program-trapped example is Eleanor Johnson of Michaeux (Group 3): 93–12 (88.6%), zero team-State seasons during her four-year run, and one individual final without a title.

### Close-dual persistence

The 2086 one-season records were not reliable labels by themselves. Valera (1A girls) was 9–0 in one-point duals in 2086, but is only 40–31 (56.3%) across 2079–2086. Emerson (6A girls) was 8–0 in 2086 but is 33–37 over the full window. Paddock Tech (2A girls) was 8–0 in 2086 but 44–32 overall. The genuine persistent outliers are different: Cliffside (9A boys) is 49–18 (73.1%) and above .500 in all eight seasons; Fort Weller (4A girls) is 49–19 (72.1%) and above .500 in seven of eight. On the negative side, Warren (4A girls) is 16–45 (26.2%) with zero above-.500 seasons, Silver Junction (Group 3 girls) is 15–41 (26.8%) and below .500 all eight seasons, and Kernwood County (6A girls) is 20–49 (29.0%). These are persistent conversion patterns, not one-season clutch labels.

### Flight efficiency that persists across cohorts

Several program-flight residuals survive eight years of roster turnover. Sea Cliff (5A boys) D3 is +11.0 percentage points over rating expectation across 192 matches, positive in seven of eight seasons. Arthur (5A boys) D3 is +10.5 across 217 matches and positive all eight seasons. Tungsten (Group 3 girls) D2 is +10.4 across 223 and positive all eight. Banneker (9A boys) D2 is +9.7 across 287 and positive all eight. Canaan Bend (2A boys) S1 is +9.3 across 216 and positive all eight.

The inverse is equally durable: Copper Gap (1A girls) D2 is -10.0 across 203 matches and negative all eight seasons; Borough Beach (8A girls) D2 is -9.7 across 200 and negative all eight; Michaela East (4A girls) S2 is -8.8 across 192 and negative all eight; Quincy (Group 3 girls) D1 is -8.9 across 245 and negative all eight.

This also revises some 2086-only conclusions. Mission Terrace (7A boys) S2 was a large 2086 positive anomaly, but across eight years the same flight is only +3.1 and splits 4 positive / 4 negative seasons. Temescal (8A girls) D1's 2086 collapse is also not a long-term D1 identity: the eight-year residual is only -2.8. Robledo (7A boys), however, does retain a durable D2 edge: +8.8 across 240 matches, positive in seven of eight seasons.

### Program system resilience after an elite S1 graduates

An anchor is the primary S1, a senior, and top-decile by S1 rating within that year's class. The metric compares the program's average class-rank percentile in the anchor's final two seasons with the next two seasons. This is descriptive program resilience, not a claim that one player's graduation caused every subsequent change.

Largest collapses include Maritime (6A girls) after Kaylyn Fay in 2081: from an average 5.6th percentile in-class position to 96.5th percentile, with win percentage down 66.9 points; Kettle Ridge (7A boys) after Lukas Lathrop in 2081: +86.3 percentile points worse; Portola-Caverly (1A boys) after Micah Swanson in 2082: +84.8; and B. Harrison (7A girls) after Kristie Dewberry in 2080: +80.0.

Repeated reloaders are more convincing evidence of program structure. Baptist (7A boys) had three qualifying anchor departures and averaged only +0.3 class-percentile deterioration; all three events met the reload threshold. Westfield Friends (5A girls), Joseph (1A boys), Terrace Park (Group 1 boys), Averill (4A boys), and Porterfield (2A girls) each handled two qualifying anchor departures with essentially no sustained deterioration or actual improvement. Oakhaven (6A girls) improved sharply after both qualifying anchor departures.

### Rivalries and curses

Some pairings are actual eight-season series rather than isolated results. Chicago Island (Group 2 boys) and Spring Harvest (Group 2 boys) are exactly 10–10 over 20 duals. Rockridge (9A boys) and Mater Dei (9A boys) are 10–10 over 20. Quarmont (3A boys) and San Lorenzo (3A boys) are 10–10 over 20, with nine one-point duals. California Beach (1A girls) and Pellford (1A girls) are 10–9 with 11 one-point duals. Wyalusing (4A boys) and Sulphur Springs (4A boys) are 9–10 with 12 one-point duals.

There are also true program curses. Baptist (7A boys) is 19–0 against Lone Pine (7A boys) in this window. Walter-Kenny (4A boys) is 19–0 against Indigo Bend (4A boys). Chaminade (6A boys) is 18–0 against Redfork West (6A boys). Westfield Friends (5A boys) is 18–0 against Pine Barrens (5A boys). These are much stronger universe lore than a single upset or streak.

### Coefficient pedigree versus current form

The Program Coefficient now creates a clean institutional-vs-current-form split. Larchmont Ridge (8A girls) is coefficient No. 2 but finished 2086 class-rank 57; Canyonlands (7A girls) is coefficient No. 3 but current rank 57; Chaparral (8A boys) is coefficient No. 4 but current rank 66; La Grande (5A girls) is coefficient No. 5 but rank 80. Those are true fallen-pedigree programs.

The opposite quadrant identifies current powers without much recent championship pedigree. Driftwood (4A boys) is class-rank 4 with coefficient rank 34. Springfield (6A boys) is class-rank 4 with coefficient rank 34. Deaconsburg (7A boys) is class-rank 9 with coefficient rank 36. De La Salle (9A girls) is class-rank 10 with coefficient rank 58. These are breakouts rather than established powers.

## What HS2085 changed

Adding HS2085 closes the only hole in the 2079–2086 series. It matters particularly for active nemesis detection, two-year post-anchor windows for 2084 seniors, and distinguishing 2086 one-point-record noise from actual persistence. The most important correction is that several 2086 'clutch' examples disappear as long-run signals, while Cliffside (9A boys), Fort Weller (4A girls), Warren (4A girls), Silver Junction (Group 3 girls), and Kernwood County (6A girls) remain extreme across the full eight-season sample.
