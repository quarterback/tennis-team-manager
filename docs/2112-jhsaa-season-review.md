# The 2038 (2112) JHSAA Tennis Season

*Internal annual review*

This was a realignment season. The four year cycle moved 318 boys and 332 girls programs to a new class, about a third of the association, and it was the first cycle to sort the private schools between 10B and 11B. Basalt, Peregrine and Washington San Cordero came out of 11B into their public classes, 4A, 3A and 1A. Trois Ilets dropped from 9A to 5A. The 2112 transfer class landed, with 5,574 of its 5,633 players at the school it named.

## Executive findings

**Canal View's boys won the Tournament of Champions, the first public school to win it since Chaparral in 2108.** They won Group 1 for a second straight year, 6-3 over Kishwaukee, then beat Mabry Township 4-1, Barley Gap 5-0, Chaparral 3-2 and Romero-Finniski 3-2 in the final. Romero-Finniski had won three straight 3-2 duals to get there.

**Romero-Finniski's girls went 33-0 and won a second straight Tournament of Champions.** They won 11B 7-0 over The Greaves School, then beat Marigny 4-1 and New Casper, Seawall and Mercy Academy Valley, each 3-2. Mercy Academy Valley, the 10B runner up, came through the qualifier and reached the final.

**Realignment produced seven champions in a new class.** Barley Gap moved from 3A to 1A and swept it, the boys 26-1 and the girls 27-3. Brackwood Pass's girls went the other way, from 1A to 3A, and won again at 37-1. Bush's boys moved from 7A to 8A and won, Penobscot's from 6A to 7A and won a third straight title, Mt Jacqueline's from 4A to 2A and won, Mabry Township's from Group 2 to Group 3 and won from the 9 seed, and Elk Bluff West's girls from Group 1 to Group 3 and won.

**Programs that moved up won less and programs that moved down won more, by about ten points each.** The 329 that moved up fell from winning 61 percent of their matches to 52 percent. The 321 that moved down rose from 33 to 41 percent. Chickasaw's boys went from 21-9 in Group 2 to 4-19 in Group 1 and the girls from 25-8 to 5-18, the only two collapses of the year.

**Six programs won a first State title:** Millworks (4A boys), Windward (3A boys), Mabry Township (Group 3 boys), Santa Cruz del Norte (8A girls), Severn (7A girls) and New Casper (2A girls). Millworks lost its Zonal and its Super Regional and came through the Divisionals as the 7 seed.

**The qualifier split by gender again, the other way.** Mendoza (8A) beat Condotti Vanguard Academy and Chaparral (9A) beat Walter-Kenny on the boys side. Chaparral then reached the TOC semifinal. Mercy Academy Valley (10B) and Marigny (8A) won the girls qualifiers. Qualifier winners have won 6 of 40 entries and reached ten more finals or semifinals.

**Sitting seniors rose to 12.6 percent** from 11.1, with the class in place both years. The realignment moved a third of the programs to a new lineup size after the class was built on the old ones.

**No Circuit champion won State** for the first time since 2107. Tunnelton reached the 4A boys final and six more reached a semifinal. Sector coverage fell to 72 percent of class seasons, the lowest since the round began, with the realignment redrawing every Circuit class.

**L.B. Johnson's boys won a third straight 9A title at 31-1,** 7-2 over Chaparral, and Canyonlands, up from 8A at 39-1, lost the semifinal. Seawall's girls repeated 9-0 over Glen Park, also up from 8A.

## How this review reads the season

Team results come from records, seeds and the championship brackets. Program history comes from the archive back to 2027. Private schools keep their league class in program history, and their State result is on the 10B or 11B road. No player rating appears anywhere in this review. Prior year records for programs that changed class are their records in the old class.

## Pre high school development, eighth season

There were 548 seventh graders and 1,079 eighth graders, 1,627 in all, up from 1,477. 58 percent of the seventh graders and 64 percent of the eighth graders appeared in a varsity dual, and they won 50 and 49 percent of their flights. 1,047 of the 14,034 seniors had played early, up from 982.

The boost recorded for early players has a median of 15.9 percent of base ceiling. The bottom tenth got 5.3 percent or less and the top tenth 39.6 percent or more. 28.3 percent got 25 percent or more, 4.0 percent got 50 percent or more and 27.3 percent got under 10 percent. Players who started early sit 0.73 standard deviations ahead of their classmates, against 0.68 a year ago.
