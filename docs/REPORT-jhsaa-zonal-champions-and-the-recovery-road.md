# REPORT: Zonal champions and the recovery road

Two questions, asked while weighing whether the JHSAA needs a new way to sort its best teams, something like a New Jersey sectional title or a CIF section setup:

1. How often does a team that did not win a Zonal title win State?
2. After the Zonals, who is still alive, how many rounds are left, and which of those teams actually do anything once they reach State?

The association treats the playoffs as access and a reward for participating as much as a way to crown champions. So the point here is to see where the title is actually decided and where the participation happens, before changing anything.

## Part 1: Zonal champions win State nine times in ten

Every State title from 2027 through 2096 was checked, both genders, all classes: 1,487 titles.

| Seasons | Titles | Won by a Zonal champion |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2027 to 2073 | 935 | 88% |
| 2074 to 2092 | 456 | 93% |
| 2093 to 2096 | 96 | 93% |
| All seasons | 1,487 | 90% |

Boys and girls are close: 89% of boys titles and 91% of girls titles went to Zonal champions. In 2096 all 24 champions were Zonal champions.

### The Epiregionals narrow it further

Since the Epiregionals began in 2069, each class has four Epiregional champions drawn from its eight Zonal champions. Those four teams won **83%** of the 667 titles played since.

The No. 1 seed alone won 45% of all titles. Seeds 1 and 2 together won 65%.

### Who wins the other 10%

About two titles a season across all 24 classes go to a team that did not win its Zonal. Nineteen of the 70 seasons had none at all, and the most in one season was ten.

## Part 2: The road after the Zonals

### How the State field is built

Most classes build State from a road of 32 plus committee at large bids:

| Class | Road | At large bids | Entrants | Counted as State appearances |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9A, 8A, Group 1 | 32 | 16 | 48 | 40 |
| 7A through 2A, Group 2 | 32 | 8 | 40 | 36 |
| 1A, Group 3 | 24 | 8 | 32 | 32 |

A Metastate loss does not count as a State appearance, which is why the last column is smaller in the Metastate classes.

### Every round, in order (32 team road, one gender)

**Road to State**

1. **Areas and Sectionals** reduce the non-protected field to 32.
2. **Wards:** 32 play, 16 advance.
3. **Regionals:** the 16 Ward winners plus 16 protected teams. 32 play, 16 advance.
4. **Zonals:** 16 play, and the **8 Zonal champions qualify**.
5. **Epiregionals:** the 8 Zonal champions play each other to set seeds 1 through 8.

**Recovery, for the other 24 road berths**

6. **Super Regionals:** the 16 Regional losers play, 8 advance.
7. **Semi-State:** the 8 Super Regional winners against the 8 Zonal losers. **8 qualify.**
8. **Divisionals:** the 8 Semi-State losers against the 8 Super Regional losers. **8 qualify.**
9. **Semi-Conference:** 16 teams, drawn from district champions still outside the field and then the best Ward, Sectional and Area losers. 8 advance.
10. **Conference:** the 8 Semi-Conference winners against the 8 Divisional losers. 8 advance.
11. **State Specials:** each of the 8 Conference winners plays a challenger. **8 qualify.**

**Committee side**

12. **Metastate:** 9A through 2A, Group 1 and Group 2. The lowest seeded at large teams play each other.
13. **Parastate:** the remaining at large teams and Metastate winners play the lowest road seeds.

**State main draw**

14. Round of 32, Octofinals, Quarterfinals, Semifinals, Final.

That is six recovery rounds after the Zonals, then one or two committee rounds, before the main draw starts. 1A and Group 3 run the smaller version, with four Divisional berths and four Specials berths.

### Who is still alive once the Zonals finish

| Group | Count (32 team road) | What they are playing for |
|---|---:|---|
| Zonal champions | 8 | Already in, seeded 1 through 8 |
| Zonal losers | 8 | Semi-State, then the Divisionals if they lose |
| Regional losers | 16 | Super Regionals, then Semi-State or the Divisionals |
| Semi-Conference entrants | 16 | Conference, then the State Specials |
| Specials challengers | 8 | One dual against a Conference winner |
| At large candidates | 8 or 16 bids | Metastate and Parastate |

Roughly 40 to 50 teams are still in play for the 24 remaining road berths plus the committee bids.

## Part 3: What each route does once it reaches State

State results by entry route, 2093 to 2096, both genders:

| Route into State | Teams | Median seed | Reach QF | Reach SF | Reach final | Titles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Zonal champion | 768 | 4.5 | 76% | 44% | 23% | 89 |
| Semi-State winner | 768 | 13 | 19% | 6% | 2% | 7 |
| Divisional winner | 704 | 21 | 4% | 0.3% | 0% | 0 |
| State Specials | 767 | 27 | 2% | 0% | 0% | 0 |
| Committee at large | 481 | 34 | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0 |

The 2087 to 2092 seasons show the same pattern:

| Route into State | Teams | Reach QF | Reach SF | Titles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Zonal champion | 1,152 | 76% | 42% | 137 |
| Semi-State winner | 1,152 | 20% | 7% | 6 |
| Divisional winner | 1,056 | 4% | 0.8% | 1 |
| State Specials | 1,239 | 0.7% | 0.3% | 0 |
| Committee at large | 1,257 | 0.2% | 0% | 0 |

### What the table shows

1. **The deep runs belong to the Semi-State winners.** The dozen or so semifinalists each season who did not win a Zonal are almost all Semi-State winners. That round, a Zonal loser against a Super Regional winner, is where the upstarts come from.
2. **The lower routes cause early damage, not runs.** From 2093 to 2096, 46 Zonal champions lost their Round of 32 dual to a team seeded 25 through 32, and 138 more lost in the Octofinals. Divisional, Specials and at large teams also beat a share of the Semi-State winners in the Round of 32. They almost never survive the Octofinals.
3. **Everything from the Divisionals on fills seeds 17 through 40 or 48.** That part of the road is the participation layer. It produces State appearances and first round upsets, not champions.

## Part 4: Shapes considered

None of these is adopted. They are the options the data points to.

### A. A 16 team State

The 8 Zonal champions plus the 8 Semi-State winners. Over the last four seasons this field would have held every champion and almost every semifinalist.

It removes the Divisionals, Semi-Conference, Conference, State Specials, Metastate and Parastate, about eight rounds in total. State appearances fall from 32 to 48 entrants per class to 16. That cuts directly against the access mandate.

### B. A 24 team State with one sorted round

1. Zonal champions are seeds 1 through 8 and sit out the first round.
2. Semi-State winners are seeds 9 through 16.
3. Every other team still alive is sorted into 8 buckets. Each bucket plays down to one winner, and those 8 winners are seeds 17 through 24.

The Divisionals, Semi-Conference, Conference, State Specials, Metastate and Parastate collapse into that one bucket stage. The first round still pairs seeds 9 through 16 against seeds 17 through 24, which keeps the lane where lower teams knock out Semi-State winners.

Open question: are the buckets sorted purely by rating, or by the round a team was eliminated in, the way the Semi-Conference orders its pool now?

### C. Keep the full field, and split off the bottom

State keeps 32 to 48 entrants, but seeds 17 and below play their own bracket at the State site and the main draw starts at 16. The main decision is whether that bracket counts as a State appearance, the same question the Metastate answered with no.

## Method

Source: the 2096 research export's `jhsaa_program_history.csv` for both genders, which carries every season from 2027. A team counts as a Zonal champion when its `unit_wins` field lists a Zone title. That flag matches State seeds 1 through 8 in 11,376 of 11,655 cases. The four Epiregional champions per class are the teams whose `unit_wins` lists an Epiregional title. Entry routes use the highest qualifying title in `unit_wins`: Zone, then Semi-State, then Division, then State Special or Challenge; everyone else is a committee at large entrant. Round counts come from the 2096 `duals.csv` postseason phases and the `STATE_FIELD` and `AT_LARGE_BIDS` tables in `app/jhsaa.py`. Round order within State and the TOC comes from `jhsaa_championships.json`, because dual dates in those rounds do not follow bracket order.
