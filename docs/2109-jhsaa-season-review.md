# The 2035 (2109) JHSAA Tennis Season

*Internal annual review*

This was the third season of the Circuit round and the first season of the seniors first transfer class. It was not a realignment year. The export predates the public and private status fixes, so Evans Larsen Day, Basalt, Peregrine and Washington San Cordero appear in this review in the classes they were in before.

## Executive findings

**Gottschalk-Herman's girls won the Tournament of Champions again after losing the 10B final.** They were the top seed in 10B at 36-1, lost the 10B final 5-4 to Mater Dei, won the TOC Qualifier 3-2 over Cassius, and then won four duals: 4-1 over 9A champion Seawall, 4-1 over Evans Larsen Day, 3-2 over Romero-Finniski and 4-1 over Mater Dei in the final. Mater Dei had beaten them in the 10B final, and the two could meet only in the TOC final. Qualifier winners have now won 4 of the 14 Tournaments of Champions since the qualifier began.

**Mater Dei's boys won the Tournament of Champions at 46-1.** They won 10B 6-3 over Walter-Kenny and then beat Orchard Union 3-2, Chaparral 3-2, Jackson Hole 4-1 and Canyonlands, the 8A champion, 3-2 in the final.

**Two Circuit champions won State titles.** Taft's girls won 4A as the 8 seed, 3-2 over Texas Beach, and Latgaway's girls won Group 3 as the 4 seed, 3-2 over top seeded Trout Lake. Bianchi's boys reached the 4A final from the 9 seed. In all, 9 of the 144 Circuit champions reached a State semifinal or better, up from 8 in 2108.

**Chaparral's boys won 9A for the third straight year**, their fifth title, 6-3 over Natchitoches, which reached its first final. Pacific Gate's boys, under Nolan Martinez, went 32-3 and reached the semifinal. Pacific Gate's girls reached the 9A girls final and lost 5-4 to Seawall.

**The seniors first transfer class changed who plays.** Seniors with no varsity appearance fell from 15.3 percent of the class in 2108 (1,989 of 13,020) to 10.7 percent in 2109 (1,401 of 13,079). 4,309 seniors moved in the class, 90 percent appeared in a varsity dual, and 26 percent played 20 or more varsity flights.

**The large city programs fell back in year four.** The 22 programs won 36 percent of their matches. 3 of 11 boys programs and 4 of 11 girls programs made State.

**Three programs won a first state title:** Netherwood (3A boys), Iron Gate (6A girls) and Lewis (2A girls). Netherwood swept 3A.

**Romero-Finniski swept 11B,** its 11th boys title and 18th girls title. Three of its girls went unbeaten: sophomore Amelia Kumar (34-0), freshman Kaylee Williams (34-0) and freshman Aylin Voss (31-0).

**The 2109 transfer class landed.** 5,144 of the 5,217 players in the file were on 2109 rosters and 5,143 were at the school named. The other 73 were on no 2109 roster. Nobody was sent to Trois Ilets.

## The Tournament of Champions

**Boys.** Walter-Kenny, the 10B runner up, beat Banfield Day, the 11B runner up, 4-1, and Orchard Union, the 8A runner up, beat Natchitoches, the 9A runner up, 3-2 in the qualifier. Orchard Union then lost 3-2 to Mater Dei in round one, and Walter-Kenny beat Kishwaukee 3-2 and lost 3-2 to Romero-Finniski in the quarterfinal. Mater Dei beat Chaparral 3-2 and Jackson Hole 4-1 before the final. Canyonlands beat Fort Meriwether 5-0, Netherwood 3-2 and Romero-Finniski 3-2 to reach it.

**Girls.** Gottschalk-Herman beat Cassius 3-2 and Pacific Gate beat Sherwood Bench 4-1 in the qualifier. Pacific Gate then beat Canal View 3-2 and lost 3-2 to Glen Park in the quarterfinal. Gottschalk-Herman and Mater Dei came from opposite halves of the draw. Mater Dei beat Casco 5-0, Iron Gate 5-0 and Glen Park 4-1 to reach the final, where Gottschalk-Herman won 4-1.

**Qualifier record since it began.** Of 28 qualifier winners across seven seasons, four won the Tournament of Champions (Pacific Gate girls in 2104, Jesuit boys in 2106 and Gottschalk-Herman girls in 2108 and 2109), six more reached a semifinal or final, and 18 were out within two rounds.

## Pre high school development, fifth season

There were 488 seventh graders and 895 eighth graders, 1,383 in all, down from 1,494. 59 percent of the seventh graders and 63 percent of the eighth graders appeared in a varsity dual, and they won 53 percent of their flights. 710 of the 13,079 seniors had played early, up from 389.

The boost recorded for early players has a median of 15.9 percent of base ceiling. The bottom tenth got 5.5 percent or less and the top tenth got 39.0 percent or more. 27.7 percent got 25 percent or more, 4.1 percent got 50 percent or more and 27.3 percent got under 10 percent. 54 percent of recorded players have two early seasons. Compared with classmates in the same program and grade, early players in grades 10 to 12 sit about 0.58 standard deviations ahead on ability, against 0.59 a year ago.
