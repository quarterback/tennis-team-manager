# JHSAA 2033 Season Retrospective and Association Report

The uploaded research export is internally labeled `2107`; this report treats it as canonical **2033**, following the established retrofit (`2106` = 2032).

## Executive findings

- **Mater Dei girls completed a 43-0 season by winning 10B and the Tournament of Champions.** They were the only undefeated varsity program in either gender.
- **Chaparral boys won 9A and then the TOC**, finishing 39-2. The two TOC winners therefore came from the top of the ordinary championship structure rather than the qualifier this year.
- **The Circuit debuted at full scale and behaved almost exactly like the model.** Across the nine Circuit classes in both genders, Circuit champions averaged **5.06 of the best eight** teams in each 32-team Circuit field and **7.17 of eight from the best sixteen**. The 70-season model projected 4.6 and 7.0.
- **The Circuit did not dilute the State championship layer.** None of the 144 Circuit champions across both genders reached a State final. Only four reached a State semifinal. **Twenty-six of the 28 State champions were Zonal champions**; the other two came through later recovery.
- **The former 16-team State classes expanded sharply.** In 2032, 4A, 3A, 2A, 1A and Group 3 each crowned from 16. In 2033 the played fields were 32 in 4A/3A/2A and 24 in 1A/Group 3. That added 64 State duals per gender. Lower-seed wins in those five classes fell from 30.7% to 18.7% for boys and from 32.0% to 19.4% for girls.
- **The new TOC qualifier seeding rule was visible immediately.** Qualifier winners occupied the 15/16 lines. No qualifier won the TOC, but boys #15 Forest Park still upset #2 Toussaint and reached the semifinal. The low seed is a penalty, not an exclusion.
- **The four-year Condotti Vanguard / Romero-Finniski Sunkist anomaly survived one final season in the archive.** Both were 11B but again played a two-team Sunkist League. CV went 2-0 in district in both genders and reached both State semifinals; RF went 0-2 in both. The post-season code investigation identified stale public-ladder play-up routing as the cause; the archive remains honest about the season as played.
- **No coaching-carousel cycle was committed between 2032 and 2033.** Among programs with head-coach rows in both seasons, **0 of 940 boys programs and 0 of 976 girls programs changed heads**, and there are no 2107 coaching events. This season therefore provides no new evidence about carousel rates; it documents the performance failure that prevented the cycle from being run.
- The middle-school pipeline continued to grow: **687 boys and 754 girls in grades 7-8**, with 501 boys and 529 girls appearing in varsity play. The accelerator and exception frequencies remained close to design.
- The rising-freshman portal remained opportunity-focused: **163 boys and 193 girls moved**, and 162 boys / 187 girls appeared in varsity competition.
- The calendar retained its hard rule: **zero district doubleheaders and zero undated district duals** in both genders.

## Tournament of Champions

### Boys

The qualifier winners were **Forest Park over Belyakov Vo-Tech, 3-2**, and **Mercy Academy Valley over Hazel Country Day, 4-1**.

Under the new rule Forest Park entered at **No. 15** and Mercy Academy Valley at **No. 16**. Mercy opened against No. 1 Chaparral and lost 3-2. Forest Park immediately upset No. 2 Toussaint 4-1, then beat Chaminade 3-2 before Plainfield stopped the run 5-0 in the semifinal.

Chaparral's TOC path was Mercy Academy Valley 3-2, Meridian 3-2, Texas Beach 5-0 and Plainfield **4-1** in the final. Chaparral finished 39-2 with both the 9A and overall titles.

### Girls

The qualifier winners were **Gottschalk-Herman over Jesuit, 3-2**, and **Narragansett over Lone Pine, 3-2**. Both then lost in the opening TOC round: No. 16 Gottschalk-Herman to Mater Dei 4-1 and No. 15 Narragansett to California Canyons 3-2.

Mater Dei beat Gottschalk-Herman 4-1, Perry Green 3-2, Mt Jacqueline 4-1 and California Canyons **3-2** to finish the season 43-0.

### The 15/16 rule

The first live season supports the basic rule. Three of the four qualifier winners exited immediately, while Forest Park showed that a State runner-up strong enough to beat champions can still make a run from the 15 line. The qualifier no longer gives a runner-up a seed line comparable to the champions it failed to join automatically.

One protection edge needs a regression check. On the boys side, Mercy Academy Valley was the 11B runner-up and entered at No. 16 while 11B champion Georgia Mills remained on the No. 8 line. Under the strict seed-line bracket those lines occupy the same half, so the stated State-final rematch protection was not actually satisfied even though Georgia Mills lost before the teams could meet.

## The Circuit: first live season

The Circuit ran in **1A, 2A, 3A, 4A, Group 1, Group 2, Group 3, 10B and 11B**. Every class produced the intended 32-team Circuit population, eight four-team geographic Circuits and eight Circuit champions.

The best test is the one used when the rule was designed: how much merit survives after geography organizes the 32.

| Live 2033 result | Boys | Girls | Combined | 70-season model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Best 8 of the 32 who won a Circuit | 4.67 / 8 | 5.44 / 8 | **5.06 / 8** | 4.6 / 8 |
| Circuit champions drawn from best 16 | 7.11 / 8 | 7.22 / 8 | **7.17 / 8** | 7.0 / 8 |

The live result is slightly more merit-heavy than the model, but it is the same shape. Geography costs roughly one of the eight top-half entrants, not half the field.

The championship layer remained much harder than the Circuit itself:

- 72 Circuit champions were crowned per gender;
- **no Circuit champion reached a State final**;
- one boys Circuit champion and three girls Circuit champions reached a State semifinal;
- nine boys and fifteen girls Circuit champions reached at least a State quarterfinal;
- the average Circuit champion entered State around seed 14-15.

At the same time, Zonal champions won **12 of 14 boys State titles and all 14 girls titles**. The two exceptions were Morne Caribou boys in 6A, which came through Semi-State, and Dufur boys in Group 3, which survived later recovery through a Divisional.

That is a useful first-season result: the Circuit created another meaningful geographic championship and a State berth, but it did not become the easiest way to win State.

A rough geography check moves in the expected direction. Using the association's named Areas rather than the modeled eight sectors, the mean share of a Circuit class's Areas represented in its State field rose from **76.7% to 84.9% for boys** and **78.6% to 83.6% for girls** from 2032 to 2033. Because several State fields also expanded, this is directional evidence rather than a clean estimate of the Circuit alone.

## State-field expansion

The Circuit season also ended the practical 16-team shape in the five pilot classes.

| Class | 2032 State field | 2033 State field |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4A | 16 | **32** |
| 3A | 16 | **32** |
| 2A | 16 | **32** |
| 1A | 16 | **24** |
| Group 3 | 16 | **24** |

Across all fourteen classes, State grew from **378 to 442 duals per gender**.

The same five classes changed texture substantially:

| | 2032 boys | 2033 boys | 2032 girls | 2033 girls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lower seed won | 30.7% | **18.7%** | 32.0% | **19.4%** |
| One-position margin | 42.7% | **38.8%** | 53.3% | **33.1%** |

This is not evidence that the Circuit itself reduces upsets. The most obvious intervention is field size: the 2033 tournaments include substantially more teams before reaching the rounds that used to constitute the entire 16-team State event. It does show that the old conclusion about the 16-team pilot — unusually concentrated, close State play — no longer describes these classes.

Association-wide, lower seeds won **21.5%** of State duals in both genders, down from 27.5% boys / 23.5% girls in 2032.

## Championship turnover

The boys title map turned over almost completely. **Chaminade (10B)** was the only boys champion to repeat from 2032.

The girls had four repeat champions:

- Glen Park, 7A;
- Hackensack, 5A;
- Mt Jacqueline, 1A;
- Monongahela, Group 2.

Mater Dei's 10B title was its fifteenth State championship in the archive and its first since 2031. The 43-0 season then ended with the TOC title.

## The last Sunkist season

The archive confirms that the Condotti Vanguard / Romero-Finniski anomaly lasted **four seasons, 2030-2033**.

In 2030-32 the pair sat alone in 10B Sunkist. By 2033 both had moved to 11B, but the stale play-up league path still reassigned their School objects to the public 8A Sunkist name while leaving their championship group at 11B. The result was the same two-team district for a fourth year.

| 2033 | Boys | Girls |
|---|---|---|
| Condotti Vanguard Academy | 19-2, **2-0 district**, State semifinal | 17-1, **2-0 district**, State semifinal |
| Romero-Finniski | 4-10, **0-2 district**, Wards | 15-6, **0-2 district**, State octofinal |

The competitive story is not that both programs needed protection. Romero-Finniski is no longer the power it once was; its girls had a strong rebound this season, but the boys remained ordinary. CV is still a State-level program, reaching both 11B semifinals, but it still has **not won a 10B/11B State title**.

The code defect was identified after this season: Non-Public programs were correctly blocked from changing championship group through public play-up logic, but `_compute_playup_league` could still give them the public play-up league name. Excluding 10B/11B from that path removes the mechanism that recreated Sunkist. The 2033 archive should remain as played.

## Coaching carousel

There is no 2033 carousel result to evaluate.

Between the archived 2032 and 2033 staffs:

| | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Continuing programs with head rows | 940 | 976 |
| Changed head coach | **0** | **0** |
| Assistant-to-head moves | **0** | **0** |
| Assistant lateral moves | **0** | **0** |
| 2107 coach events | **0** | **0** |

That is not evidence that the churn model suddenly became stable. The carousel is owner-triggered, and no cycle successfully committed. The performance investigation after the season found that `propose_cycle` still reconstructs and rescans the statewide market repeatedly while filling a large number of churn-created vacancies. Until that execution path is made cheap enough to run, 2033 should be treated as a frozen-staff season rather than another sample of the labor-market model.

## Calendar

The hard scheduling constraints remained intact:

- **zero district doubleheaders** in both genders;
- **zero undated district duals** in both genders;
- undated varsity duals were ancillary/non-district only.

Displayed spans were August 2-November 13 for boys and March 2-June 14 for girls. There were 995 undated boys varsity duals and 1,205 undated girls varsity duals.

## What 2033 validates

Working as intended:

- the Circuit can run at association scale without special stocking or quota rules;
- the Circuit's observed merit cost is close to the 70-season model;
- geography can be inserted into the recovery path without weakening Zonals;
- the 15/16 TOC qualifier penalty still allows a genuinely strong runner-up to make a run;
- early-participant intake, development rolls and portal conversion remain stable at larger scale;
- district scheduling still respects the no-doubleheader rule.

Needs follow-up:

- the Sunkist defect is visible in the 2033 archive and has now been traced to stale play-up league routing for Non-Public programs;
- the coaching carousel did not run, so its statewide-market implementation needs the performance redesign before 2034 if staff movement is expected;
- the boys TOC draw did not satisfy the stated rematch protection for Mercy Academy Valley / Georgia Mills even though the 15/16 seeding itself worked;
- the expanded State fields changed the old 16-team pilot's competitive texture substantially, so 2034 should be treated as the second observation of a new format rather than compared casually with the old pilot.

## Association judgment for 2034

The Circuit's first live season gives no competitive reason to reverse it. The most important modeled tradeoff appeared almost exactly where expected: about five of the strongest eight recovery teams survived, while about seven of the eight qualifiers still came from the top half of the 32. At State, the Zonal path remained overwhelmingly dominant. The Circuit therefore added geography and a meaningful title without turning itself into an easier championship route.

The biggest structural change to watch is actually downstream: the former 16-team State classes are no longer playing the same championship event. Their fields expanded, the number of State duals nearly doubled in some classes, and their upset/close-match profile changed immediately. Another season is needed before deciding whether that is the desired permanent shape.

The two concrete implementation defects are narrower. The four-year Sunkist artifact now has an identified cause and should end with 2033. The carousel needs a computational redesign, not a change in its labor-market rules. The TOC protection rule needs one regression case where both qualifier runners-up lost to champions initially drawn into the same half.

The early-participation system and portal require no structural change from this export.

## Method

Primary data: uploaded boys/girls JHSAA research export, internally `2107`, treated as canonical 2033.

Comparison baseline: the uploaded 2032 (`2106`) export and `docs/reports/2032-jhsaa-season-review.md`.

Circuit merit uses the 32 actual Super Regional entrants in each Circuit class, ranked by archived TOSS, and checks how many of the eight Circuit champions came from the best eight and best sixteen. State upset calculations use the archived State field order as seed order. Area coverage uses the association's named `area` field, not the eight modeled geographic sectors. Early-participant varsity appearances are measured from archived varsity line participation. Coaching movement compares coach IDs in 2106 and 2107 head/assistant seat-season rows.
