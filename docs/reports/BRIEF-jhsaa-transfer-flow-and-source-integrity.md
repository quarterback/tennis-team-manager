# BRIEF: JHSAA Transfer Flow — Opportunity, Stranded Talent, and Source Integrity

**Status:** Canonical offseason transfer guidance for future agents  
**Updated after:** 2075 intake + source-repair pass  
**Scope:** JHSAA manual/agent-assisted transfer classes and bulk placement proposals

## Why this exists

The transfer system has repeatedly solved the player-level problem while creating a program-level problem somewhere else.

The recurring mistake is to treat a player as independently movable because that player is buried, underused, or could rank higher at another school. That is not enough.

The governing rule for future transfer work is:

> **Transfers redistribute opportunity and talent. They must not create a new opportunity problem by hollowing out the source program or piling players onto a new bench.**

A move is valid only when all three are true:

1. the player has a legitimate reason to move;
2. the destination gives the player a materially better competitive role;
3. the source can afford to lose the player **after every other move in the wave is accounted for**.

The third condition is the part agents have repeatedly failed to enforce.

This brief updates, rather than replaces, the repo's earlier transfer-market work:

- `docs/AAR-jhsaa-bulk-transfers.md`
- `docs/AAR-analytics-ability-layer-and-scouting-desk.md`
- `docs/reports/BRIEF-jhsaa-opportunity-clearing-market.md`
- `docs/reports/BRIEF-jhsaa-reserve-cohort-mobility.md`
- `docs/reports/REPORT-2040-opportunity-clearing-class.md`

Those documents correctly established that JHSAA has several distinct transfer markets, that opportunity should be searched laterally before pushing players downward, that projections must use next season's roster, and that destination arrivals must be recalculated. This update adds a missing hard constraint:

> **Source integrity is checked dynamically during the transfer run, not audited after the slate is finished.**

## 1. Do not collapse every transfer problem into "buried player"

The analytics work already established that a mismatch is a fact, not a transfer recommendation. There are several different markets and they should remain separate.

### A. Stranded talent — good player on a bad team

This is the market that matters most in the 2075 update.

The scouting desk defines the basic shape as:

- top-decile player in the championship group;
- top three on the source ladder;
- bottom-third program.

The practical interpretation is:

> **A good player is already playing, but the program is bad and is not plausibly becoming competitive around them.**

These players are not "buried." They may be No. 1 on their team. The opportunity is a stronger competitive environment rather than simple playing time.

This is the correct pool for moving good players off bad teams that are not going to get better.

Use more than one bad record if the data is available. Check:

- current and recent program record;
- projected returning core;
- graduation losses;
- next-season projected ladder;
- whether the program is actually rebuilding or simply remaining weak;
- whether the player would have a meaningful role at the destination.

Do **not** turn this into universal poaching from weak programs. It should be a selective market for players whose talent and program trajectory are clearly mismatched.

### B. Buried / blocked surplus — good enough to play, cannot crack the source lineup

Typical annual intake candidates:

- current 10th- or 11th-graders;
- currently deep on the ladder, often No. 16 or lower;
- still projected outside the useful next-season lineup after seniors graduate;
- strong enough to take a real varsity seat elsewhere.

This market exists because deep programs can carry players who are varsity-caliber somewhere else.

But **being buried is necessary, not sufficient**. The source must also have genuine surplus after all proposed departures.

### C. Opportunity clearing — marginal/lower-middle players who need any useful role

This is the market described in `BRIEF-jhsaa-opportunity-clearing-market.md`.

Its rules remain:

- exact-class/lateral search first;
- then one real competitive step downward;
- recalculate after each level;
- do not skip directly to the bottom of the association.

The old "everyone gets a home" rule applies **only to a deliberate opportunity-clearing market**. It is not a rule for every annual transfer intake.

For blocked talent and ordinary offseason redistribution, **leaving a player in place is better than manufacturing a bad move**.

### D. Reserve-cohort mobility — an actual second varsity team

This is not a justification for individually transferring eight players out of one deep roster.

If a program has a true varsity-caliber second unit, that is the separate reserve-cohort market described in `BRIEF-jhsaa-reserve-cohort-mobility.md`.

A cohort move is explicit and intentional. Ordinary individual transfer logic must not accidentally recreate it by strip-mining the same source one player at a time.

### E. Top-end strategic portal

Elite or established varsity players may move for stronger competition, championships, a No. 1 role, coaching, teammates, or other strategic reasons.

These are not opportunity-clearing moves and should not be forced through the same destination logic.

## 2. What the historical runs taught us

### 2039: downward clearing worked but skipped the middle

The earlier experiment created opportunity and thickened the bottom of the association, but too much talent bypassed programs at its own competitive level.

That led to the lateral-first, sequential-clearing philosophy.

### 2040: next-season projection and final-seat checks mattered

The 2040 clearing class established several still-valid rules:

- project against next season's roster;
- later arrivals count;
- rising seniors need a real final varsity position;
- low-end depth at a destination is a penalty, not an invitation;
- program outcomes matter alongside player outcomes.

It also explicitly asked whether 4A–7A remained healthy rather than being hollowed out.

That program-health question must now be enforced at the **source-school level**, not only reviewed by classification after the fact.

### 2072: broad opportunity redistribution can work

The 2072 transfer class remains the useful benchmark for a large opportunity-oriented pass:

- roughly 1,778 players moved;
- median roster position improved from about No. 19 to No. 11;
- median varsity appearances rose from about 1 to 22–23;
- 97–98% of the class made 10+ varsity appearances;
- zero-appearance outcomes effectively disappeared.

The lesson is not "move thousands." The lesson is that a large class can work when the selection pool is disciplined and the destination test is genuinely about playing time.

### 2073: focus-school concentration was a failure

The 2073 sweep moved ordinary surplus players into a relatively small group of designated programs.

The result:

- only 20.8% finished in the top 11;
- only 23.4% made 10+ varsity appearances;
- 28.8% made zero varsity appearances;
- recipient JV winning percentage jumped dramatically.

The intervention moved players from one bench to another and accidentally built reserve-depth superclasses.

**Program archetypes and focus labels are not destination requirements.** Ordinary players should be dispersed statewide to actual open varsity roles.

### 2075: individually defensible moves still strip-mined sources

The first 2075 opportunity pass made 1,852 moves and protected destination congestion reasonably well, but it did not enforce source depletion.

That is the failure this brief is written to prevent.

From that slate:

- 685 gender-programs sent at least one player;
- 188 sent more than three;
- 56 sent more than five;
- 10 sent more than eight;
- the maximum outgoing count from one gender-program was **13**;
- 179 gender-programs finished with net losses of four or more;
- 56 finished with net losses of six or more;
- six finished with net losses of ten or more.

Examples of the damage included:

- Walter-Kenny boys: net −13
- Truman girls: net −12
- West Oberlin boys: net −11
- Port Meridian North boys: net −10
- Jersey City boys: net −10
- Sunset Hills boys: net −10

Every one of those moves could look reasonable when inspected player-by-player. The class was still wrong because the source programs were evaluated only at the beginning of the run.

### The 2075 repair pass: use stranded talent, not more stripping

The owner repaired the first pass with a different flow: good players from bad programs were moved into programs that had been over-depleted.

The repair slate contained 367 players:

- **100%** were top-three players at their source;
- 288 of 367 (**78.5%**) were source No. 1 players;
- median source winning percentage was about **.346**;
- 274 of 367 (**74.7%**) came from programs at .400 or worse;
- the 367 players went to 158 depleted programs;
- the destination's median prior net loss was **5 players**;
- the maximum repaired net loss was **13**.

This is the cleanest demonstration of the stranded-talent market:

> **Good players on bad teams can be a legitimate supply of talent even though they are not buried.**

It also shows why a transfer class needs a global flow view. Had source integrity been respected during the first pass, a large repair wave would not have been necessary.

## 3. The new operating model

Future agents should treat the annual transfer process as a **network flow**, not a list of independent player decisions.

### Step 0 — Build the next-season state first

Before selecting anybody:

1. remove graduating seniors;
2. use the next-season roster build when available, including incoming freshmen and development;
3. compute the projected ladder for every gender-program;
4. record the regular-season lineup need and the program's postseason starter need;
5. classify each program as:
   - deep donor;
   - ordinary/healthy;
   - thin/depleted;
   - bad but rebuilding;
   - bad and likely to remain bad.

Never use today's ladder as the sole basis for a next-season transfer.

### Step 1 — Identify program need before assigning players

Create a destination-needs table across the entire association.

For every program, record:

- projected returning roster size;
- projected top-lineup strength;
- projected lower-lineup weakness;
- current incoming/outgoing count;
- net flow;
- whether the program can offer an actual varsity seat.

Do not begin with a preferred-school list.

The question is:

> **Which programs need players, and what level of player would actually help them?**

### Step 2 — Run stranded talent deliberately

Search for good players on bad programs whose trajectory remains poor.

A useful starting shape is the existing Stranded finder:

- top-decile in group;
- top three on ladder;
- bottom-third program.

Then add next-season context.

Match these players to programs where:

- the competitive environment is materially stronger;
- they still receive a meaningful role;
- the destination has actual need;
- the move does not simply create another overloaded prestige roster.

This pass should be selective.

### Step 3 — Run buried/blocked surplus with source integrity

Now consider players outside the projected useful lineup, especially current sophomores and juniors deep on the ladder.

For each candidate, ask **source first**:

> If this player leaves, is the source still healthy after every move already approved?

If not, the player is no longer surplus and must remain.

A candidate can begin the run at No. 18 and cease to be eligible after three teammates leave. The ladder must be recomputed.

### Step 4 — Run low-end opportunity clearing only if intentionally requested

If the goal includes marginal/low-end placement, use the sequential clearing market.

Do not silently mix it into the blocked-talent pass.

### Step 5 — Handle reserve cohorts separately

If more than a few ordinary moves appear to be coming from the same deep program, stop.

That may indicate a reserve-cohort problem rather than a collection of individual transfers.

Do not approximate a cohort move by independently draining a source.

### Step 6 — Rebuild everything and audit globally

After each wave:

- rebuild projected source ladders;
- rebuild projected destination ladders;
- re-evaluate every proposed transfer's final role;
- recompute source and destination net flow;
- cancel moves that no longer satisfy the source or destination test.

No final paste-ready file should be produced before this audit passes.

## 4. Source integrity is a hard constraint

This is the most important update.

### Default ordinary outflow

For an ordinary individual-transfer wave:

> **One to three outgoing players from a gender-program is normal. Four or more requires an explicit source-depth review.**

This is a review threshold, not a law of nature. A truly enormous roster can spare more. But once the answer is "six, eight, ten, thirteen," the agent must stop treating the moves as independent.

At that point one of two things is true:

1. the source has an explicit reserve-cohort problem; or
2. the source is being strip-mined.

### Protect the source's real competitive structure

Do not use one universal roster number because JHSAA formats differ.

For each source, protect at minimum:

- the full regular-season lineup;
- the full postseason starter requirement for that championship group;
- a meaningful reserve layer beyond those starters.

A 4S/5D program cannot be judged only on whether it still has 11 players. Its championship roster uses more.

The source should still have a functioning varsity and developmental layer after departures.

### Recompute candidate status after every departure

The initial candidate list is not a permanent authorization list.

After every wave:

- rank the source again;
- reclassify who is actually buried;
- stop moving players once the remaining roster no longer represents surplus.

### Watch net loss, not just gross outgoing count

Every audit must include:

- outgoing;
- incoming;
- net loss;
- returners after;
- reserve strength before/after.

A program that sends six and receives four is not the same as one that sends six and receives none.

As a practical warning:

- net loss 1–3: ordinary;
- net loss 4–5: review;
- net loss 6+: presumptive failure unless explicitly justified;
- net loss 10+: strip-mining, not ordinary redistribution.

These are guardrails for agent review, not permanent game rules.

## 5. Destination integrity remains equally important

A transfer is not successful because the destination school is weaker.

The destination must provide a **final** useful role after all incoming players are added.

General expectations:

- ordinary moves should land in the varsity group, preferably in a clear No. 5–9 type role rather than No. 11 by a thread;
- rising seniors need the strongest guarantee because they have one season left;
- a later arrival cannot silently push an earlier transfer back to JV;
- do not move a player from an elite program to a worse program if the player still will not play;
- do not pile ordinary players onto focus/blue-blood/coaching schools merely because the school is designated as interesting;
- spread ordinary transfers across all twelve championship groups wherever real seats exist.

The correct test is always:

> **Is the projected role after the full transfer class materially better than the projected role before it?**

---

## 6. Good players on bad teams are not a mistake in the model

Future agents should not reflexively "protect" every source star merely because that player is No. 1.

A source No. 1 can be exactly the right transfer if:

- the player is genuinely strong relative to the championship group;
- the program is poor;
- the returning program still projects poorly;
- the player has a meaningful competitive pull elsewhere.

This is different from stripping talent out of a healthy or improving program.

The key question is program trajectory.

A good player on a 7–18 team with no credible returning core is a different case from a good player on a young 7–18 team projected to jump the next season.

The transfer system should be willing to recognize the first case.

## 7. Required audit output for every future agent-generated class

Do not return only `pid, school`.

Every transfer run should produce four artifacts.

### A. Paste-ready move file

Exactly:

```text
pid, School
pid, School
...
```

### B. Player-level audit

At minimum:

- player id
- player name
- gender
- grade
- OVR / POT
- market type (`stranded`, `buried`, `clearing`, `cohort`, `top-end`)
- source
- championship group
- current source rank
- projected source rank before moves
- projected source rank at time of departure
- source record / trajectory marker
- varsity/JV appearances
- destination
- destination group
- projected destination rank before arrivals
- projected destination rank after all arrivals

### C. Program-flow audit

For every gender-program touched:

- returners before;
- outgoing;
- incoming;
- net flow;
- returners after;
- protected starter requirement;
- reserve layer before/after;
- count of ordinary moves vs explicit cohort moves.

Sort this table first by worst net loss.

### D. Unplaced / rejected candidates

Include players who looked transferable but were not moved, with a reason:

- source no longer has surplus;
- destination would not provide varsity;
- only available move is downward without benefit;
- source already reached outflow limit;
- player belongs to a different transfer market.

An unplaced player is not evidence of algorithm failure.

## 8. Acceptance tests after the next season

Judge the transfer class on **both player and program outcomes**.

### Player outcomes

Track the exact transferred cohort:

- final ladder position;
- varsity appearances;
- JV appearances;
- 10+ varsity appearance rate;
- zero-appearance rate;
- singles/doubles role;
- whether a rising senior actually played.

### Source outcomes

Measure:

- roster size after departures;
- top-lineup strength;
- postseason-depth strength;
- reserve/JV depth;
- whether the source itself became an opportunity problem;
- whether the source had to be repaired with incoming transfers.

### Destination outcomes

Measure:

- final role of incoming players;
- whether transfers displaced incumbent seniors;
- whether a destination accumulated a new buried layer;
- whether ordinary transfers actually increased varsity participation.

### System outcomes

Measure:

- distribution across all twelve championship groups;
- number of programs with net loss 4+, 6+, 10+;
- number of programs receiving 4+, 6+, 10+ ordinary players;
- change in JV concentration;
- whether the middle of the association remains healthy;
- whether stranded talent from bad programs found stronger competition.

A class can have excellent individual moves and still be systemically bad. The 2075 first pass demonstrated that.

## 9. Pseudocode for future agents

```text
build next-season projected rosters

classify every program:
    deep donor
    healthy
    depleted
    bad/rebuilding
    bad/stagnant

build destination-needs table

moves = []

# 1. Stranded talent: good players on bad teams
for player in stranded_candidates:
    if source_is_bad_and_stays_bad(player.source):
        dest = best_meaningful_competitive_fit(player)
        if dest:
            apply_provisionally(player, dest)
            rebuild(source, dest)

# 2. Buried / blocked surplus
for player in buried_candidates:
    rebuild(player.source)

    if not source_can_spare(player):
        reject("source no longer has surplus")
        continue

    dest = best_actual_playing_time_fit(player)

    if not dest:
        reject("no materially better role")
        continue

    apply_provisionally(player, dest)
    rebuild(source, dest)

# 3. Optional low-end clearing
if owner_requested_clearing_market:
    run_sequential_lateral_first_clearing()

# 4. Cohorts remain separate
if source_outgoing_count_becomes_large:
    stop ordinary flow
    classify as reserve-cohort question
    require explicit review

# 5. Global close
rebuild_all_touched_programs()

for move in moves:
    assert destination_role_is_still_valid(move)
    assert source_integrity_is_still_valid(move)

audit net flow
cancel/reassign failures
rebuild again

emit:
    paste-ready file
    player audit
    program-flow audit
    rejected/unplaced file
```

## 10. Rules future agents should not reinterpret

1. **Do not strip-mine deep programs.**
2. **Do not treat every initially buried player as permanently eligible to leave.**
3. **Do not concentrate ordinary transfers into designated focus programs.**
4. **Do not move a player to another bench.**
5. **Do not optimize for the largest ladder jump.**
6. **Do not force every candidate into a destination unless the owner explicitly requested the low-end clearing market.**
7. **Do not use an ordinary individual-transfer pass to simulate reserve-cohort mobility.**
8. **Do not assume a No. 1 player on a bad team should stay. Stranded talent is a real, separate market.**
9. **Do not judge a class only by transferred-player outcomes. Source and destination health are co-equal outcomes.**
10. **Do not emit a final paste list until the post-wave source/depletion audit passes.**

---

## Bottom line

The JHSAA transfer philosophy is not "move buried players."

It is:

> **Move players whose current program cannot give them the right competitive future, place them where they have a real role, and preserve enough structure at every source that solving one player's problem does not create another program's problem.**

For good players on bad teams, the future may be somewhere stronger.

For buried players on genuinely deep teams, the future may be somewhere they can start.

For marginal players, the answer may be lateral clearing or a step down.

For a whole varsity-caliber reserve unit, the answer may be cohort mobility.

The market should recognize those as different jobs and manage the flow of talent across the association as a system.
