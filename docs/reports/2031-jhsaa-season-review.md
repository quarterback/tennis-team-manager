# JHSAA 2031 Season Retrospective and Association Report

The uploaded research export is internally labeled `2105`; this report treats it as canonical **2031**, following the established retrofit (`2104` = 2030).

## Executive findings

- **Mater Dei boys went 47-0**, won 10B and then won the Tournament of Champions. They were the only undefeated boys varsity program.
- **Harmon Valley girls went 25-0**, won 2A and then won the Tournament of Champions. They were the only undefeated girls varsity program.
- Three schools swept a class across both genders: **Mater Dei (10B), Barley Gap (1A), and Ninemile (Group 3)**.
- The 16-team State classes remained much closer than the larger public fields, but for the first time in this run of seasons they were **not more upset-prone**. Lower seeds won 18.7% of boys matches and 24.0% of girls matches in the five 16-team classes, versus 22.6% and 24.9% in the other seven public classes. Close one-position matches remained much more common in the 16-team fields.
- The new **24-outlet committee is live** in the archive: every active committee group carries 24 voters, a 21-voter lock threshold and a 12-voter fringe threshold.
- The **Markov rating is live** and meaningfully different from the record systems. Its average absolute rank difference from Bradley-Terry was about five places in both genders, while still remaining strongly correlated overall.
- The redesigned **middle-school intake and development system is now visible at association scale**. Grades 7–8 rose from 278 boys / 314 girls in 2030 to **558 boys / 613 girls** in 2031. Of those, 385 boys and 439 girls appeared in varsity competition.
- On eligible 1A/2A/Group 3 rosters, the new intake produces about **2.4 early players per roster**, median 2, 90th percentile 4. Roughly one in ten eligible rosters has none.
- The new accelerator is landing where intended: **19.9% of boys early-season rolls and 20.8% of girls rolls** hit an accelerator; the exceptional tail was **3.9% in both genders**.
- The portal stayed small and functional: **67 boys and 85 girls** moved in the current cohort, and every mover appeared in varsity competition.
- The new calendar model largely did what it was supposed to do: **zero district doubleheaders** in both genders and all undated varsity matches were non-district. The boys display calendar ran August 2–November 18 including TOC; girls ran March 1–June 14.
- One implementation problem remains visible in the export: **10B still contains a two-team Sunkist League** in both genders, despite the new rule that Non-Public leagues are supposed to be redrawn like ordinary classes. That needs investigation before treating the Non-Public league repair as fully validated.

## Tournament of Champions

### Boys

The qualifier winners were Jesuit over Pope Francis, 4-1, and Wells over Tidegate, 3-2.

Mater Dei then won the TOC, beating Trois Ilets 5-0, Chaparral 4-1, Bianchi 4-1 and Canal View **3-2** in the final.

That completed a **47-0** season for Mater Dei.

### Girls

The qualifier winners were Jesuit over Sage Summit, 3-2, and Morgan Park over East Moscow, 3-2.

Harmon Valley won the TOC, beating Ford 4-1, Morgan Park 3-2, Seawall 3-2 and Barley Gap **3-2** in the final.

That completed a **25-0** season for Harmon Valley.

The TOC therefore produced undefeated champions in both genders, but from very different paths: Mater Dei came from 10B and Harmon Valley from 2A.

## State-field behavior

The five 16-team classes remain structurally different, but the signal has narrowed.

| State format | 2030 boys lower-seed | 2031 boys | 2030 girls | 2031 girls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16-team classes | 26.7% | **18.7%** | 28.0% | **24.0%** |
| Other seven public classes | 20.2% | **22.6%** | 26.1% | **24.9%** |

The upset-rate advantage disappeared this season. What persisted was **closeness**:

- boys 16-team classes: 44.0% one-position matches;
- boys other public classes: 26.1%;
- girls 16-team classes: 40.0%;
- girls other public classes: 27.2%.

Across all fourteen State tournaments, lower seeds won 80 of 378 boys duals (21.2%) and 89 of 378 girls duals (23.5%).

The multi-year conclusion should now be stated narrowly: the 16-team fields consistently create **closer** State matches. They do not necessarily create more upsets every season.

## Middle-school intake and development: first live season of the stronger model

This is the clearest system change in 2031.

### Intake

| | 2030 | 2031 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Boys grades 7-8 | 278 | **558** |
| Boys appearing varsity | 190 | **385** |
| Girls grades 7-8 | 314 | **613** |
| Girls appearing varsity | 219 | **439** |

On eligible 1A/2A/Group 3 programs:

| | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean early players / roster | 2.36 | 2.45 |
| Median | 2 | 2 |
| 90th percentile | 4 | 4 |
| Maximum | 6 | 10 |
| Rosters with none | 10.7% | 8.9% |

That is almost exactly the intended shape of the 12-21% intake band: most programs have a couple of middle schoolers, some have four or more, and a small minority have none.

### Development rolls

Across recorded early-season rolls:

- boys accelerator hit rate: **19.9%**
- girls accelerator hit rate: **20.8%**
- boys exceptional hit rate: **3.9%**
- girls exceptional hit rate: **3.9%**

That matches the intended model very closely: roughly one accelerator per five early-player seasons and an exceptional event around one in twenty-five.

The important conceptual result is that early participation is no longer a rare all-or-nothing maturity lottery. The intake is large enough to matter, every early season carries development opportunity, and the accelerator/exception layers create the uneven classes and outliers the redesign was intended to produce.

It is still too early to use 2031 alone as proof of long-term career effects. The useful finding this season is that the **distribution machinery is firing at the intended scale**.

## Ratings and the 24-outlet committee

The new ratings/selection layer is present in the archive rather than merely in code.

- Every computer-ratings table uses **Markov** and no current Colley column.
- Every championship group is connected in this season, so no least-squares system had to be withheld.
- Markov differs from Bradley-Terry by about **5.1 boys rank places** and **5.3 girls rank places** on average; Spearman correlation is about .94. That is the intended profile: related to results, but not another copy of the same ordering.
- Every active committee class archives **24 voters**.
- The lock threshold is **21 of 24**.
- The fringe threshold is **12 of 24**.

The 24-voter panel therefore survived the full season/export path and is now part of the actual historical record.

## Non-Public classes

The awards naming correction is working. Current 10B/11B history rows use:

- All-State
- All-Region
- All-District

There are no current `All-Star` honour lines.

Competitive results were strong:

- Mater Dei swept 10B State and the boys team also won the TOC.
- Mercy Academy Valley won boys 11B.
- High Desert Christian won girls 11B.

However, the league repair is not fully validated by this export.

10B still contains **seven** active districts in each gender, including a **two-team Sunkist League** containing Condotti Vanguard Academy and Romero-Finniski. The intended new rule was to eliminate pods/thin inherited private leagues and redraw Non-Public leagues like every other class.

11B looks healthy: six leagues of 8-11 teams. Most 10B leagues are also normal-sized, but the two-team Sunkist remnant means the repair path needs another look.

## Calendar

The new per-school cosmetic calendar fixed the issue that triggered the redesign.

Observed in the export:

- **zero district doubleheaders** in boys;
- **zero district doubleheaders** in girls;
- zero undated district duals;
- undated varsity matches are ancillary/non-district only.

The displayed spans were:

- boys: August 2 through November 18 including TOC;
- girls: March 1 through June 14 including TOC.

The Road-to-State/State portion ran October 28-November 13 for boys and May 24-June 10 for girls; TOC followed through November 18 and June 14.

The core rule is therefore working: league matches retain their own dates and ancillary matches are what lose dates when the cosmetic window gets crowded.

## What 2031 validates

Working as intended:

- stronger middle-school intake is producing about two to three early players per eligible roster rather than one;
- accelerator and exceptional-roll frequencies match their designed probabilities;
- early players are still converting heavily into varsity appearances;
- Markov is present and produces a genuinely different rating column;
- the 24-outlet committee survives through archival export;
- 10B/11B honors use the normal All-State / All-Region / All-District language;
- district doubleheaders are gone from the cosmetic calendar;
- the portal remains small and strongly tied to varsity opportunity;
- 11B's competitive structure continues to function.

Needs follow-up:

- the two-team 10B Sunkist League survived the Non-Public redraw and contradicts the intended ordinary-league model;
- the 16-team fields should now be described primarily as a **close-match** experiment, not automatically an upset-producing one;
- long-term effects of the new early-development model need multiple cohorts, not one season.

## Association judgment for 2032

The major new systems all fired at scale in 2031. The middle-school redesign is no longer too timid: intake roughly doubled, the roll frequencies landed almost exactly on target, and early participation remained a minority pathway rather than taking over eligible rosters. The Markov/24-voter selection architecture also made it through a full archived season without collapsing back into the old five-member model.

Competitive structure does not need another broad redesign based on this season. The one concrete structural bug visible in the export is the surviving two-team 10B Sunkist League. Fix that repair path, then let the current system accumulate another season of evidence.

## Method

Primary data: uploaded boys/girls JHSAA research export, internally `2105`, treated as canonical 2031.

State upset calculations use the archived State field order as seed order. Middle-school counts use current grade 7-8 players. Varsity appearances are measured from archived varsity line participation. Accelerator and exception rates are read from the exported early-development roll markers.
