# JHSAA Circuit Round — Geographic Recovery Qualification

**Status:** Proposed postseason redesign  
**Classes studied:** 1A, 2A, 3A, 4A, Group 3  
**Study window:** 2100–2106  
**Sample:** 70 class-seasons

## Decision

Replace the current **Super Regional → Semi-State** recovery mechanism with a geographically organized **Circuit Round**.

The Circuit is not an additional postseason layer. It occupies the same two-round place in the Road to State and produces the same **eight qualifiers**.

Zonals remain unchanged.

Everything downstream of the eight Circuit qualifiers remains unchanged.

The Circuit shape is:

**32 eligible non-Zonal teams → 8 geographic Circuits of 4 → 8 Circuit champions**

Each Circuit plays:

- semifinal: 1 vs. 4;
- semifinal: 2 vs. 3;
- Circuit championship.

That is two rounds, **24 duals**, and eight qualifiers.

The current Super Regional/Semi-State mechanism starts from a 24-team recovery population and uses **16 duals**. Expanding the recovery population from 24 to 32 is what adds the extra eight duals.

Geographic bucketing itself adds no games: a single statewide seeded draw of the same 32 teams would also require 24 duals to produce eight survivors.

## The 32 eligible teams

The Circuit should preserve the existing recovery population and extend it.

The field is:

1. the **24 teams already eligible for the current Super Regional/Semi-State recovery mechanism**;
2. the **next eight eligible non-Zonal teams by ATR**.

ATR is the correct selection measure because it is already the JHSAA's postseason ordering rule.

The current rule of record is:

- TOSS remains the association's rating;
- **ATR seeds postseason fields**;
- every existing recovery pool uses ATR;
- a new postseason field should not revert to raw TOSS.

A separate model using the best 32 by rating produced essentially the same Circuit outcomes, but there is no reason to create a second selection rule when the existing postseason already has one.

So the working Circuit field is:

> **current recovery 24 + next 8 by ATR**

## What geography does

Geography does not choose the 32-team field.

The 32 are selected first.

Only then are those teams divided into eight equal groups of four.

This uses the same basic bucketing idiom already used by the JHSAA JV Regions:

1. order the field by **Area**;
2. then **county**;
3. then **city**;
4. then school name;
5. divide the ordered list into eight contiguous groups of four.

Because the field is exactly 32, the geography is simple:

**8 Circuits × 4 teams each**

There are:

- no three-team groups;
- no stocking rules;
- no geographic substitutions;
- no permanent statewide Section boundaries;
- no requirement that a Circuit match one of Jefferson's named Areas.

A Circuit is simply a geographically local tournament bucket drawn from that season's 32 eligible teams.

## The Circuit draw

Once the eight groups of four are set, each Circuit is seeded by ATR.

The bracket is:

- No. 1 vs. No. 4
- No. 2 vs. No. 3
- winners meet for the Circuit championship

The winner becomes the Circuit champion and advances.

Across the classification:

- 16 Circuit semifinals;
- 8 Circuit finals;
- 8 Circuit champions.

Every qualifying place is won on court.

## What the 70-season study found

The model was tested across 1A, 2A, 3A, 4A and Group 3 for seasons 2100 through 2106: **70 class-seasons**.

The central comparison is:

| Model | Class sectors without a State team, of 8 | Seasons with every sector represented | Qualifiers from the best 16 of the 32 | Average class rank of the 8 qualifiers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing two-step recovery: 24 teams, 16 duals | 0.71 | 43% | 7.8 of 8 | 13th |
| One statewide seeded draw of 32: 24 duals | 0.71 | 43% | 7.8 of 8 | 13th |
| **8 Circuits of 4: 24 duals** | **0.30** | **72%** | **7.0 of 8** | **16th** |

The statewide 32-team draw is an important control.

Simply enlarging the recovery population from 24 to 32 does **not** improve geographic representation. The statewide seeded draw produces almost exactly the same geographic outcome as the current recovery system.

The geographic improvement comes from the Circuit bucketing.

## Geographic representation

Under the current recovery system:

- at least one of the eight broad class sectors is missing from State in **57% of class-seasons**;
- an average of **0.71 sectors** is missing.

A statewide seeded draw of 32 does not change that.

Under the Circuit:

- every sector is represented in **72% of seasons**;
- the average number of missing sectors drops to **0.30**.

The Circuit does not guarantee complete representation.

Eight winners can still come from the same broader portions of Jefferson, even when the 32-team field itself reaches almost everywhere.

That is acceptable.

The purpose of the Circuit is to make the recovery route substantially more regional, not to impose geographic quotas on State qualification.

## The 32 already cover almost all of the map

The Circuit does not need a stocking or guarantee rule because the selected field already reaches nearly every part of the classification.

Across the study, only **0.06 of eight broad class sectors per season**, on average, had no team among the 32 Circuit entrants.

So the geographic problem is not getting teams from different parts of Jefferson into the recovery field.

They are already there.

The issue is that a statewide seeded recovery draw can eliminate those teams against opponents from anywhere, allowing the eight winners to concentrate geographically.

The Circuit changes the matchups so that more of the State field survives from different parts of the map.

## Competitive cost

The Circuit does trade some pure seed efficiency for geography.

Looking at the strongest teams among the 32:

### Best eight by rating

- existing two-step recovery: **6.1 of 8** qualify;
- statewide seeded draw of 32: **6.0 of 8**;
- Circuit: **4.6 of 8**.

### Best sixteen by rating

- existing two-step recovery: **7.8 of the eight qualifiers** come from the best 16;
- statewide seeded draw of 32: **7.8**;
- Circuit: **7.0**.

### Average class rank

The average qualifier moves from approximately:

- **13th in the class** under the statewide systems;
- to **16th** under the Circuit.

The studied classes average roughly 67 programs.

So geographic bucketing costs about three positions of average class rank and causes roughly one additional qualifier to come from outside the top half of the Circuit field.

That is the actual competitive price.

## The extra eight duals are not the price of geography

This distinction matters.

The existing recovery pool has 24 teams and uses 16 duals.

Any system that expands that pool to 32 teams and reduces it to eight qualifiers needs **24 duals**.

That is true whether the 32 are:

- placed into one statewide seeded draw; or
- divided into eight geographic Circuits.

Therefore:

> **the additional eight duals come from expanding the recovery field from 24 to 32, not from making the draw geographic.**

The geographic decision changes the pairings and therefore the distribution of winners. It does not change the match count once the 32-team field has been chosen.

## Geographic compactness

The eight-by-four model creates meaningfully local brackets.

Across the study, the median geographic spread within a Circuit was approximately **153 miles**.

For a state the size of Jefferson, that is a substantial localization of what is currently a statewide recovery process.

The Circuit therefore creates a recognizable regional championship without requiring permanent geographic administration.

## Why no hard geographic guarantee

A hard-guarantee variant was also modeled.

It forced every broad geographic sector to have a route that could produce a State team and reached 100% representation.

But it produced essentially no improvement in competitive quality over the ordinary Circuit.

The ordinary Circuit already:

- places almost every broad sector into the 32-team field;
- increases complete State representation from 43% to 72%;
- does so without substituting teams for geographic reasons.

The JHSAA therefore does not need a hard stocking rule.

The more restrained principle is:

> select the recovery field competitively, organize it geographically, and let the matches determine which regions ultimately survive.

## Why Zonals stay untouched

Zonals remain the strongest automatic State path and are highly predictive.

There is no reason to weaken them to manufacture geographic balance.

The eight Zonal champions are removed before the Circuit field is constructed.

The architecture is:

> **Zonals identify the eight automatic qualifiers. The Circuit geographically organizes the second route.**

A strong geographic area can still send several Zonal champions.

The Circuit does not cap or penalize that success.

## Relationship to the Road to State

The current sequence contains:

**Areas → Sectionals → Wards → Regionals → Zonals → Super Regionals → Semi-State → downstream postseason**

The proposed sequence becomes:

**Areas → Sectionals → Wards → Regionals → Zonals → Circuit → downstream postseason**

The Circuit replaces only **Super Regionals and Semi-State**.

It does not replace or remove:

- Sectionals;
- Wards;
- Regionals;
- Zonals;
- Divisionals;
- Semi-Conference;
- Conference;
- State Specials;
- State.

Whatever the current system does with the eight Super Regional/Semi-State outputs, it should do with the eight Circuit champions instead.

## Why four-team Circuits

Four teams fit the purpose exactly.

Each Circuit champion must win:

1. a semifinal;
2. a final.

There are no byes.

A Circuit championship therefore represents a real two-round regional tournament.

And because eight four-team Circuits consume exactly 32 teams, no special-case field balancing is required.

## Naming

Use **Circuit**, not Section.

The JHSAA already has Sectionals earlier in the Road to State.

The eight winners are Circuit champions.

The tournament units can be numbered within the classification:

- Circuit I
- Circuit II
- …
- Circuit VIII

The names do not imply permanent geography. As with the JV Region buckets, the geography is an organizing device for that tournament field.

## Operating rule

The proposed rule is:

> The Super Regional and Semi-State stages shall be replaced by the Circuit stage. The Circuit field shall consist of the 24 teams eligible for the existing recovery mechanism plus the next eight eligible non-Zonal teams by ATR. The resulting 32-team field shall be ordered geographically by Area, county, city and school and divided into eight four-team Circuits. Teams within each Circuit shall be seeded by ATR. Each Circuit shall play semifinals and a championship dual, and the eight Circuit champions shall advance into the same qualifying positions previously produced by Super Regionals and Semi-State. Zonal champions and all postseason stages before and after the replaced stages shall remain unchanged.

In short:

**select 32 using the existing postseason hierarchy and ATR; bucket them geographically; play eight four-team championships; send the eight winners forward.**
