# JHSAA 2028 Season Retrospective and Association Report

2028 is the first season played under the new salt after the historical retrofit. The research export retains the former internal season label `2102`; this report uses canonical 2028 throughout. The pinned archive before it is the permanent JHSAA past.

## Executive findings

- State remained ordered at the title level but not throughout the bracket. Lower seeds won 73 of 332 boys State duals (22.0%) and 80 of 332 girls duals (24.1%); 18 of 24 champions were top-four seeds.
- The five 16-team classes were much more volatile. Lower seeds won 29.3% of boys and 38.7% of girls State duals in 4A, 3A, 2A, 1A and Group 3, versus 19.8% in the other seven classes. One-position results were 45.3% boys and 48.0% girls in those five classes.
- Semi-State remained the meaningful recovery line. Its teams produced 22 boys and 22 girls quarterfinalists, eight boys and nine girls semifinalists, two boys and four girls finalists, and two girls champions. Everything below Semi-State combined for four quarterfinalists, one semifinalist, no finalist and no title.
- The 2027 reclassification immediately affected State: six boys champions and six girls champions had changed classes; 40 moved boys programs and 41 moved girls programs reached at least a quarterfinal.
- The first rising-freshman portal cohort persisted. Of 1,147 players moved before 2027, all 548 boys and 598 of 599 girls remained at the destination in 2028; 1,129 played varsity.
- The 2028 portal moved another 880 rising freshmen. All 428 boys and 451 of 452 girls played varsity, with a median 21 varsity duals.
- The first season with no grade restriction in JV individual events produced four full 128-entry State draws containing players from seventh through twelfth grade.
- Active rosters contained 515 seventh/eighth-graders. 353 played varsity, and five all-state doubles selections included a middle-school player.
- Hazel Country Day won both Tournament of Champions titles; its boys finished 33-0 and girls 32-1.

## Reclassification

The 2027 cycle moved 345 schools. Twelve 2028 champions had just changed classes.

Boys: Hazel Country Day 7A→8A; Glen Park 8A→7A; Barclay 3A→4A; Villard 2A→3A; Mt Jacqueline 2A→1A; Elgin Group 1→Group 2.

Girls: Hazel Country Day 7A→8A; Canyonlands 9A→7A; California Canyons 3A→4A; Mt Jacqueline 2A→1A; Jackson Hole Group 2→Group 1; Mirage Crossing Group 1→Group 2.

Mt Jacqueline illustrates why school-level reclassification should not be read as a deterministic competitive result: its boys won as the No. 1 seed while its girls won from the Semi-State line at No. 9.

## Historical results and program movement

Perry Green boys won their first title in the available archive. Frontier boys ended a 21-season drought, Klickitat 12 and Elgin nine.

The girls produced the longest waits. Ninemile won Group 3 for the first time since **1958**, a 70-season drought. Cheney had not won since 1992; Christ the King since 1998; Jackson Hole since 2008; Mirage Crossing since 2014.

Important 2027→28 gains included Borough Beach boys 5-21→18-10 and State; Portola-Caverly 10-20→20-9 and an octofinal; Wyoming Valley 10-18→21-9 and an octofinal; Carter girls 5-18→21-9 and State; Doyleville Union 14-16→22-5 and an octofinal; Fort Lassiter 12-12→25-5 and a quarterfinal.

Large reversals included Delbarton boys 18-11→5-18, Kernwood County 21-5→12-16, Marlow girls 18-7→3-20, Clinton 21-5→8-18 and Singleton 30-4→13-14.

## Tournament of Champions

Hazel Country Day swept the TOCs. The boys completed a 33-0 season by beating Gottschalk-Herman in the final; the girls finished 32-1 and beat Jackson Hole. The TOC is reported as its own cross-classification championship, not as a universal ranking that retroactively supersedes the class tournaments.

The 2028 portal moved 428 boys and 452 girls. Geography remained local: 503 moves stayed in-county, 150 elsewhere in the same area and 227 in neighboring areas. The median projected destination seat was 7.5 boys and 8 girls. Every boys mover and all but one girls mover appeared in varsity competition.

The portal is therefore clearing actual playing opportunities, not merely changing roster addresses.

## Early participation

The reduced intake produced 240 boys and 275 girls currently in grades 7–8. Of them, 172 boys and 181 girls appeared in varsity competition.

Five all-state doubles selections included a middle-schooler: Samuel Hanchett (New Casper, 7th), Emmylou Ketchum (Banfield Day, 8th), Rebecca Ausberry (Fogline, 8th), Kayla Rodrigues (Silton Ridge, 8th) and Anna Sanchez (Aspen Hollow, 8th).

The smaller intake has not made early participation a JV-only track.

## JV individual championships: first fully grade-open season

The repo's rule is now **JV only, any grade**: eligibility is determined by roster position and the event rank guard, not age. All four State events filled 128-entry draws.

The age spread is the useful finding. Seventh- and eighth-graders can now qualify, but the event did not become a youth tournament. Mater Dei senior Seigo Oshima won boys JV Singles; sophomore Eva Redondo won girls JV Singles. JV team State remains a separate program competition and should not be combined with these classless individual-event counts.

## Player layer

Player of the Year voting behaved as designed. Twenty-three awards went to first-singles players; Fruitvale senior **Emelia Fonseca** won girls 6A from second singles at 34-0. That is an intended consequence of evaluating season value rather than mechanically awarding No. 1 singles.

Individual State remained more volatile than team titles. First-singles champions included No. 8 Shawn Callaway (North San Francisco, boys 6A), No. 7 Victoria Broach (Providence Academy, girls 7A), No. 7 Adalyn Portillo (Larchmont Ridge, girls 9A), No. 6 Kent Coyle (Benedetti, boys 2A) and No. 6 Emma Griffin (Pine Eagle, girls Group 2).

Deeper-flight surprises included New Hope Christian mixed-doubles champions from the No. 17 seed in both genders and No. 10 doubles champions at Lyle (boys Group 3) and Meridian (girls 3A).

The returning first-singles champion group for 2029 includes Jedidiah Belcher and Kellan Sherman on the boys side, and Kayla Phillips, Abbie Cruz and Mckenna Wine on the girls side. Underclass Players of the Year also include 9A sophomore Savannah Pettit and 8A junior Chloe Boone.

## What 2028 validates

Observed:
- the 16-team classes were more upset-prone and closer in 2028;
- Semi-State remained the meaningful recovery line;
- reclassification immediately changed the State map;
- the first portal cohort retained real varsity roles;
- the second portal cohort converted projected seats into actual varsity work;
- the smaller middle-school intake still produced varsity and all-state players;
- removing the JV grade rule produced mixed-age 128-entry fields.

Not established:
- field size alone did not necessarily cause the 16-team-class differences;
- a new salt changes realized history, not the competitive rules;
- same-season `bloom_grades` cannot be used to declare maturity success or failure because events affect subsequent builds;
- portal playing-time conversion is better established than long-term developmental effect.

## 2029 measures

Repeat the 16-team/full-field comparison; follow the 2028 portal cohort one year later; measure JV State entrants moving into varsity roles; evaluate maturity from the next archived build; and track whether the immediate effects of the 2027 reclassification persist.

## Method

Source: the 2028 boys-and-girls research export after the historical retrofit. Its internal directory remains `2102`; historical program years were translated to the canonical backdated chronology. The reclassification ledger already uses canonical years and was not shifted again.

Programs were joined on `program_id`, players on `player_id`, and competitive comparisons use `championship_group`. Player ratings are not surfaced.

State routes use played seed bands: 1–8 Zonal, 9–16 Semi-State, 17–24 Divisional, 25–32 State Specials, 33+ committee/other. JV age counts come from the archived `ALL` JVS/JVD draws; doubles counts are athlete-seats.

Methodology follows `docs/writing-guide-update.md`, the repository's season-review and longitudinal-report conventions, `docs/AAR-jhsaa-early-participation.md`, `docs/AAR-jhsaa-jv-128-qualifying.md`, `docs/AAR-jhsaa-reclassification.md`, and `docs/reports/REPORT-jhsaa-zonal-champions-and-the-recovery-road.md`.
