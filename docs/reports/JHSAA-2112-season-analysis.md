# JHSAA 2112 — A realignment season that changed the competitive map

*Expanded internal season analysis · World 2038 · both genders · October 2026*

This is a companion analysis to [the complete 2112 annual review](../2112-jhsaa-season-review.md). The annual review retains the full championship tables, Player of the Year ledger, long-wait records, class notes and alternate-outcome forks. This report places those results alongside the two-gender 2038 research export, the coaches who were responsible for the competing programs, and the first research baseline for the newly implemented development system. The methods and attribution limits are documented in [2112 coaching and development baseline](JHSAA-2112-coaching-and-development-baseline.md).

**The season's central story:** 2112 was the first State championship season after a large-scale reclassification and the new 10B/11B private-school split. The transfer class, district lineup formats, qualification routes and schools' recent competitive histories all met in one season. One team won its first State title from a seventh seed through recovery; another moved up two classes and repeated; two schools fought over the Tournament of Champions, and the same private school produced an undefeated girls' season. The outcomes make more sense when read as a changing association rather than as a list of unrelated title winners.

## The association changed underneath the teams

**318 boys and 332 girls programs — 650 program/gender placements — changed classification** in the four-year realignment. That is about a third of the competing association. There were **329 moves up and 321 moves down** across the two genders. Movement matters here because high-school team records and tournament access depend on which opponents a program is newly required to play.

The win-rate shifts are close to symmetric:

| Realignment direction | Boys: prior → 2112 | Girls: prior → 2112 |
|---|---|---|
| Moved up | .602 → .504 | .610 → .527 |
| Moved down | .321 → .410 | .332 → .408 |

A broad average shift of roughly ten winning-percentage points is not a diagnosis of coaching quality. In the season the groups were redrawn, thousands of teams faced a different competitive field. A program's improvement, decline and coefficient ranking require its old and new classification alongside the results. The underlying program-history table carries stable program_id values even when the displayed name changes.

The examples make the point more sharply than the aggregate. **Chickasaw's boys** moved from Group 2 to Group 1 and went from **21–9 to 4–19**; **Chickasaw's girls** went from **25–8 to 5–18**. Conversely, **Trois Ilets** dropped four classes, from 9A to 5A: the boys moved from **1–25 to 16–14**, the girls from **0–23 to 17–17**. Neither pair can be explained responsibly as just a one-year coaching gain or collapse.

The private split also became a real competitive ladder: **15 private schools moved from 11B into 10B and 11 moved from 10B into 11B**, consistently between genders. Basalt, Peregrine and Washington San Cordero left the private classifications for public placement in **4A, 3A and 1A**. Basalt's boys went 15–15 and reached the State Specials; both Peregrine programs reached the round of 32. These are not marginal identity changes: the championship group and the comparison population actually changed.

### Eight title results from seven named moving schools

Seven named schools earned a State title after moving classification, with **eight team titles** because Barley Gap won both genders. The source annual review also describes this as seven champions; the gender-level title table makes the eight outcome count explicit.

**Barley Gap** fell from 3A into 1A and swept: boys **26–1**, girls **27–3**. **Brackwood Pass's girls** went the other direction, from 1A to 3A, and won at **37–1**. **Bush's boys** moved 7A → 8A and won; **Penobscot's boys** moved 6A → 7A and won a third straight title. **Mt Jacqueline's boys** moved 4A → 2A, **Mabry Township's boys** Group 2 → Group 3, and **Elk Bluff West's girls** Group 1 → Group 3, all winning their new groups. The distinctions matter: winning after moving up is different evidence from winning after moving down, even when both end with a State trophy.

Mabry Township deserves particular notice. Its boys went **24–10**, entered Group 3 as the **ninth seed** and won their first State title. That is a genuine tournament run from outside the expected top group rather than a typical dominant top-seed season.

## The Tournament of Champions separated two stories

**Canal View's boys** won the Tournament of Champions, becoming the first public winner since Chaparral in 2108. They first won Group 1 **6–3 over Kishwaukee**, a team that had moved up from Group 2, then beat Mabry Township **4–1**, Barley Gap **5–0**, Chaparral **3–2** and Romero-Finniski **3–2** in the final. Romero-Finniski arrived having won three consecutive **3–2** duals. Canal View's season was **38–3** and the champion faced substantially different opponents from its own State group along the way.

**Romero-Finniski's girls** completed a **33–0** season and won their second consecutive TOC. Their 11B State final was a **7–0** win over The Greaves School. They then beat Marigny **4–1** and New Casper, Seawall and Mercy Academy Valley **3–2** each. Three consecutive one-flight finishes are a reminder that an unbeaten record can still contain very narrow elimination margins.

**Mercy Academy Valley** is the crucial other half of the girls' story. The 10B runner-up reached the TOC through the 9A/10B qualifier, beat Canal View **3–2**, Saltbush **4–1** and Gagarin **3–2**, and reached the final. This is why treating the TOC merely as a replay of State champions misses the actual competitive mechanism.

The qualifier results reflected the new public/private pairings: boys **Chaparral 3–2 over Walter-Kenny** (9A/10B) and **Mendoza 3–2 over Condotti Vanguard Academy** (8A/11B); girls **Mercy Academy Valley 3–2 over Glen Park** (9A/10B) and **Marigny 3–2 over The Greaves School** (8A/11B). Since the qualifier began, **six of its 40 winners** have won the TOC and ten more have reached a semifinal or final. Over the two seasons using the public/private pairing, public runners-up have won **five of eight** qualifying duals. The format opens a real second path; it does not guarantee an advantage to either school type.

The annual review's alternate futures show how narrow the tournament was: one flipped flight in the boys' final gives Romero-Finniski its first boys TOC, and one flipped flight in the girls' final gives Mercy Academy Valley the title while ending Romero-Finniski's perfect season. These are **explicit counterfactuals**, not changes to the recorded result.

## First titles and championships after long waits

The championship archive begins in **2027**; every "first" or "long wait" here means **within that archive**, not necessarily in the fictional school's entire earlier history.

The season produced **six first State championships**: **Millworks boys (4A)**, **Windward boys (3A)**, **Mabry Township boys (Group 3)**, **Santa Cruz del Norte girls (8A)**, **Severn girls (7A)** and **New Casper girls (2A)**.

**Millworks** is the most instructive route. It did not win every round on the main road. The seventh-seeded team lost its Zonal and Super Regional, entered through the Divisionals, and defeated **Tunnelton 3–2** in the 4A final. Tunnelton was the year's lone Circuit champion to reach a State final. One reversed flight in the final would have made it a Circuit championship instead of Millworks' first recorded State title.

**Windward's boys** won 3A **4–1 over Estuary Bay**, finishing **30–3**. **Santa Cruz del Norte's girls**, the **fifth seed**, won 8A **7–2 over Marigny**. **Severn's girls** won 7A **5–4 over Boggs**, ending a **70-season wait for a girls' State final** and earning their first archive title. **New Casper's girls** won 2A **3–2 over Puerto Alma**. These paths were neither equal in bracket difficulty nor identical in pre-2112 program history.

Notable waits elsewhere ended without a title. **Mount Ruth's boys** reached the 1A semifinal after **86 seasons** without one in the archive. **Welsh Plains' boys** reached a 5A semifinal after **72**; **Topeka's boys** reached a Group 2 semifinal after **35**. Girls **Mercy Academy Valley** reached its first archive final in **69 seasons**, and **Grand Fond** reached the 5A final for the first time in **56** — then won it **6–5 over top-seeded Brightwater**. For these programs, qualifying for a new stage is a meaningful outcome independently of winning the final dual.

**Fourteen of the 28 classification finals** were decided by a single dual point. The source review also finds **eight winners seeded outside the top three**. That level of bracket uncertainty must be allowed when assessing whether a strong coaching staff "caused" a title.

## The highest classes: dominance and its limits

**L.B. Johnson's boys** won a third consecutive 9A title, finishing **31–1** and defeating Chaparral **7–2**. This was the eighth season of the association's 9A boys boost. **Canyonlands**, newly moved up from 8A, went **39–1** and took the top seed but lost a semifinal. A virtually perfect team could still miss the title. Two of the four semifinalists belonged to the boost cohort; all 14 boosted programs remained in 9A after realignment.

**Seawall's girls** repeated in 9A **9–0 over Glen Park**, the first 9A final shutout in ten years of exported seasons. Its broader girls program also sent several players into the season's highest win totals. These results support Seawall's reputation as a competitive power; they do not by themselves quantify the incremental teaching value of any assistant.

The 10B and 11B finals showed no simple public/private hierarchy. **Chaminade's boys** won 10B **5–4 over Walter-Kenny**, while **Romero-Finniski's boys** won a fourth consecutive 11B title **6–1 over Condotti Vanguard Academy**. **Evans Larsen Day's girls** won 10B **5–4 over Mercy Academy Valley**, and Romero-Finniski completed its girls' undefeated season in 11B. The State and TOC paths therefore tell a richer story than the number of titles by school type.

## The Circuit's sixth run

The Circuit remained a qualification and competitive proving ground, even in a season when none of its **144 champions** won a State title. Its outcomes in 2112 were **one finalist, six semifinalists, 22 quarterfinalists, 62 round-of-16 finishes and 53 first-round-or-earlier exits**. Tunnelton's 4A final was the best of those runs.

For comparison, Circuit champions had produced at least one State title in each of 2108–2111, including three in 2110. The 2112 zero is unusual, but one tournament year is not evidence that the qualification route has stopped working.

Realignment also redrew the Circuit's geography. The median Circuit covered **183 miles**, up from **164**; **45 of the 144** sat within a single named area, down from 52. The share of class seasons where all eight geographical sectors placed at least one team into State was **72%**, down from **100%** in 2111. Geography is an evaluation lens here, not an alternative rule for naming a State champion. Long routes and shifting membership have operational implications for schools irrespective of the eventual bracket winner.

## District lineup changes had participation consequences

This was the second season in the class-specific district-dual formats. Seniors entered 2112 from cohorts partly built under older class placements; realignment changed many programs' available formats at precisely the wrong time for easy year-over-year participation comparisons.

The share of seniors with **no varsity appearance** rose to **12.6% — 1,764 of 14,034 seniors** — from **11.1%** in 2111. The no-class 2110 season had **14.1%**, and the 2109 class-format season **10.7%**. Across the two most recent seasons, overall varsity participation remained around **78% of rostered players**. Treat the senior sitting measure as a roster/format signal as well as a possible coaching-selection signal; this export cannot assign the whole change to a new coach or rule.

The 2112 transfer intake placed **5,574 of 5,633** listed students at their selected schools, a reported **99%** intake. That matters for later career interpretation: transferred students' playing opportunities and development histories must follow the same player identity rather than looking like new students arriving from nowhere.

The simulation also used substantially more split-squad duals: **941 boys' duals and 1,052 girls' duals**, against **615 and 620** the preceding year. These are participation and fixture changes that may affect a student's exposure without proving anything yet about skill acquisition.

## Players and individual accomplishments

The 2112 Player of the Year ledger includes two 9A boys winners from Canyonlands: **Anthony Schumacher** and **Pedro Freeze**, both juniors at **30–3**. **Madisyn Goss of Oakhaven**, a senior at **30–4**, won the girls' 9A honor. At Group 1, **Kareem Scott of Canal View** finished **35–5**, and **Pari Kakar**, also Canal View, **33–8**. **Mason Meola of Romero-Finniski** won 11B boys at **41–1**; **Amari Waenngoen of Blue Mountain Christian** won the girls' 11B honor at **29–3**.

Individual winning streaks were not confined to the biggest programs. **Manuel De Sousa**, a Sherwood Bench junior, went **34–0**; **Riyad Bouallegue**, a Dufur freshman, **32–0**; **Caleb Ryu**, an L.B. Johnson sophomore, **31–0**. **Mya Kirk**, a Canal View junior, went **38–0**. Canal View's senior **Gracie Aguilar** and Romero-Finniski's junior **Naomi Danielson** were **33–0**.

These are meaningful records for the in-world season. They should be compared over time using player_id, their actual singles/doubles flight histories, opponents, honors and State results. **Do not import backend OVR or POT ratings into this in-world sports narrative.** The separate research report treats those backend values only as calibration inputs.

## The geographic, municipal and expansion-program experiment

The eleven selected large-city boys programs won about **43%** of their matches, up from 39%, and four made State; the eleven girls programs won about **33%**, down from 41%, and three made State. **Belyakov Vo-Tech's boys** improved from 14–15 to **20–12**; **Alameda's boys** improved from 8–15 to **17–13**; **Chisholm's girls** improved from 10–17 to **17–11**. Conversely **Vista Terrace's girls** dropped from 19–14 to **7–16**. Some of these programs also moved up in classification, so the direction of the percentage change alone is insufficient to judge the experiment.

The older expansion cohort is ten seasons into its life. Its overall win rates have settled near even: **.506 boys and .492 girls** in 2112, compared with **.731 and .729** in 2103. Programs in that cohort once won several State titles in the first three seasons; neither gender produced one in 2112. The original intervention is best assessed across the whole decade, with changing class placement and the graduation of original cohorts, rather than by labeling the absence of this year's title a failure.

The private programs added in 2110 are in their third season. Their boys' aggregate win rate was **.485** with eight State teams; their girls' **.526** with twelve State teams. **The Greaves School's girls** reached the 11B final at **28–2**. This newer program pool is already capable of meaningful postseason performance, but the time horizon is short.

## Early participants and the old development track

There were **548 seventh-graders and 1,079 eighth-graders** on JHSAA rosters, **1,627** in all. Of those, **58%** of the seventh-graders and **64%** of the eighth-graders appeared in varsity duals, winning about **50% and 49%** of their flights respectively. **1,047 of 14,034 seniors** had played before ninth grade.

The earlier maturity/early-POT mechanism remains active and separate from the new attribute-teaching model. The supplied season review reports a **15.9% median** early-player potential boost as a share of base ceiling, with a substantial upper tail: **28.3%** received at least 25%, and **4.0%** at least 50%. Early starters sat about **0.73 standard deviations** ahead of classmates, compared with 0.68 previously.

The newly implemented coach-stock era is now appearing in the research export, but only **44 students** have positive recorded coached skill gains. Most new-profile students entered in 2112 or have not yet had a qualifying archived coaching year. The complete research finding — including which teachers made the offers and which attributes moved — belongs in [the companion bond/annuity/development report](JHSAA-2112-coaching-and-development-baseline.md). It would be premature to attribute any of the 2112 championship changes to this new instruction system.

## The coaches make the year more legible

The export allows the season narrative to name the head coaches, while keeping **past bond** separate from **this season's result**.

**Mia Huang** entered Canal View boys' TOC season at **bond 1.1071**, after **16 completed head seasons** at the program. Huang qualified for both annuity and veteran developer protection. **Ellie Hasse** entered Romero-Finniski girls' undefeated TOC season with **1.1071**, only **four completed head seasons** and no annuity. Those programs reached the same championship stage through coaches with very different career profiles.

**Owen Gibson** at Millworks entered 2112 at **0.9833**, then won the program's first recorded State title. **Kessler Church** at Mabry Township entered at **1.0000** in a first head season and won Group 3 from the ninth seed. **Mia Orosco** at Barley Gap girls entered at **0.9788**, age 34, and won 1A. These coaches are direct evidence that *the entering bond isn't a score handed out for the just-completed State bracket*. Their achievements should change the subsequent longitudinal record.

The reverse cases are equally important. **Lilly Flores** at Chickasaw girls entered with **bond 1.0967**, annuity and veteran protection, even though the newly promoted team went **5–18**; **Maxwell Salama** at Southern Pacific Tech boys entered at **1.1031** while the team finished **0–21**. An earned reputation can outlast a losing year. That is a design choice, not proof of this year's technical instruction.

The export counts **1,962 current head coaches**, **489 annuity-eligible**, and **655 protected veteran development heads**. Those labels should be preserved for longitudinal comparison, not used as substitutes for watching what a roster actually learned. The distinction is analyzed in detail in the linked [first coaching baseline](JHSAA-2112-coaching-and-development-baseline.md).

## What 2112 changed for 2113

The annual review's **returning-strength** calculation uses matches and flights won by players who are not seniors; it is evidence about the actual competitive roster that comes back next year. Some of its leading prospects are already familiar champions: **Chaparral, Banneker and L.B. Johnson** lead 9A boys; **Seawall, Oakhaven and Banneker** lead 9A girls. **Canal View, Kishwaukee and Dawkins** lead Group 1 boys; **Canal View, Ocean Park and Dawkins** lead Group 1 girls. **Mercy Academy Valley, Walter-Kenny and Ashbury Latin** lead 10B girls.

The next experiment is more demanding. Teams will play a complete season under the revised classes, more first-time coaching-stock players will begin to register instruction from a completed prior season, and the **2112 title/improvement data will enter 2113 head-coach bond calculations**. We should ask whether programs such as Millworks, Mabry Township, Brackwood Pass, Chickasaw and Trois Ilets maintain their trajectories once they have lived with the new class and roster constraints longer.

Do not ask a one-year report to decide whether every realignment was correct, whether a veteran protected teacher is unusually good at instruction, or whether an elite staff can create a State finalist from a ninth-grade beginner. Those are precisely the reasons to keep the exportable longitudinal panel.

## End-of-season judgment

The 2112 championship design still produces surprises: a seventh-seeded first champion, a ninth-seeded first champion, newly promoted repeat winners, long-awaited finals and one-flight TOC decisions. The post-realignment averages show substantial competition-level shifts in both directions. The Circuit still sends competitive teams into State even without producing this year's champions, and the qualifier still creates a meaningful public/private path.

The coaching system introduces a new, separate question beyond trophies: **which school actually acquires new player capability?** The bond and annuity measure the coach's competitive standing and accumulated service. Individual teaching portfolios and the player-development ledger measure the offered and realized skill instruction. This archive is the beginning of that investigation, not the end.
