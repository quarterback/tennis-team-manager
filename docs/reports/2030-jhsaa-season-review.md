# JHSAA 2030 Season Retrospective and Association Report

The uploaded research export is internally labeled `2104`; this report treats it as canonical **2030**, following the repo retrofit (`2103` = 2029).

## Executive findings

- **Both girls TOC Qualifier winners reached the final.** Pacific Gate, the 9A State runner-up, beat Mater Dei, the 10B State runner-up, 3-2 for the TOC title. Neither TOC finalist was a State champion.
- **Orchard Union boys went 35-0**, won 8A and the TOC, and were the only undefeated varsity program in either gender.
- Westfield Friends swept 10B. The girls State final over Mater Dei was later reversed under the TOC's 1S/4D card.
- The first 11B State tournament at **3S/4D** ran cleanly: every State dual used seven flights.
- The rising-freshman portal stabilized at **69 moves in each gender**. Every mover appeared in varsity competition.
- Early participation remained small: 278 boys and 314 girls in grades 7-8; 190 boys and 219 girls appeared in varsity competition.
- The **24-date participation rule is not holding exactly as written**. Counting regular, early-window and showcase dates, 435 boys and 401 girls exceeded 24. Almost all were one date over, but one boys player reached 43 dates and one girls player 37.

## Tournament of Champions: the qualifier changed the title

The boys qualifiers were both 3-2: Condotti Vanguard Academy over Mercy Academy Valley and Brookmont over Wells. Both lost in the octofinals. Orchard Union completed its 35-0 season by beating Monongahela 3-2 in the TOC final.

The girls tournament was the important one. Mater Dei, the 10B runner-up, beat Calderwood 3-2. Pacific Gate, the 9A runner-up, beat Orellana Central 4-1. Both reached the final.

- Westfield Friends beat Mater Dei **5-4** for 10B State; Mater Dei beat Westfield Friends **3-2** in the TOC quarterfinal.
- Seawall beat Pacific Gate **6-3** for 9A State; Pacific Gate beat Seawall **3-2** in the TOC semifinal.
- Pacific Gate beat Mater Dei **3-2** for the TOC championship.

In the second expanded TOC, the girls final contained **no State champion**. The finalist qualifiers are not decorative. State and TOC remain separate accomplishments.

## Private championships and 11B format

Westfield Friends swept 10B. Gottschalk-Herman won boys 11B and Fletcher-Garrison Hall won girls 11B. Lower seeds won 34.8% of boys matches in both private classes, 30.4% of girls 10B matches and 21.7% of girls 11B matches.

2030 was the first 11B State at **3S/4D** rather than 1S/4D. Every 11B State dual totaled seven flights; both finals were 4-3. The TOC remained 1S/4D.

The thin-pod issue remains: **Sunkist League still has only two active 10B tennis programs in each gender**, while the other private pods generally have seven to ten.

## Third year of the 16-team State fields

| State format | 2029 boys lower-seed | 2030 boys | 2029 girls | 2030 girls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16-team classes | 33.3% | **26.7%** | 29.3% | **28.0%** |
| Other seven public classes | 21.8% | **20.2%** | 22.2% | **26.1%** |

The more persistent signal is closeness. One-position results were **46.7% of boys and 41.3% of girls matches** in the 16-team classes, versus **25.3% and 23.7%** in the other seven public classes.

Across all fourteen State tournaments, lower seeds won 88 of 378 boys duals (23.3%) and 100 of 378 girls duals (26.5%). The three-year evidence now supports a narrower claim: the 16-team fields consistently produce closer State matches and usually somewhat more seed volatility, but the size of the upset effect moves by season and gender.

## Continuity, turnover and expansion

Repeat boys champions were Pacific Gate, Bush, Villard, Mt Jacqueline, Canal View and Ninemile. Villard has now won **four straight 3A boys titles**. Repeat girls champions were Seawall, Trois Ilets, Grizzly Gulch and Garden City.

Orchard Union, Stone Ridge, Bianchi and Monongahela won their first boys State titles in the available ledger. Bluewater and Harmon Valley did the same for girls.

The Alderwold expansion produced two of those champions in year two: **Bianchi boys won 2A and Bluewater girls won 3A**. Both first appear in the program history in 2029 and both were State quarterfinalists in year one. None of the seven expansion-program champions from 2029 repeated.

## Program movement and individual State

Largest boys turnarounds included Kingsley (13-19 to 23-4), Dry Creek (3-21 to 14-14), Bridgewater (7-20 to 17-11), East Simmons (10-14 to 20-7) and J. Madison (12-18 to 22-9). The steepest declines included Vespertine North (21-10 to 6-17), Mission Ridge (21-5 to 9-14), Western Slope (12-12 to 3-20), Warren (17-11 to 5-16) and Gold Run (11-16 to 1-25).

Girls turnarounds included Bowerstock (12-18 to 23-5), Pellburg (3-23 to 12-12), Bracken (2-21 to 11-13), Sinkford (6-18 to 22-15) and Prunedale (11-16 to 18-6). The largest declines included Ransoms Landing (16-7 to 4-22), Mount Dylan Lake (16-11 to 3-23), Cardinal Echevarria (19-2 to 13-15), Haddon (19-7 to 9-18) and Valley Christian (24-3 to 14-14).

Individual State again separated individual strength from team strength. Denzel Maples of Alderwell won 2A boys from the **No. 12 seed**; King Corcoran of Saltbush won Group 1 boys from No. 9; Angelina Simpson of Chicago Island won Group 1 girls from **No. 10**; Violet Rivas won 11B girls even though Sinkford went out in the Round of 24; Maite Gayà won 3A girls even though Villard's team season ended in Super Regionals.

## Portal, early participation and split squads

The portal is now stable rather than shrinking again: **69 moves in each gender**, versus 70 boys / 68 girls in 2029. Boys destinations were 42 county, 13 same-area and 14 neighboring-area moves; girls were 44 county, 16 same-area and 9 neighboring-area moves. Every mover played varsity; 61 boys and 58 girls reached at least ten varsity duals.

Early-participant counts were 278 boys and 314 girls. Of those, 190 boys and 219 girls appeared in varsity competition.

V2/V3 produced 334 boys V2 and 147 boys V3 varsity duals (**481 total**), and 341 girls V2 plus 137 girls V3 (**478 total**). JV State champions were Canal View boys from the No. 26 seed and Harmon Valley girls from No. 12.

## Participation-limit audit: correctness issue

The repo rule is 24 regular-season competition dates across V1/V2/V3/JV, postseason exempt. Counting regular, early-window and showcase dates, the export has:

| Dates | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| exactly 24 | 1,419 | 1,584 |
| 25 | 389 | 363 |
| 26 | 41 | 34 |
| 27 | 4 | 3 |
| extreme outlier | 43 | 37 |

That is **435 boys and 401 girls above the stated cap**. The dominant pattern looks like a one-date leak, but the two extreme cases show another path also bypasses the shared budget. No player appeared in both a varsity and JV dual on the same date. This is a correctness issue before 2031, not a calibration question.

## Association judgment for 2031

The championship architecture itself does not need another redesign. The private classes are producing distinct champions, 11B's 3S/4D card works, the TOC qualifiers materially affect the statewide tournament, the 16-team State fields continue to produce a distinct close-match environment, and the portal has settled at a stable scale.

The two concrete issues are operational: fix the 24-date leak, and decide whether a two-team active 10B pod is acceptable. Everything else can proceed to another season with measurement rather than structural change.
