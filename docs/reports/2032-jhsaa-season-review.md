# JHSAA 2032 Season Retrospective and Association Report

The uploaded research export is internally labeled `2106`; this report treats it as canonical **2032**, following the established retrofit (`2105` = 2031).

## Executive findings

- **Jesuit boys won the Tournament of Champions as a 10B State runner-up.** They beat Seawall 3-2 in the TOC final after qualifying into the field.
- **Glen Park girls won the Tournament of Champions** after winning 7A State, beating Jackson Hole 3-2 in the semifinal and Mater Dei 3-2 in the final.
- **Seawall boys, Belyakov Vo-Tech boys, Dutchfork boys, Monongahela boys, Potrero Hill girls, Glen Park girls, and several others produced major title turnover.** Only Canal View repeated on the boys side from 2031; Hackensack and Ciderhaven were the only repeat girls State champions.
- The 16-team classes returned to being more upset-prone in 2032. Lower seeds won **30.7% of boys matches and 32.0% of girls matches**, versus 26.5% and 23.0% in the other seven public classes. They also remained much closer: 42.7% of boys and 53.3% of girls matches were decided by one position.
- The redesigned **coaching carousel is now clearly visible in the archive**. Between the 2031 and 2032 staffs, 337 of 940 boys programs and 311 of 977 girls programs changed head coaches.
- The assistant pipeline landed almost exactly at the intended scale: **220 boys assistants (8.0%) and 202 girls assistants (7.1%) became head coaches** between seasons.
- The carousel is producing both stability and churn. Over the last five archived seasons, 494 boys programs and 535 girls programs had only one head coach, while 110 boys programs and 112 girls programs had at least three. Twenty boys programs and seventeen girls programs had four different heads in five seasons.
- Strong programs are not all being destabilized. Condotti Vanguard Academy, Mater Dei, Canal View boys and Oakhaven retained the same head throughout the recent eight-season window; Jesuit girls did as well.
- Head-coach turnover was strongly related to the prior team's competitive position: among 2031 programs, bottom-quartile teams within their classification changed heads at **56.8% boys / 47.5% girls**, versus **22.4% / 20.8%** for top-quartile teams.
- The stronger middle-school pipeline grew again. Grades 7-8 rose to **630 boys and 712 girls**, with 435 boys and 481 girls appearing in varsity competition.
- Eligible 1A/2A/Group 3 rosters now average **2.70 boys and 2.87 girls early participants**, with a median of 2 boys and 3 girls.
- Early-development rolls remain broad rather than deterministic. Across players with recorded rolls, the median accumulated early-potential lift is about **15% of ceiling**, the 90th percentile about **39%**, and the 99th percentile about **64% boys / 58% girls**.
- Accelerator hits remained close to design at **21.6% boys and 20.5% girls**; exceptional hits landed at **3.2-3.3%**.
- The larger intake is now feeding the rising-freshman portal: current moves rose to **156 boys and 168 girls**, up from 67 and 85 in 2031. Nearly all movers appeared in varsity competition (152 boys, 167 girls).
- The cosmetic calendar remained clean on the hard rule: **zero district doubleheaders** in both genders. Undated varsity matches were ancillary only.

## Tournament of Champions

### Boys

The qualifier winners were Jesuit over St. Lucia Academy, 3-2, and Rampart over Bienville, 4-1.

Jesuit then beat Porterfield 3-2, Miles Davis 4-1, Canal View 3-2 and Seawall **3-2** to win the TOC.

That makes the TOC qualifier consequential again: the boys statewide champion was not a State champion but a 10B runner-up who entered through the qualifier.

### Girls

The qualifier winners were Mater Dei over Gottschalk-Herman, 3-2, and Natchitoches over Shasta, 3-2.

Glen Park won the TOC, beating Fletcher-Garrison Hall 5-0, Mt Jacqueline 3-2, Jackson Hole 3-2 and Mater Dei **3-2** in the final.

## State-field behavior

The five 16-team classes moved back toward the more volatile pattern seen in 2029-30.

| State format | 2031 boys lower-seed | 2032 boys | 2031 girls | 2032 girls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16-team classes | 18.7% | **30.7%** | 24.0% | **32.0%** |
| Other seven public classes | 22.6% | **26.5%** | 24.9% | **23.0%** |

Closeness remained the more stable distinction:

- boys 16-team classes: **42.7%** one-position matches;
- boys other public classes: **27.2%**;
- girls 16-team classes: **53.3%**;
- girls other public classes: **32.3%**.

The four-season reading remains: smaller State fields consistently produce tighter matches, while the size of the upset effect moves from year to year.

## Championship turnover

2032 was a high-turnover championship year.

Only **Canal View boys** repeated from the 2031 boys championship slate.

On the girls side, only **Hackensack (5A)** and **Ciderhaven (3A)** repeated.

That produced a particularly broad set of new or returning winners, including Seawall boys, Belyakov Vo-Tech, Alameda, Miles Davis, Dutchfork, Buckeye Ridge, Porterfield, Puerto Alma, Silton Ridge, Monongahela, Quaking Aspen, Chaminade and Coastal Christian.

The title map therefore turned over much more heavily than the prior season even though the strongest seeds still won many of the largest classes.

## Coaching carousel: first full competitive read

The new carousel is no longer just a settings change. It has materially altered the coaching labor market.

### Head turnover

Comparing archived head seats from 2031 to 2032:

| | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Programs with a head in both seasons | 940 | 977 |
| Changed head coach | **337** | **311** |
| Change rate | **35.9%** | **31.8%** |

That is substantially more movement than the one-cycle fresh-world probe because the live save contains the full firing, promotion, retirement and movement history.

The distribution matters more than the statewide average.

Using 2031 competitive records within each classification:

| 2031 competitive tier | Boys changing heads | Girls changing heads |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bottom quarter | **56.8%** | **47.5%** |
| Middle half | **32.7%** | **29.8%** |
| Top quarter | **22.4%** | **20.8%** |

That is close to the intended shape: weak programs churn much more, while good programs are materially more stable.

### The assistant pipeline

The assistant-to-head pathway reached the target range:

- **220 boys assistants became heads**, 8.0% of the 2031 assistant pool.
- **202 girls assistants became heads**, 7.1%.
- 417 boys assistants and 464 girls assistants changed programs while remaining assistants.
- 81 boys heads and 75 girls heads moved into assistant roles.
- 117 boys heads and 108 girls heads moved directly to another head job.

This is now a real carousel rather than a sequence of isolated firings.

### Stable institutions and revolving doors coexist

Across 2102-2106:

- 494 boys programs and 535 girls programs used **one head coach** across all five seasons.
- 336 boys / 330 girls used two.
- 90 boys / 95 girls used three.
- **20 boys programs and 17 girls programs used four different heads in five seasons.**

So the model is producing exactly the two tails requested: entrenched staffs and programs where the job changes hands almost every year.

Examples of stable recent staffs include Condotti Vanguard Academy, Mater Dei, Canal View boys, Oakhaven and Jesuit girls.

The strongest title teams were also more stable than the association as a whole. Of the 14 boys State champions from 2031, 12 retained the same head at the same school for 2032. Ten of the 14 girls champions did.

## Middle-school system: larger cohorts, wider outcomes

The stronger intake continued to grow.

| | 2031 | 2032 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Boys grades 7-8 | 558 | **630** |
| Boys appearing varsity | 385 | **435** |
| Girls grades 7-8 | 613 | **712** |
| Girls appearing varsity | 439 | **481** |

Among eligible 1A/2A/Group 3 rosters:

| | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean early players / roster | **2.70** | **2.87** |
| Median | 2 | 3 |
| 90th percentile | 5 | 5 |
| Maximum | 8 | 11 |
| Rosters with none | 5.4% | 4.2% |

The range-based development system is also producing the intended spread.

Across players with recorded early-development rolls:

| Total accumulated early lift | Boys | Girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10th percentile | 5.3% | 4.9% |
| Median | **15.0%** | **14.6%** |
| 90th percentile | **39.2%** | **38.2%** |
| 99th percentile | **63.8%** | **57.9%** |

Accelerator hit rates were **21.6% boys / 20.5% girls**. Exceptional events were **3.2% / 3.3%**.

The point of the redesign is now visible: early participation does not push everyone toward one predictable bonus. Most players get a moderate lift, some get a major one, and a small tail separates sharply.

## Portal

The portal increased with the larger early-participant cohorts:

| | 2031 | 2032 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Boys moves | 67 | **156** |
| Girls moves | 85 | **168** |

152 of 156 boys movers and 167 of 168 girls movers appeared in varsity competition.

The volume increase therefore looks like a direct consequence of having more rising freshmen in the early-participant pathway, not a breakdown in the playing-time purpose of the portal.

## Calendar

The cosmetic calendar retained the hard constraints introduced before this season:

- **zero district doubleheaders** in both genders;
- zero undated district matches;
- undated varsity matches were ancillary/non-district only.

Displayed spans:

- boys: August 2-November 18;
- girls: March 2-June 9.

Undated varsity matches were 923 boys and 1,264 girls, overwhelmingly ordinary non-district play. That is expected under the current rule: preserve league dates and the season window, let ancillary dates disappear first.

## What 2032 validates

Working as intended:

- assistant-to-head movement is now in the intended 5-8% range;
- assistant lateral movement is substantial enough that strong assistants are no longer permanently buried;
- head-coach churn is much higher at poor programs than at strong ones;
- stable elite programs and genuine revolving-door programs coexist;
- the middle-school intake remains a minority pathway while reaching enough players to affect the association;
- early-development outcomes are broad rather than deterministic;
- the larger early pipeline is feeding more portal movement without losing varsity conversion;
- the TOC qualifier continues to admit teams capable of winning the statewide tournament;
- the cosmetic calendar still protects district play.

Worth watching rather than redesigning immediately:

- live-save head turnover is much higher than the fresh-world one-cycle probe, so another season should show whether the 30-35% year-to-year head-seat change settles or remains this high;
- the early-development system now has enough two-year participants that 2033-34 should begin to provide a meaningful career-development read rather than just a roll-distribution check.

## Association judgment for 2033

The coaching carousel has done the thing the old model could not: it created a labor market. Assistants are becoming heads at the intended rate, assistants are moving laterally, former heads are landing elsewhere, weak programs churn more often, and prominent programs can still retain long-serving staffs.

The middle-school system also looks structurally established rather than experimental. Intake, accelerator frequency and the distribution of development lifts are all operating at scale. The next useful question is no longer whether those mechanisms fire; it is how the first full two-year cohorts change varsity quality and senior outcomes.

No broad structural change is indicated by the 2032 export.
