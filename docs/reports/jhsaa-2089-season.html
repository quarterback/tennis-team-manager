JHSAA 2089: season analysis and stories

Both 2089 seasons, girls in spring and boys in fall, worked from the Play to Clinch export: 1,791 programs, 25,636 varsity duals, about 256,000 lines, 24 state champions and two Tournaments of Champions. The first half is the numbers. The second half is six stories rebuilt from the match records, each with a counterfactual.

The reclassification moved 674 programs and the promoted ones kept winning

Between 2088 and 2089 the association moved 332 boys programs and 342 girls programs to a new class within their system. In the previous fifteen seasons the largest single year count for either gender was 8. Eleven of the 24 champions won in a class they had just been moved up into. From 2027 through 2088, a program playing its first season after a move up won a state title 6 times in total across both genders.

Ten of the 28 play in 9A. The Mater Dei boys, 45-0 a year ago, finished 10-19, which is just outside the definition.

The state format change did not favor doubles teams, because everyone restacks

The regular season is three singles and four doubles in every class. State is not. 2A through 5A and Group 3 play one singles and four doubles. 1A plays two and three, Group 2 plays three and three, 6A keeps the regular format, and 7A through 9A and Group 1 play four singles and five doubles. The Tournament of Champions is one singles and four doubles.

In the one singles classes, a team's regular season tilt toward doubles (doubles line win rate minus singles line win rate) had no relationship with how far it went relative to its seed. The correlation was -0.003 for boys and -0.011 for girls across 192 state teams each. The reason is in the lineups: 90% of boys D1 seats and 93% of girls D1 seats at state in those classes were filled by players who had spent the regular season in singles. Pairs made of two converted singles players won 51.6% (boys) and 51.8% (girls) of their state lines. Pairs with no converted player won 49.2% and 49.9%.

Stories

Everything below comes from lineups, line scores, dates, brackets and honors in the export. There are no quotes and no invented scenes. Each story ends with a counterfactual that changes one recorded thing and follows the arithmetic.

Furnace Creek lost once, on a day its best player sat

Furnace Creek finished 41-1, won its first Group 1 title since 2035 and then won the Tournament of Champions. The one loss was 4-3 at Cedar on August 12, and the lineup card explains most of it. Channing Shipley, the senior who played No. 1 singles, was not on it.

That was normal in August. Shipley sat out 12 of Furnace Creek's 42 duals, nearly all of them routs, and the team went 11-1 without him. At Cedar the usual No. 2 and No. 3, Dante Smith and Prince Brandt, moved up a line and won in straight sets. Tyler Courson filled No. 3 singles and lost to Cedar senior Abraham Frost 6-0, 3-6, 6-1. Cedar took three of the four doubles. The last of them belonged to Shaq and George Murphy, a freshman and his sophomore brother, who won No. 4 doubles 7-6, 7-6. Cedar had been moved up from Group 2 that summer and finished second to Furnace Creek in the Metro League. Four weeks later Furnace Creek had Shipley back for the rematch and won 7-0.

Shipley did not play a third set until October 6. From there he played nine and won nine. The last seven came in a row across eight days. He won the state quarterfinal line 7-6, 6-7, 7-5, the semifinal line 6-0, 4-6, 6-3 in a 5-4 win over Jackson Hole, and the final line 2-6, 6-2, 7-5. Then the format changed. The Tournament of Champions is one singles and four doubles, so Furnace Creek paired Smith and Brandt, who had gone a combined 65-8 in singles, at No. 1 doubles.

After a 5-0 first round, the next three duals had the same shape. Furnace Creek lost No. 2 and No. 3 doubles each time. Jacob Hilton and Ethan Brown won No. 4 doubles each time. Smith and Brandt won No. 1 doubles each time, twice in three sets. And Shipley won the singles in three sets each time: 4-6, 6-3, 6-2 over Leon Jurgens of Quarmont, the 4A player of the year; 4-6, 6-4, 6-4 over Iker Dipietro of Bush; 7-6, 2-6, 6-2 over Colin Lunsford of Evans Larsen Day, the 8A player of the year. Quarmont, Bush and Evans Larsen Day were a combined 99-0 when they met Furnace Creek.

What if Shipley plays on August 12?

Smith and Brandt drop back to No. 2 and No. 3, where they went 32-3 and 33-5. The line Furnace Creek lost at No. 3 becomes a line Brandt played 38 times and lost 5. A 4-3 loss becomes a 4-3 win on that change alone, and the record becomes 42-0. The other direction is just as short. Shipley lost the first set in three of his last four matches. Reverse the third set of any one of the last three and Furnace Creek goes out of the Tournament of Champions that day, because the rest of each dual was 2-2.

The 9A boys final came down to two tiebreaks

Three of the four 9A boys semifinalists had not lost. Jesuit was 39-0. Canal View was 27-0. Southridge Christian was 28-0 and had lost the final in 2086 at 34-1 and in 2087 at 40-1.

Southridge had already used up a season's worth of luck getting there. It beat Natchitoches 5-4 in the round of 16 with four of its five points coming in three sets. It beat Tamarack 5-4 the next day when senior Thomas Herring won No. 1 singles 4-6, 7-5, 7-6. Canal View reached the final by beating Jesuit 6-3, a score that hides three lines Canal View won in three sets. One was Nikolai Gomez at No. 4 singles, 3-6, 7-6, 6-3 over Emmanuel Savitt, the Jesuit freshman who finished 38-2. Another was the No. 4 doubles win by Cohen and Anibal Kaddouri, a sophomore and his freshman brother.

Canal View is a family team. Nikolai Gomez, a junior, plays No. 4 singles and his brother Victor, a sophomore, plays No. 2. A year ago the two of them lost the 9A No. 3 doubles final to a Mater Dei pair. In this final Nikolai won in three sets. Keegan Duncan won No. 1 singles for Canal View and the No. 2 and No. 3 doubles pairs won. That is four points. The Kaddouris lost No. 4 doubles in three. At No. 5 doubles, Southridge sophomores Mustafa Ferguson and Malachi Allain beat freshmen Kameron Frank and Soren Pierce 6-3, 3-6, 7-6. At No. 2 singles, Victor Gomez won the first set in a tiebreak, lost the second 6-4, and lost the third in a tiebreak to Xavier Neff. Southridge Christian 5, Canal View 4.

What if either tiebreak goes the other way?

Canal View wins 5-4 and finishes 29-0 one year after finishing third in the Capital Athletic Association behind a 45-0 Mater Dei. Southridge Christian finishes 29-1 with a third lost final in four years. Both had to go Southridge's way, and a third set tiebreak between two players who have split the first two sets is close to even. Canal View played 86% of its state lines with underclassmen, including both Gomez brothers, both Kaddouris, Frank and Pierce. Duncan is the only senior among its winners in the final.

Barclay won 3A while losing No. 1 singles in every round

Barclay finished third in the Juniper League at 10-4, behind Villard (14-0) and Governor Woods. It entered the 3A tournament 17-7 as the 15 seed. Its No. 1 singles player, junior Raiden Stewart, was 10-12 in the regular season.

3A plays one singles and four doubles at state, and Stewart lost the singles all five times: to a Cherry Hill North freshman 7-5 in the third, to Isaiah Hayes of Ostrander Union, to Vahid Crutchfield of Vale in three, to 3A player of the year Cooper Miller of Port Meridian North 7-6, 7-5, and to Cameron Wilson of Los Remolinos in the final. Barclay's doubles went 16-4. Junior Shingo Morii and senior Boston Williams, the only senior in the group, went 5-0 at No. 4. Four of the five duals finished 3-2. In the quarterfinal against Vale, the 4 seed at 30-3, three Barclay doubles wins went three sets, one of them 7-5 in the third. In the final, against a Los Remolinos team that had jumped from 1A to 3A and gone 27-2, the No. 2 and No. 4 doubles both went to a third set and Barclay won both 7-5.

Two days later, in the Tournament of Champions, Barclay changed the card. Freshman Colton Davis, who had played No. 1 doubles all through state, played the singles against Micah Renfroe of Westfield Friends, the 5A player of the year at 30-7. Davis won 6-2, 6-1. Barclay also won No. 1 doubles and lost 3-2 when Morii and Williams, unbeaten for a week, lost 6-4, 3-6, 6-4 to a Westfield freshman and sophomore.

What if Davis plays singles from the first round?

The export cannot say he would have beaten Miller or Wilson. It does say Barclay won four rounds by a single point while conceding the singles point in each, and that the one time it tried a different player there he beat a player of the year 12 games to 3. It also says the No. 1 doubles team went 2-3 at state with him on it. Barclay played 87% of its postseason lines with underclassmen, Davis included.

Mater Dei, one year after 45-0

In 2088 the Mater Dei boys went 45-0 and won 9A and the Tournament of Champions. The girls went 41-1 and did the same. In 2089 the boys went 10-19, finished eighth in the Capital Athletic Association and were out at sectionals on October 3. The girls went 3-21 and finished eleventh.

Graduation took the top of both lineups: Christopher Strickler, Sawyer Ganesan and Kraigg Cádiz from the boys, and 9A player of the year Alayna Pesce, Amia Severino, Ana Galvan and Heidi Miller from the girls. But most of the boys' card returned, and the returning players are the story. Jayden Bustillos was first team all state, league player of the year and the 9A No. 2 singles runner up in 2088. As a senior he went 12-12 at No. 2. Drew Turk and Luke Davis were first team all state as a doubles pair. Turk went 8-16 in doubles and Davis 6-12. Gavin Hoffer and Gavin Love won the 9A No. 2 doubles title together, Love as a freshman. Hoffer went 11-17 at No. 1 singles and Love 11-18 at No. 3. Malik Trivedi won the No. 1 doubles title as a sophomore and went 7-11. Mater Dei was 5-13 by the end of August.

On the girls side, Taylor Marten won the 9A No. 3 singles title as a sophomore. As a junior she played three singles lines, lost all three, and went 3-17 in doubles. Faye Ryu, a No. 1 doubles finalist a year ago, moved up to No. 1 singles and went 4-19.

Programs rise and fall in this association for reasons that never appear in a box score, and Mater Dei has been here before: the girls won everything in 2082 and went 21-11 the next year. The historical record on falls this steep is in the analysis above. Eighteen to 23 percent of crashed programs are back above .700 within three seasons and about 40 percent are not back within ten. The exception is the group that fell in 2086, nearly all of which recovered within three years and eight of which have since won titles.

What if the returning boys had simply repeated 2088?

Seven returning players had won or reached an individual state final the year before, enough to fill most of a nine line card. Instead Canal View, which lost to Mater Dei pairs in the 9A No. 2 and No. 3 doubles finals in 2088 and finished third in the league, won the league at 17-0 and came within two tiebreaks of the state title. Seven freshmen and sophomores played varsity lines for the Mater Dei boys this year.

Ciderhaven lost to Grizzly Gulch twice, then beat them for the title

Ciderhaven and Grizzly Gulch share the Far West League. Ciderhaven was moved up to 4A this year from 3A, where it lost the 2088 final. Grizzly Gulch won the league. It beat Ciderhaven 4-3 on March 11 and 5-2 on April 23, and it reached the 4A final at 37-1 as the class's computer No. 1. Grizzly Gulch had been 3-19 three years earlier.

Both regular season duals were played three singles and four doubles. The final was one singles and four doubles, and both coaches restacked. Grizzly Gulch kept senior Lilly D'Aquila at singles and moved its No. 3, Justina Alonso, to No. 1 doubles. Ciderhaven kept 4A player of the year Olivia Burnett at singles and moved its No. 2 and No. 3, Serena Franklin and Adriana Millar, to No. 1 doubles together. Franklin had beaten the Grizzly Gulch No. 2 in both league duals. D'Aquila beat Burnett 3-6, 6-3, 6-2, her second win in their three meetings. Franklin and Millar won 6-4, 7-5. Ciderhaven won No. 2 and No. 4 doubles in straight sets and the final 3-2. It then won four more duals in the Tournament of Champions, the last two 4-1.

D'Aquila was also the top seed in the 4A No. 1 singles tournament. She reached the final and lost 1-6, 6-1, 7-6 to Candice Morrison of Texas Beach, who had won the No. 3 singles title in 2087 and the No. 2 title in 2088 two classes below. D'Aquila won her line in the team final, lost the team final, and lost the individual final in a third set tiebreak.

What if state were played in the regular season format?

Then the only evidence is the two league duals, and Grizzly Gulch won both. In those duals Ciderhaven won three of six singles lines and two of eight doubles lines. The one singles format removed two of Ciderhaven's strongest lines from the card and should have hurt it. What it did instead was let Ciderhaven turn two singles winners into one doubles point at the top of the order, against a Grizzly Gulch team that had owned the doubles all spring.

Raft County Catholic won the first set of the No. 2 doubles line it lost.

Method

All joins use program_id. School names shown are the 2089 display names from the programs table. Player records in the analysis count varsity dual lines only unless a full season record from the awards file is stated. Player ratings were not used anywhere. A one point dual is any varsity dual with a final margin of one team point or less, which includes tied duals settled by tiebreak. The export flags which duals were settled by tiebreak but not the rule. Winners of those duals did not consistently hold the edge in sets, games, No. 1 singles or No. 1 doubles, so the stories report the tiebreak without describing it. Line order within a dual is not recorded, so no story says which line finished last. Crash and recovery rates use program history from 2027 and count only crashes old enough to have ten later seasons on record. Reclassification counts compare each program's class in 2088 and 2089 and leave out the 18 programs per gender that changed between the numbered classes and the Group system.
