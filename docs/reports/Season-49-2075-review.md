The useful way to read 2075 is that each class now has a recognizable upper crust, but the shape of that crust differs a lot by classification. Some have a real dynasty layer, some have two or three stable powers, and some are still open enough that a rising program can jump very quickly.

A few classes are especially interesting.

**9A is finally behaving like a major-power ecosystem rather than a revolving door.** Port Meridian Central is No. 1 in both genders in 2075, with three recent boys top-8 years and three girls top-8 years. Notre Dame is even more entrenched on the girls side: top eight in all five recent seasons and four titles. Jesuit is the third name that gives the class continuity rather than just one or two isolated dynasties. The newer layer is Potrero Hill boys and Weller girls: both are already near the top without having the same five-year title history. The best “next step” candidate is **Monroe boys**: they went from rank 57 in 2073 to 30 in 2074 to **10 in 2075**, and return a top 11 averaging about 65 OVR with eight sophomores/freshmen among those returners. That looks less like a one-year spike and more like a program arriving.

**8A has the clearest two-pole structure.** Plainfield and Minnesota City now look like permanent reference points. Plainfield girls have five straight top-8 seasons and three recent titles; Minnesota City boys have two titles and three top-8 seasons. Cherry Hill South is becoming the obvious third program because both genders are now No. 2. The one I would watch hardest is **Quarry Workers boys**: they were 35th in 2073, 44th in 2074, then jumped to **8th** in 2075 and return an absurdly strong top 11 averaging about **72.5 OVR**. That is the kind of roster where one more good intake could turn an emergent into a power immediately.

**7A is probably the most mature competitive hierarchy now.** Baptist is the anchor, Condotti and Romero-Finniski have longstanding top-tier history, and Evans Larsen Day has five straight top-8 years in both genders with two recent girls titles. Port Meridian South has moved from outsider to true top-layer status: boys are No. 1 and have four top-8 finishes in five years. The interesting intervention targets are not the obvious powers. **Mission Terrace** has both genders in the teens and multiple State appearances but no top-8 history; **Reyes Landing boys** improved from 33 → 31 → 16 and return seven young players in the projected top 11. Those are programs where one or two additions could create another durable 7A team rather than just deepen an existing giant.

**6A is more open than 7A.** Chaminade is the only program that looks fully institutionalized across both genders: No. 1 in both in 2075 and five straight top-8 seasons. Valley Christian still has the title history, but its boys side fell to No. 6 after three recent titles, which looks more like cyclical roster churn than collapse. Cleveland boys winning their first title matters because they already had three top-8 seasons in the last five; that is a breakthrough from a program that had been knocking rather than a fluke. The best next-wave program is **Antelope Butte boys**: 59th in 2073, 44th in 2074, **13th in 2075**, with eight sophomores/freshmen in the returning top 11.

**5A is becoming a very good middle-tier ecosystem.** Westfield Friends is the closest thing to a class power, but the class is not monopolized. Star City girls have five straight top-8 seasons; The Dalles boys just won their first title after climbing into the top tier; Kittery girls are now No. 1 after not having much historical footprint. **Pine Barrens boys** is the classic boost candidate: 71st → 26th → **8th** in three seasons, with a returning top 11 over 61 OVR. That is exactly the kind of program where modest talent redistribution could create a new stable contender rather than an artificial superteam.

**4A has a real aristocracy now.** Walter-Kenny, Port Veles Episcopal and Gagarin have enough repeated State/top-8 history that the class has recognizable institutional powers. Walter-Kenny is the strongest of them: boys have three titles in five years, girls two, and both are top two again in 2075. Truman girls look like the new entrant into that tier after the 2075 repair/transfers pushed them to No. 2 and a State final. The most interesting growth program is **Eastmont boys**: 47th in 2073, 6th in 2074, 11th in 2075, with a young returning group whose potential is much higher than its current OVR. That is the kind of roster that could become a repeat top-eight program if it gets a little help rather than being left to churn.

**3A is the least ambiguous class: Bay Oregon is the standard.** Boys have three recent titles, girls three, and both are at or near No. 1 again. That is now a true two-gender dynasty. The useful question is who becomes the permanent opposition. Benedetti boys have three top-8 years and five State appearances without a title; Fletcher-Garrison Hall has four top-8 years. **St. Sebastian Prep boys** is the most interesting emergent: rank 71 in 2073, 24 in 2074, **11 in 2075**, returning a top 11 around 58 OVR with eight young players. If you were deliberately trying to thicken 3A's upper-middle rather than feed Bay Oregon, that is exactly the type of program to target.

**2A finally has a real flagship in Vale.** Vale boys have two recent titles and three top-8 years; the girls also have a recent title and three top-8 years. Lanphere boys are the most stable challenger without a title. The newer layer is Netherwood girls at No. 1 and Port Meridian North boys at No. 3. **South Rim boys** is a very good development candidate: 30th → 33rd → **9th**, with a returning top 11 over 52 OVR. **Halfway House boys** is even more dramatic: 68th → 47th → **8th**. Those are the kinds of programs that make 2A look less like Vale plus churn if they can hold another two seasons.

**1A has moved from volatility toward a recognizable top set.** Salmon Bay boys are now the clearest boys power, with four top-8 seasons and the 2075 title. Christ the King girls have four top-8 years and a title. New Penzance Island girls are No. 1 and have three recent top-8 finishes. Reed Lake boys and Bravewoman girls are becoming recurring contenders rather than one-offs. The one I'd circle for intervention is **Flume boys**: they were 64th in 2073 and 75th in 2074, then shot to **14th** in 2075, with nine sophomores/freshmen in the returning top 11. That is a program at exactly the point where an exchange student or one useful transfer could turn an isolated jump into a real arc.

**Group 1 is still the least dynastic of the classes, but it is developing a recognizable cast.** Cañada Grande, Etchartville and Charles Harbor have the strongest repeated top-end evidence. Charles Harbor girls have two recent titles; Cañada Grande boys have a title and three top-8 years; Etchartville boys have four top-8 finishes. West Oberlin and Grant are the newest title entrants. The most interesting “next rung” programs are **Granite boys** and **Harriman boys**: Granite went 55 → 14 → **9**, Harriman 49 → 30 → **10**, and both return strong top-11 groups. This is probably the class where targeted boosting can still most visibly reshape the hierarchy.

**Group 2 is the opposite: Pacific Friends is the gravitational center.** Both genders are No. 1, boys have two recent titles, girls three, and both have five straight top-8 seasons. That is about as stable as a power can get. Lizarra is the strongest non-champion institutional challenger on the girls side, with five straight top-8 finishes. Kuopio and Minidoka are becoming more recognizable. **Oak Forest boys** is the best emerging candidate: 49th in 2073, 14th in 2074, **10th in 2075**, with a healthy returning top 11. If Group 2 needs more than “Pacific Friends and whoever is hot this year,” that's the profile to cultivate.

**Group 3 has quietly stabilized more than it looks.** Espoo and Sotkamo Union now sit at the top in both genders, and Riverview has five straight State appearances with four boys top-8 seasons and a title. Those three give the class an actual identity. Northrup Tech boys at No. 3 are the newer arrival. **Fort Lassiter boys** is the strongest next-wave signal: 29th → 40th → **10th**, with a returning top 11 just over 50 OVR. **Nicodemus girls** also moved from 50th to 12th in two years and returns a young roster.

The broader pattern is that the classes where I would actively boost are no longer the ones with no good teams. They are the ones with **one established top layer and a thin second layer**.

The clearest targets for that kind of strengthening are:

**Monroe 9A boys, Quarry Workers 8A boys, Mission Terrace 7A, Antelope Butte 6A boys, Pine Barrens 5A boys, Eastmont 4A boys, St. Sebastian Prep 3A boys, South Rim/Halfway House 2A boys, Flume 1A boys, Granite/Harriman Group 1 boys, Oak Forest Group 2 boys, and Fort Lassiter Group 3 boys.**

Those are not random mid-ranked teams. They are programs whose recent trajectory and returning roster say they are already halfway into the next tier. The useful intervention is enough talent to let that become institutional rather than another one-season spike.
