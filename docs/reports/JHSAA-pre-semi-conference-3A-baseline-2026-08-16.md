# JHSAA Road to State: Pre–Semi-Conference 3A Baseline

**Date:** August 16, 2026  
**Status:** Historical analysis / pre–Semi-Conference format  
**Season analyzed:** 2027  
**Classification:** 3A girls and boys  
**Purpose:** Preserve a second full-size baseline of the old 40-team Road to State before the Semi-Conference layer is applied, so the new format can be evaluated against what the previous system actually produced.

## Executive summary

The 2027 3A girls and boys seasons reinforce the central finding from the 2A-1A baseline: the old 40-team Road to State was not producing obviously bad State fields, but its final recovery round flattened distinctions between teams that had earned very different postseason standing.

The field sizes were:

- **3A boys:** 114 programs, 40 State qualifiers — **35.1%** of the classification.
- **3A girls:** 127 programs, 40 State qualifiers — **31.5%** of the classification.

Both used the same old recovery shape:

`Super Regionals → Semi-State → Divisionals → Conference`

with the State field filled by:

- **8 Zonal champions**
- **12 Semi-State winners**
- **6 Divisional winners**
- **14 Conference winners**

Conference therefore supplied **35% of State**.

In both 3A fields, Conference again contained **28 teams**:

- 6 Divisional losers
- 22 teams whose last loss came before Regionals

The exact source of those 22 differed:

| Conference source | 3A boys | 3A girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Divisional losers | 6 | 6 |
| Ward losers | 10 | 9 |
| Sectional losers | 7 | 10 |
| Area losers | 5 | 3 |
| **Total** | **28** | **28** |

The final 14 Conference berths broke down as:

| Conference winner source | 3A boys | 3A girls |
|---|---:|---:|
| Divisional losers | 3 | 4 |
| Ward losers | 6 | 5 |
| Sectional losers | 3 | 4 |
| Area losers | 2 | 1 |
| **Total** | **14** | **14** |

So:

- 3A boys sent **11 pre-Regional Conference teams** to State.
- 3A girls sent **10 pre-Regional Conference teams** to State.
- Combined, **21 of 28 Conference berths (75%)** went to teams that had not reached Regionals.
- Those 21 teams represented **26.3% of the combined 80-team 3A State fields**.

As in 2A-1A, this is not evidence that broad recovery is wrong. Several of those recovered teams won again at State, including one of the strongest examples in the archive: **Tamarack Harbor girls lost in an Area, recovered through Conference, entered State as the No. 35 seed, and reached the State quarterfinals.**

The design defect was the cost of entry. An Area loser could move directly into a berth-bearing Conference dual, while a team that had survived much deeper into the postseason could arrive at the same match only after several recovery rounds.

Semi-Conference should preserve the former team's ability to recover while requiring it to earn the Conference seat first.

# 3A Boys

## Field size

- Programs: **114**
- State field: **40**
- State participation rate: **35.1%**
- Regional field: **32**
- Minimum State teams that must come from outside Regionals: **8**

This is the broadest State field of the four pre–Semi-Conference classes analyzed so far. More than one in three 3A boys programs reached State.

That makes the old Conference especially important: a 40-team State cannot be filled solely from a 32-team Regional field, so the format necessarily needs a route for some teams that did not reach Regionals.

## Recovery shape

The boys' recovery path was:

- 16 Regional losers
- 8 Super Regional duals
- 24-team Semi-State field
- 12 Semi-State duals
- 12-team Divisional field
- 6 Divisional duals
- 28-team Conference
- 14 Conference winners to State

As in the 2A-1A examples, this particular season did **not** strand a recovery-round loser before the next scheduled recovery stage.

All eight Super Regional losers were represented in Semi-State, and all twelve Semi-State losers were represented in Divisionals.

That means this archive does not demonstrate the specific bug Semi-Conference now fixes when a Super Regional or Semi-State loser is skipped in favor of a lower-rung team. It does, however, show why a separate qualifying layer was needed before Conference.

## Conference composition

| Last previous loss | Entrants | Conference winners | Win rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Divisional | 6 | 3 | 50.0% |
| Ward | 10 | 6 | 60.0% |
| Sectional | 7 | 3 | 42.9% |
| Area | 5 | 2 | 40.0% |
| **Total** | **28** | **14** | **50.0%** |

Thus:

- **22 of 28 entrants (78.6%)** had not reached Regionals.
- **11 of 14 Conference berths (78.6%)** went to pre-Regional teams.
- Those 11 teams represented **27.5% of the entire State field**.

The old Conference reached all the way back to five Area losers.

## Case study: Telfair Country Day

Telfair Country Day is a strong example of a recovered Ward loser proving State-level quality.

Path:

- Lost **Ward 10**, 2-3 to St. Brigid.
- Entered Conference.
- Beat Katherine Johnson, 4-1, to qualify for State.
- State seed: **24**.
- Won the State Qualifiers Round.
- Won the State First Round.
- Reached the **Octofinals**.

This is precisely the kind of outcome the recovery system should preserve.

A Ward loss did not correctly identify Telfair Country Day as outside the top State-level population. The Conference gave the program a chance to prove otherwise, and it did so repeatedly on court.

Under the new format, a comparable Ward loser should still be able to make this run, but it should first win Semi-Conference to earn its place in the berth-bearing Conference.

## Case study: St. Lucy

St. Lucy took the long recovery route:

- Lost Regional 4.
- Lost Super Regional 5.
- Re-entered Semi-State and lost again.
- Entered Divisionals and lost again.
- Won Conference.
- State seed: **34**.
- Reached the **Octofinals**.

St. Lucy demonstrates that repeated recovery opportunities can still identify a team capable of winning at State.

It also shows why the system cannot be understood as ordinary double elimination. The ladder is a hierarchy of qualification opportunities, not a fixed loss-count tournament.

## Case study: Area-level Conference entrants

Five boys' Conference entrants had last lost in Areas:

- Alben Barkley
- Fort Weller Independent
- Goldbank County
- Jersey City
- Walter Mondale

Two won Conference:

- **Fort Weller Independent**
- **Jersey City**

Both then lost in the State Qualifiers Round.

This is a useful contrast with the stronger Cinderella outcomes. The old system was broad enough to discover legitimate deep-run teams, but some Area recoveries were simply competitive enough to win one Conference dual and no more.

Semi-Conference adds another piece of evidence before such a team occupies one of the final 14 State berths.

# 3A Girls

## Field size

- Programs: **127**
- State field: **40**
- State participation rate: **31.5%**
- Regional field: **32**
- Minimum State teams that must come from outside Regionals: **8**

## Recovery shape

The girls' field used the same old 40-team arithmetic:

- 16 Regional losers
- 8 Super Regional duals
- 24-team Semi-State field
- 12 Semi-State duals
- 12-team Divisional field
- 6 Divisional duals
- 28-team Conference
- 14 Conference winners

Again, this simulation did **not** strand a recovery team before the next scheduled stage. Every Super Regional loser returned at Semi-State, and every Semi-State loser reached Divisionals.

## Conference composition

| Last previous loss | Entrants | Conference winners | Win rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Divisional | 6 | 4 | 66.7% |
| Ward | 9 | 5 | 55.6% |
| Sectional | 10 | 4 | 40.0% |
| Area | 3 | 1 | 33.3% |
| **Total** | **28** | **14** | **50.0%** |

Thus:

- **22 of 28 Conference entrants (78.6%)** had not reached Regionals.
- **10 of 14 Conference berths (71.4%)** went to pre-Regional teams.
- Those 10 teams represented **25.0% of the entire State field**.

The girls' Conference was somewhat more favorable to Divisional losers than the boys' version, but the underlying structure remained the same: most of the Conference field was populated by teams entering directly from much earlier stages.

## Case study: Tamarack Harbor

Tamarack Harbor is the strongest pre–Semi-Conference argument for preserving a broad recovery system.

Path:

- Lost **Area 18**, 2-3 to Fort Lassiter.
- Entered Conference directly under the old format.
- Beat Dahlberg Summit, 3-2, for a State berth.
- State seed: **35**.
- Won the State Qualifiers Round.
- Won the State First Round.
- Won in the Octofinals.
- Reached the **State quarterfinals**.

A team that did not survive Areas became one of the final eight teams in the State tournament.

That is important evidence against treating early postseason elimination as definitive.

But it is equally strong evidence for Semi-Conference.

The desirable story is not:

> An Area loser should never be allowed back.

The desirable story is:

> An Area or otherwise distant recovery candidate should have to produce another win before it is placed opposite a deep-recovery team for a State berth.

Tamarack Harbor was clearly capable of doing that. The old system simply never asked.

## Case study: Presentation Academy

Presentation Academy provides the sharpest contrast.

- TOSS rank: **15**
- **District champion**
- Lost at Regionals.
- Won a Super Regional.
- Lost Semi-State.
- Lost a Divisional.
- Reached Conference.
- Lost Conference to Tunnelton.
- Did not qualify for State.

Meanwhile Tamarack Harbor, after an Area loss, received direct Conference entry and ultimately reached the State quarterfinals.

There is nothing wrong with Tamarack Harbor qualifying while Presentation Academy did not. Tamarack Harbor kept winning when its final opportunities arrived.

The asymmetry is in the qualification burden.

Presentation Academy accumulated substantially more postseason standing before arriving at Conference. Tamarack Harbor was allowed to contest the same kind of berth immediately after its earlier loss.

The new system should preserve the result if Tamarack Harbor can reproduce it, but should make it win Semi-Conference first.

## Case study: Elk Bluff

Elk Bluff came through the deep recovery track:

- Lost Regional 15.
- Won Super Regional 4.
- Lost Semi-State.
- Lost Divisional.
- Won Conference.
- Entered State as the **No. 29 seed**.
- Won the Qualifiers Round.
- Won the First Round.
- Reached the **Octofinals**.

Again, the data supports the broader principle: teams arriving through recovery are not merely filler.

# Combined 3A findings

| Metric | Boys | Girls | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Programs | 114 | 127 | 241 |
| State berths | 40 | 40 | 80 |
| Conference entrants | 28 | 28 | 56 |
| Conference berths | 14 | 14 | 28 |
| Divisional-loser Conference entrants | 6 | 6 | 12 |
| Pre-Regional Conference entrants | 22 | 22 | 44 |
| Divisional-loser Conference winners | 3 | 4 | 7 |
| Pre-Regional Conference winners | 11 | 10 | 21 |

The composition of the 44 pre-Regional Conference entrants was:

| Source | Entrants | Winners |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ward | 19 | 11 |
| Sectional | 17 | 7 |
| Area | 8 | 3 |
| **Total** | **44** | **21** |

The lower the prior stage, the lower the aggregate Conference success rate:

- Ward: **57.9%**
- Sectional: **41.2%**
- Area: **37.5%**

That is directionally what a sensible hierarchy should produce. Teams eliminated later were more likely to win the final berth-bearing match.

But the old system did not use that hierarchy to determine how much proof a team owed before Conference.

Semi-Conference now can.

# Comparison with the 2A-1A baseline

Across the four archived fields analyzed so far — 2A-1A girls, 2A-1A boys, 3A girls, and 3A boys — the pre–Semi-Conference pattern is remarkably stable.

| Metric | Four-field total |
|---|---:|
| Programs | 504 |
| State berths | 160 |
| Conference entrants | 112 |
| Conference berths | 56 |
| Pre-Regional Conference entrants | 88 |
| Pre-Regional Conference winners | 44 |

So across these four 40-team fields:

- **78.6% of all Conference entrants** came from teams that had not reached Regionals.
- **78.6% of all Conference berths** went to those teams.
- **44 of 160 State teams — 27.5% of the entire State population — entered through Conference after failing to reach Regionals.**

The consistency is important.

This was not an isolated bracket oddity. It was a structural property of the old 40-team format.

The old Conference needed 28 bodies, but only six Divisional losers existed. The remaining **22 seats** necessarily came from farther back in the ladder.

Without an intermediate round, those 22 teams were placed directly into State-berth matches.

# What the 3A baseline adds to the case for Semi-Conference

The 2A-1A analysis established that the old Conference was too permissive in how teams entered the final berth-bearing round.

The 3A data adds three useful findings.

## 1. The 22-seat problem is structural

Both 3A genders again produced:

- 6 Divisional losers
- 22 other Conference entrants

The source mix changed, but the size did not.

That confirms that the old Conference was structurally dependent on a large secondary pool, not occasionally topping itself up with one or two extra teams.

## 2. Early recovery can identify real contenders

Tamarack Harbor went from an Area loss to the State quarterfinals.

Telfair Country Day went from a Ward loss to the State Octofinals.

Those results argue strongly against simply deleting the secondary recovery population.

The extra opportunity can reveal teams that belong.

## 3. The extra opportunity should itself be earned

The same evidence supports adding Semi-Conference rather than maintaining direct Conference access.

If Tamarack Harbor is actually a State quarterfinal-level team, asking it to win one additional qualifying dual should not destroy the opportunity.

Instead, it makes the eventual Conference appearance more comparable to the standing already accumulated by a Divisional loser.

# Evaluation criteria once the new format lands

The first post–Semi-Conference 3A simulation should be compared against this archive on the following measures.

## Semi-Conference provenance

For every Semi-Conference entrant, record why the team was selected:

1. district champion still outside State;
2. recovery-round team left behind by the ordinary arithmetic;
3. top remaining Ward-level team by the relevant rating/order.

The important test is whether a later-stage recovery casualty is ever skipped for a Ward loser.

It should not happen.

## Conference provenance

Under the new format, Conference should consist of:

- Divisional losers; and
- Semi-Conference winners.

No team should arrive directly from a Ward, Sectional, Area, or an unplayed rating selection.

## State provenance

Track the deepest primary-ladder stage reached by every State qualifier.

Do not expect the number of teams that failed to reach Regionals to collapse to zero. A 40-team State with a 32-team Regional field mathematically requires outside entrants.

The meaningful comparison is whether those outside entrants now arrive after an additional on-court win.

## State performance by route

Track how far the following groups advance:

- Zonal champions;
- Semi-State qualifiers;
- Divisional qualifiers;
- Conference qualifiers from Divisional losses;
- Conference qualifiers from Semi-Conference.

If Semi-Conference winners continue to produce outcomes like Tamarack Harbor or Telfair Country Day, that is evidence the new round is filtering rather than merely punishing.

If essentially none survive the State Qualifiers Round, revisit whether the additional hurdle is too aggressive.

# Baseline judgment

The 3A data strengthens the conclusion from 2A-1A.

The old 40-team field was broad but not unserious. Recovery teams repeatedly demonstrated that they could beat State-level opposition.

The problem was not that too many kinds of teams were permitted to recover.

The problem was that **the system did not price recovery according to how far a team had already advanced**.

A Divisional loser and an Area loser could both arrive at Conference, even though one had spent several rounds proving itself and the other had not won since the beginning of the ladder.

Semi-Conference is the correct kind of reform because it changes the burden without changing the core philosophy:

> **A rating or prior result may buy another match. It does not buy a berth.**

And the governing ordering principle remains:

> **A team the postseason dropped later should never be skipped for one it dropped earlier.**

The new format should be judged against those two rules, not simply by whether its final 40 looks more highly rated.
