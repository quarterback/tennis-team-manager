›How these are selected résumé, not ratings

Selections are made from what happened this season — the match-by-match record — and never from ability ratings. Nothing here reads OVR, talent, class year, recruiting rank or last season's honours, so a great player on a poor team can win Player of the Year, and an unbeaten record against a weak slate does not automatically outrank a strong one against the best in the class.

What the selectors weigh:

  • Record and volume — wins, losses and how much the player actually played.
  • Position — the flight it was earned at. A win at #1 singles or #1 doubles counts for far more than one at #5.
  • Opposition — the quality of the individual opponents faced, not the opposing team's overall strength.
  • Quality wins — beating other award-calibre players is the fastest way up.
  • Good losses — a competitive loss to an elite opponent costs very little; a loss to a weak one costs plenty.
  • Head-to-head — decides between candidates whose résumés are otherwise level.
  • Postseason — Sectionals through State count for more. There is no individual state tournament in Jefferson; this is the team postseason the player played.

Every All-State and All-District team is {{ view.team_singles }} singles + {{ view.team_doubles }} doubles selections. 7A crowns a Fourth Team; every other classification stops at Third. Honorable Mention is a threshold, not a team — there is no slot count, so a deep classification honours more players and a thin one fewer, and a good season can miss it entirely. At most two Honorable Mention selections per school; the numbered teams have no such cap.
