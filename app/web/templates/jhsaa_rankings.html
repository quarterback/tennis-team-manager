TOSS — 40% strength of schedule, 40% flight-weighted results, 20% game share. It is computed over the whole association's regular season, not one classification, and is archived with the season: at-large bids and state seeds were drawn from exactly these numbers, so the ranking and the bracket cannot disagree.

ATR — the Average Team Rating, half TOSS and half win percentage, archived the same way. It is the only figure here that is not pure TOSS, and it exists for one job: ranking the Conference pool, the last recovery round. TOSS measures who you played, so a middling team in a brutal district is propped up by the company it keeps while a twenty-win season against an ordinary schedule rates below it — the right trade for seeding a draw, the wrong one for the last seat in the field. Seasons archived before ATR existed show a dash.

The SHOWCASE columns answer one question: what does the State format do to this team? State is decided at one singles line and four doubles; the regular season is played at five and two. The mid-season Match Showcases are the only place a team plays the State card before it counts, so these compare the two.

SC is how many showcases the team played. Fmt is its showcase result minus its own regular-season result, in points: 0 means the format changes nothing for them, +15 means they play fifteen points better once the card drops to 1S/4D, −15 means worse. Dbl is the same reading for doubles alone — how much more of the win doubles carries under the State card, 0 being the share it already carries in the regular season.

Both are measured against the team's OWN regular season, not against the field, so a weak team and a champion can both sit at 0: it means the format suits them the same either way. And both are damped by how many showcases were played — three duals is not evidence of a fifteen-point swing, so a thin sample reads near 0 until it earns otherwise. Hover a cell for the dual count. None of this is TOSS or ATR and none of it is archived toward a seed.
