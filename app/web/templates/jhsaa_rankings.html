TOSS is 40% strength of schedule, 40% flight-weighted results and 20% game share. It is computed over the whole association's regular season and archived with the season: at-large bids and state seeds were drawn from exactly these numbers, so the ranking and the bracket cannot disagree.

ATR, the Average Team Rating, is half TOSS and half win percentage, archived the same way. It ranks the Conference pool, the last recovery round. TOSS measures who you played, so a middling team in a brutal district is propped up by the company it keeps while a twenty-win season against an ordinary schedule rates below it. That suits seeding a draw and misjudges the last seat in the field. Seasons archived before ATR existed show a dash.

The SHOWCASE columns answer one question: what does the State format do to this team? State is decided at one singles line and four doubles, while the regular season is played at three and four. Doubles already carries about half the regular-season dual, so the gap is small, and State drops two singles lines. The mid-season Match Showcases are the only place a team plays the State format before it counts, so these columns compare the two.

SC is how many showcases the team played. Fmt is its showcase result minus its own regular-season result, in points: 0 means the format changes nothing for them, +15 means they play fifteen points better once the format drops to 1S/4D, −15 means worse. Dbl is the same reading for doubles alone: how much more of the win doubles carries in the State format, with 0 being the share it already carries in the regular season.

Both are measured against the team's own regular season, so a weak team and a champion can both sit at 0 when the format suits them the same either way. Both are damped by how many showcases were played, so a thin sample reads near 0. Hover a cell for the dual count. These columns play no part in TOSS, ATR or seeding.
