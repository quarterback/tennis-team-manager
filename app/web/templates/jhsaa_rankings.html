TOSS — 40% strength of schedule, 40% flight-weighted results, 20% game share. It is computed over the whole association's regular season, not one classification, and is archived with the season: at-large bids and state seeds were drawn from exactly these numbers, so the ranking and the bracket cannot disagree.

ATR — the Average Team Rating, half TOSS and half win percentage, archived the same way. It is the only figure here that is not pure TOSS, and it exists for one job: ranking the Conference pool, the last recovery round. TOSS measures who you played, so a middling team in a brutal district is propped up by the company it keeps while a twenty-win season against an ordinary schedule rates below it — the right trade for seeding a draw, the wrong one for the last seat in the field. Seasons archived before ATR existed show a dash.

The SHOWCASE columns compare each team's mid-season 1S/4D Match Showcases against its 5S/2D regular season — the format State is decided in against the format the regular season is played in. SC Rating is the showcase weighted win share on a 0-10 scale. Fmt ° is showcase weighted win share minus regular-season weighted win share, read as a signed "degree": positive ("warmer") means a team plays UP once the card drops to one singles line and four doubles, negative ("colder") means down. Dbl+ is an ERA+/OPS+-style index for how much of a team's wins come from doubles: 100 x (showcase doubles win share ÷ regular-season doubles win share) — 100 means identical to the team's own regular season, above 100 means doubles is carrying more of the win under 1S/4D, below means less; a dash means the team won zero of its regular-season weighted points on doubles, so no baseline exists to index against. SC Volatility is how much a team's result swings dual to dual within its showcases; a smaller card means one line flipping moves a bigger share of the outcome. None of this is TOSS or ATR, none of it is archived toward a seed, and none of it means anything on a thin sample — hover any of these cells for the dual count (SC n) it was built on, and a team with n=1 has played one dual, not established a trend.
