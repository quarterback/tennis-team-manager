# JHSAA Road-to-State vs. TOSS Backtest, 2066–2073

## Purpose

This report measures how often teams that ranked highly in TOSS nevertheless failed to reach the State field through the Road to State. It is intended as a historical baseline for the new 48-team State / Parastate / at-large structure in 7A and Group 1 beginning in 2074, and as a reference dataset for the computer-ratings archive across all twelve championship groups.

The analysis treats **State qualification as the outcome** and asks how often teams inside the **TOSS top 24, top 32, and top 40** were left out. It does **not** assume TOSS is the correct selector. The point is to quantify disagreement between an earned Road-to-State pathway and a season-long power measure.

All competitive comparisons use **championship group**, not enrollment classification.

## Dataset

- Seasons: **2066–2073**
- Genders: boys and girls
- Championship groups: all 12 JHSAA groups
- Team-season rows: **14,300**
- Fields retained: season, gender, championship group, school, enrollment classification, record, TOSS, TOSS rank, State qualification, State-field size

## Association-wide finding

Across all twelve championship groups and both genders, highly ranked TOSS teams missed State often enough to be a recurring structural feature rather than a rare anomaly.

| TOSS range | Missed State | Team-seasons in range | Miss rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Top 24 | **358** | 4,608 | **7.8%** |
| Top 32 | **939** | 6,144 | **15.3%** |
| Top 40 | **1,936** | 7,680 | **25.2%** |

The central number is the top-32 result: **roughly one in six team-seasons ranked inside the best 32 in their championship group by TOSS still failed to reach State.**

This does not mean those teams automatically deserved selection. It means Road qualification and season-long power ranking have historically produced materially different fields.

## The equal-and-opposite side: lower-TOSS road qualifiers

Because the historical State field is fixed, every high-TOSS team omitted corresponds to a road qualifier from farther down the TOSS order. That is not automatically a flaw. It is evidence that the Road and the computer rating reward different forms of legitimacy.

- **7A:** average **3.62** State qualifiers per gender-season ranked outside the TOSS top 32, and **1.12** ranked outside the top 40.
- **Group 1:** average **4.25** State qualifiers per gender-season ranked outside the TOSS top 32, and **1.69** ranked outside the top 40.

The new 48-team model preserves those earned Road berths. It does not replace them with computer selections. Instead, it adds a second route for teams whose season-long résumé is strong enough to survive committee review.

## Why 16 at-large berths is a generous but sensible number

Historical top-40 omissions provide a useful scale check:

- 7A averaged **9.1** top-40 misses per gender-season and never exceeded **11**.
- Group 1 averaged **9.7** and never exceeded **12**.

Sixteen committee places therefore provide enough room to capture the historically obvious high-TOSS omissions **and** still force genuine committee decisions among teams whose cases differ across Colley, Bradley-Terry, Massey, SRS, game margin, set share, SOR, Elo, and raw record.

If JHSAA simply selected the 16 highest-TOSS nonqualifiers, it would usually reach into roughly the high-40s of the TOSS order. The new committee intentionally does something more interesting: it allows teams to rise or fall because the nine systems disagree about what constitutes strength.

## Interpretation for the 2074 7A / Group 1 model

The historical backtest supports the architecture now being introduced:

1. **Road qualification remains primary.** Teams that win through the Road retain seeds 1–32 and can never be displaced upward or downward by an at-large team.
2. **At-large selection addresses a recurring omission problem.** Every season produces credible teams outside the Road field, including occasional extreme cases.
3. **At-larges belong at 33–48.** The committee provides access, not retroactive Road status.
4. **Parastate is the correct proving round.** Every committee team must immediately face a road qualifier, so résumé legitimacy is tested on the court before the Round of 32.
5. **The nine-system committee is preferable to TOSS-only selection.** TOSS demonstrates that disagreement exists; it should not be allowed to define the answer by itself.

## Bottom line

From 2066 through 2073, missing State despite a strong TOSS position was common enough to be an enduring feature of the JHSAA postseason:

- **358 top-24 team-seasons missed State.**
- **939 top-32 team-seasons missed State.**
- **1,936 top-40 team-seasons missed State.**

For the two groups adopting the new system, the historical average was about **3.6 top-32 omissions per gender-season in 7A and 4.25 in Group 1**.

The new 48-team structure therefore is not solving an invented edge case. It formalizes a second route for a population that has existed every season, while preserving the Road as the superior earned path. The committee's job is not to identify 'the next sixteen by TOSS.' Its job is to adjudicate the real disagreement that the historical data shows has always existed between qualification results and season-long team strength.
